The trip back to Rome tends to get less planning attention than the arrival did, which is a mistake — the return leg from Sorrento has its own set of practical details worth sorting out before departure day, especially if it ends with a flight out of Fiumicino. Here's what's actually worth knowing before you book this leg.

## The Drive Itself

A Sorrento to Rome private transfer covers the same approximately 260 km as the outbound trip, in around 3 hours under normal conditions. The route runs back through the Sorrentine Peninsula's local roads before joining the motorway corridor north toward Rome, the same corridor shared with the Rome-Naples route for most of the distance. Traffic around Naples and the peninsula itself can vary depending on time of day, so the 3-hour figure is a reasonable average rather than a fixed guarantee.

## Timing the Departure Around a Flight

If this transfer ends at Fiumicino for an international or connecting flight, timing deserves more attention than the drive time alone suggests. Airlines typically recommend arriving two to three hours before an international departure, and that clock starts at the airport, not when you leave your hotel. Working backward from your flight's departure time, you'll want to account for:

- The approximately 3-hour drive from Sorrento to Rome
- Recommended airport arrival buffer before an international flight (commonly 2-3 hours)
- A margin for traffic, since the route passes through areas around Naples where congestion is common, particularly during peak travel seasons

For a flight departing at, say, 6pm, that math can mean leaving Sorrento shortly after lunchtime once buffers are factored in. It's worth doing this calculation honestly rather than optimistically — arriving at Fiumicino with time to spare is a far better outcome than a stressful dash through the terminal.

One advantage of booking a private transfer rather than a fixed-time taxi or shuttle for this leg is that departure time can be built around your actual flight, and adjusted if your plans shift in the days before departure — something considerably harder to do with a pre-purchased train ticket.

## Luggage After a Stay on the Coast

Most travelers leave Sorrento with more luggage than they arrived with — the natural result of a week or more spent shopping, collecting souvenirs, or simply repacking after living out of a suitcase. A private transfer accounts for this in a way public transport doesn't: your driver loads bags directly from your hotel, with no need to manage trolleys through a station or fit expanded luggage into a train's limited storage space.

If your luggage situation has changed meaningfully since arrival — an extra bag, bulkier items, or simply more than you started with — it's worth mentioning this when booking your return transfer so the right vehicle size is confirmed in advance rather than discovered as a tight squeeze on departure morning.

## An Optional Stop at Pompeii on the Way Back

Just as it's a popular addition on the way down to Sorrento, a stop at Pompeii works equally well on the return leg toward Rome, since the road passes close to the site either direction. Travelers who didn't fit it into their arrival day, or who'd simply rather see the ruins before heading home rather than at the start of a trip, can arrange this as part of the return transfer.

This needs to be planned in advance rather than decided spontaneously, since it adds real time to the day and needs to be weighed against your departure buffer for a flight. If you're working with a tight flight time, it's generally safer to skip an add-on stop on the return leg specifically, since there's less flexibility to absorb delays than there was on the arrival day.

## Departing Sorrento's Hotels

Many hotels in and around Sorrento sit on narrow streets or along the cliffside with limited vehicle access right to the door. A driver who works this route regularly will typically know the practical pickup point closest to your specific hotel, so it's worth confirming your exact address and any access notes when booking, rather than assuming a vehicle can pull directly up to the entrance.

## Choosing a Vehicle for the Return Leg

Vehicle sizing matters just as much heading north as it did on arrival — arguably more, given the tendency to accumulate extra luggage during a stay. An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits a couple or solo traveler with standard luggage. A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), fitting up to 5 passengers and 4 suitcases, tends to work better for families or anyone who's picked up extra bags. Groups can use an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), each seating up to 7 passengers with 6 suitcases.

## If Rome Itself Is the Destination

Not every Sorrento to Rome transfer ends at an airport. If you're heading into the city for a hotel stay before flying out later, or continuing on by another means, timing is naturally more flexible — but it's still worth sharing your destination address at booking so your driver can plan the most sensible route into the city given traffic conditions on the day.

## What If You're Continuing to Civitavecchia Instead of Fiumicino

Not every Sorrento to Rome transfer ends at an airport. For travelers whose trip continues with a cruise departure, Civitavecchia is a common next stop rather than Fiumicino, and it's worth knowing that the drive time and route differ from the airport transfer — Civitavecchia sits further from central Rome, along the coast to the northwest. If this applies to you, it's worth confirming your exact destination and, if you know it, your ship's boarding or all-aboard time, so your driver can plan the timing appropriately rather than defaulting to the more common Fiumicino routing.

## Traffic Patterns Worth Knowing

The stretch of motorway between Naples and Rome carries a substantial amount of both local and long-distance traffic, and congestion tends to build around the usual peak periods — weekday mornings and early evenings, and more generally during the height of summer travel season when the route is busiest. A departure timed to avoid the worst of a predictable rush, where your schedule allows that flexibility, can shave meaningful time off an otherwise average 3-hour drive. This is another area where a private transfer has an advantage over a fixed train departure: if your flight or plans allow some flexibility in departure time, a driver familiar with the route can offer a reasonable sense of which window tends to move more smoothly on a given day.

## Booking Ahead

As with the outbound leg, this transfer works best when arranged in advance rather than the day before departure. Booking ahead means your flight details (if relevant) are on file, your vehicle is confirmed for your actual group size and luggage, and any stops — Pompeii or otherwise — are planned into the day rather than improvised.

## Final Thoughts

The return trip from Sorrento to Rome isn't fundamentally different from the trip down, but the practical details shift slightly — more luggage, a flight departure to plan around, and less flexibility to absorb a spontaneous stop if time is tight. Planning for those specifics, rather than assuming the return leg mirrors the arrival exactly, tends to make for a smoother last day in Italy.
