Getting to Naples Airport on time for a flight is a different planning problem than arriving on the coast in the first place. On arrival, a delay mostly costs you some frustration. On departure, it can cost you a flight — which makes the return leg from the Amalfi Coast worth planning with a bit more care than the trip out.

## Start With Your Flight, Then Work Backward

The most reliable way to plan this leg is to work backward from your flight's check-in or boarding time, rather than forward from when you'd like to leave your hotel. International flights typically call for arriving at the airport with a couple of hours to spare; domestic and short-haul European flights generally need less, though it's worth checking your specific airline's guidance.

From there, add the drive time itself, which varies depending on which coast town you're leaving from — more on that below — and then add a buffer for the coast road's unpredictability. There's no single verified drive time covering the whole Amalfi Coast to Naples Airport route, since it depends heavily on your exact starting point and conditions on the day, so it's worth being generous with this buffer rather than cutting it close.

## Your Starting Town Changes the Timing

Positano, Amalfi, and Ravello sit at different points along the coast road, and departing from each involves a somewhat different amount of driving before you even reach the faster motorway sections leading back toward Naples.

**Leaving from Positano** generally means less distance to cover along the narrow coast road compared with towns further along, since Positano sits closer to the Naples-facing end of the coastline. That said, getting out of Positano itself can take longer than the map distance suggests, given the town's steep, congested streets near the center, particularly in peak season.

**Leaving from Amalfi town** means a bit more coast road to cover before reaching the faster roads, though the town's easier vehicle access near the waterfront generally makes the pickup itself more straightforward than in Positano.

**Leaving from Ravello** adds the downhill stretch from its hillside position back to the coast road before the drive to the airport even begins, so it's worth budgeting a bit more time from here than from the beachfront towns.

None of these differences are dramatic, but they're worth mentioning to your driver specifically — "the Amalfi Coast" isn't precise enough when timing a flight departure, but "Ravello, near the Duomo" is.

## Luggage From a Hotel With Limited Access

This is often the part of departure day that surprises people, even those who managed it fine on arrival with less on their mind. Many hotels along the coast, especially in Positano and parts of Amalfi and Ravello, have limited or no vehicle access right up to the entrance. If that was true when you arrived, it's equally true on the way out — and on a departure day, you're working against a flight time rather than an open afternoon.

A few things worth doing in advance:

- Confirm with your hotel the evening before roughly how long it takes to get luggage from your room to the nearest point a vehicle can reach, especially if you're in an upper part of a hillside town.
- If your hotel offers porter assistance to the drop-off point, arrange it ahead of time rather than assuming it'll be available on request that morning.
- Build the walk (or the wait for a porter) into your overall timing, not just the drive itself — it's easy to account for the driving time and forget the 10 or 15 minutes it might take to get bags out of a hotel with stepped access.
- If you're checking out early in the morning, confirm the hotel's front desk and any shuttle or porter service will actually be staffed at that hour — some smaller coast properties run reduced service outside normal daytime hours.

## The Coast Road Itself, One More Time

Even on the way out, the SS163 doesn't change character just because you're leaving rather than arriving. It's the same narrow, winding cliffside road, and it moves at the same unhurried pace regardless of direction of travel. Traffic patterns can actually be worse on certain mornings, particularly in peak season, as delivery vehicles restock hotels and shops before the day's visitors arrive.

An experienced driver factors this into departure timing based on the day of the week and time of year, which is one advantage of booking with someone who knows the route rather than estimating the drive purely from a map distance. If you have any flexibility in your flight time when booking your trip, an early or midday flight tends to be easier to plan around confidently than a tight evening departure at the end of peak season.

## What to Confirm the Day Before You Leave

A short list worth going through the evening before departure:

- Your driver has your flight number and departure terminal, so timing can be adjusted if anything changes overnight.
- Your pickup point at the hotel is confirmed, including whether it's at the property itself or a fixed point down the hill.
- You've allowed enough buffer for both the coast road and any walk or porter wait at the start of the journey.
- You know roughly how the drive compares to the general 60-to-75-minute Naples Airport to Sorrento benchmark — since towns further along the coast beyond Sorrento take longer, and that gives you a rough floor to plan above rather than a number to rely on exactly.

## If You're Connecting to a Flight Beyond Naples

Some travelers use Naples Airport as a connecting point rather than the final leg of their trip, flying onward to another Italian city or back home via a hub elsewhere in Europe. If that's your situation, it's worth applying the same generous buffer to your connection as you would to the airport arrival itself — a tight connection built on the assumption that the coast road runs exactly to a map estimate is one of the more common ways travelers end up rushing through an airport unnecessarily.

It's also worth checking whether your connecting flight is with the same airline or a separate booking, since a delay caused by the drive itself is treated very differently by an airline depending on how your tickets are structured. If there's any flexibility in how you book your flights home, leaving a wider gap between your Amalfi Coast checkout and your final departure removes one more variable from a day that already has a few moving parts.

## Choosing the Right Vehicle for the Return Leg

As with the outbound trip, vehicle size matters on the narrower sections of the coast road. A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) handles a family's luggage comfortably without being difficult to maneuver through tight village centers. Groups traveling together often prefer an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), both seating up to 7 passengers, with your driver factoring the group's luggage into how the drive out of a tighter town like Positano is planned.

## Final Thoughts

The return trip to Naples Airport deserves the same respect as the trip out, if not more, simply because a flight has a fixed departure time that a leisurely arrival day doesn't. Knowing which town you're leaving from, planning around your specific hotel's access, and giving yourself a generous buffer on the coast road are the three things that matter most for a stress-free departure.
