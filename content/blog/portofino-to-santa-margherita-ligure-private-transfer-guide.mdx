Santa Margherita Ligure is the town most visitors pass through on their way to Portofino, whether they realize it at the time or not. It sits just along the coast as the nearest larger town, and a huge share of Portofino's day-trip traffic — buses, boats, and private cars alike — either starts there or passes through on the way in. For travelers already based in Portofino, the hop over to Santa Margherita Ligure is one of the easiest and most popular short excursions on this part of the coast.

## Why This Is Such a Common Pairing

Portofino is a small place. It's a stunning harbor, a handful of boutiques and restaurants around the piazzetta, and some scenic walks to the lighthouse and the castle above town, but it doesn't take long to see the core of it, and accommodation options within Portofino itself are limited and tend to be expensive. Santa Margherita Ligure, by contrast, is a proper town with a larger selection of hotels, restaurants, shops, and a more everyday Ligurian atmosphere alongside its own resort appeal. A lot of visitors base themselves in Santa Margherita Ligure and treat Portofino as the day-trip destination, rather than the other way around — and plenty of travelers staying in Portofino head over to Santa Margherita Ligure for a change of pace, a wider choice of dinner spots, or simply to see more of the coast than the one harbor they're staying in.

## How Far Is It, Really?

This is one of the shortest, most frequently made trips in the whole Portofino area, and that's worth saying clearly — but we're still not going to attach a specific number of minutes or kilometers to it, because there's no single verified figure that holds up across every condition. Road traffic along this stretch of coast can back up considerably in peak summer season and on busy weekends, when both towns see heavy day-trip crowds, so even a short hop can take longer than you'd expect purely from looking at a map. On a quiet day outside peak season, the same trip might feel almost immediate.

The honest takeaway is that this is a short regional transfer, not a long drive, but "short" doesn't mean "always fast" on a coastal road that gets genuinely congested at certain times. ## What Santa Margherita Ligure Offers

Santa Margherita Ligure has its own appeal beyond being a gateway to Portofino. It has a genuine working waterfront with boats that aren't all pleasure craft, a palm-lined seafront promenade, and a town center that feels lived-in rather than built purely for tourism. There's a notable villa and gardens open to visitors, a historic church, and a beach area along the town's edge that gives it more of a relaxed seaside-resort feel than Portofino's more polished harbor scene.

For travelers who find Portofino beautiful but a little too compact or a little too geared toward high-end shopping and photo stops, Santa Margherita Ligure often comes as a pleasant surprise — a town you can actually spend an unhurried day in in its own right, not just a stepping stone to somewhere else.

## Why Travelers Combine the Two

The two towns complement each other well precisely because they offer different things within a short trip of each other. Portofino gives you the postcard harbor, the boutique shopping, and a certain exclusivity. Santa Margherita Ligure gives you more breathing room, a wider range of price points for dining and shopping, and a slower-paced seafront that doesn't feel quite as geared toward a single Instagram shot. Visiting both lets you see two sides of the same stretch of coast without a long drive in between.

Some travelers also use Santa Margherita Ligure as a practical base specifically because of accommodation availability — Portofino's hotels are few and often booked out or priced well above what Santa Margherita Ligure offers for a comparable standard of stay, so basing yourself in the larger town and making short trips over to Portofino for the day is a common and sensible approach.
