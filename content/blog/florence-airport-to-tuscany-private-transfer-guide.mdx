Landing at Florence Airport is usually the easy part of a Tuscany trip. The harder part is what happens in the first couple of hours afterward — because where you tell your driver to take you sets the tone for the whole stay, and for a lot of travelers, that destination isn't Florence's city center at all.

## Florence Airport's Real Advantage: It's Close

Florence Airport (officially Amerigo Vespucci, sometimes called Peretola) sits only about 5 km from central Florence, a drive of roughly 15 to 20 minutes in normal traffic. That's a short hop by any standard, and it's one of the reasons the airport works well as a gateway not just to the city but to Tuscany more broadly.

What it doesn't have is a single, fixed figure for "airport to the Tuscan countryside," and that's worth saying plainly rather than guessing at a number. Tuscany is a large region, and whether you're headed to Chianti, the hills around Siena, or somewhere further out toward the coast or the south, travel time depends heavily on which part of the region you're actually going to. A villa twenty minutes outside the city is a very different drive than one closer to Siena or Montalcino. If a specific hotel or address has already been booked, that's the number worth asking a driver about directly rather than relying on a general airport distance.

## Planning Your Arrival Day Around the Flight, Not the Map

Long-haul arrivals into Florence are often a connection through a larger European hub, which means jet lag, an early landing time, and luggage that's been through more than one airport by the time you reach the car. This changes how an arrival day should be planned.

A few things worth building into the plan before you land:

- **Flight tracking and a realistic pickup buffer.** Share your flight number when booking so a driver can adjust for delays rather than working off a fixed schedule.
- **Luggage reality.** If you're arriving for a longer Tuscany stay — multiple stops, maybe a wine region visit — you're likely traveling with more than carry-on bags. Mention passenger and bag counts when booking so the right vehicle is sent.
- **A light first day, if possible.** Arrival day is rarely the best day for an ambitious countryside itinerary. Even a short transfer feels longer after an overnight flight, and pushing straight into a full day of hill towns and tastings often backfires.

## Should Your First Stop Be the City or the Countryside?

This is really the central decision for anyone flying into Florence with Tuscany, not just the city, on their itinerary. There are two reasonable approaches, and which one fits depends on what the rest of the trip looks like.

**Heading straight into the countryside or a hill town.** If your trip is centered on rural Tuscany — an agriturismo, a villa in Chianti, a base near Siena — there's an argument for skipping the city altogether on arrival day and going directly to where you're staying. This avoids checking in and out of a city hotel you'll barely use, and it means your first evening is already spent somewhere quieter, which tends to suit a jet-lagged arrival better than navigating central Florence's traffic and pedestrian zones with luggage.

**Spending a night or two in Florence first.** If Florence itself is part of the plan — and for most travelers to the region, it is at some point — starting there lets you handle the first, most disoriented day somewhere with easy access to food, pharmacies, and everything else a traveler might need after a long flight, before setting out into the countryside once you've settled in.

Neither approach is wrong. What matters is deciding in advance rather than figuring it out at arrivals, since it changes what you tell your driver and how the first leg of the trip should be timed.

## Why a Private Transfer Suits This Kind of Arrival

Public transport options from Florence Airport are limited once you're heading beyond the city itself. There's no direct train service from the airport, and reaching a specific hill town or countryside address generally means a taxi, a rental car, or a private transfer — there isn't a practical bus-and-train combination for most rural Tuscan addresses straight from arrivals.

A [private airport transfer](/airport-transfers/florence) removes that problem entirely. A driver meets you at arrivals, already aware of your destination, and takes you directly there — whether that's a hotel in central Florence or an address well outside it. There's no need to first reach a car rental counter, work out unfamiliar roads with jet lag, or transfer between a shuttle and a taxi to finish the final leg of the journey.

This matters more on this particular route than it might on a simple city-to-city transfer, because countryside addresses are often harder to find. GPS directions to a rural villa or farmhouse can be unreliable, and a driver who already knows the area is a real advantage over trying to self-navigate on the first day of a trip.

## Choosing the Right Vehicle for an Arrival Transfer

For most arrivals, an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) comfortably covers up to 3 passengers and 2 suitcases, which suits a couple or a solo traveler with standard luggage. Families or anyone arriving with extra bags — not unusual on a trip that includes a longer countryside stay — often do better with a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), which seats up to 5 passengers with 4 suitcases. For larger groups arriving together, an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) seats up to 7 passengers with 6 suitcases, useful if you're arriving as an extended family or a group splitting a countryside rental.

## Building the Rest of the Trip Around This First Leg

Once you're settled, whether in Florence or already out in the countryside, the wider region opens up. [Tuscany](/destinations/tuscany) covers a lot of ground, and many travelers who start with an airport transfer go on to arrange further travel to [Siena](/destinations/siena) or between towns as the trip develops — something worth discussing with a driver early, since planning it during the arrival transfer is often easier than arranging it later once you're already settled in.

If your Tuscany plans include Florence itself before or after the countryside portion of the trip, it's also worth looking at how [Florence connects to nearby Pisa](/blog/florence-to-pisa-private-transfer-before-you-book), since a number of travelers combine the two on the same trip.

## The Bottom Line

Florence Airport puts you close to the city, but for many travelers it's really the start of a trip into the wider region. Knowing in advance whether your first stop is the city or the countryside, sharing accurate flight and luggage details, and arranging a transfer that goes directly to your actual destination — rather than assuming a single fixed transfer time applies everywhere in Tuscany — makes the first few hours of the trip far less stressful than trying to work it out after you land.
