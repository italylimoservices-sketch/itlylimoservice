Bari Karol Wojtyła Airport is the main gateway to Puglia, and for most travelers it's also the starting point of a trip that goes well beyond the city of Bari itself. Alberobello, Ostuni, Polignano a Mare, the Valle d'Itria, and the Salento peninsula further south are all reachable from the airport, which means arrival day often doubles as the first leg of a longer journey rather than a simple hop into town.

That makes planning arrival day a bit different than if you were only staying in Bari. This guide covers what to expect on landing, how to think about timing when your real destination is somewhere else in the region, and how to choose the right vehicle for whatever comes next.

## What to Expect on Arrival

Bari Airport handles both domestic and international flights, and like most regional Italian airports, the pace through arrivals can vary quite a bit depending on how many flights land close together. If you're arriving from outside the Schengen area, passport control adds another step before baggage claim. Budget some flexibility here rather than assuming you'll be through the terminal in minutes — on a quiet afternoon it might be fast, but on a busy summer morning with several flights landing together, it can take longer.

A private driver arranged in advance will typically be waiting in the arrivals area with a sign showing your name, which removes the guesswork of finding transportation once you're through the doors. Sharing your flight number at the time of booking means the pickup is tracked against your actual landing time, not a fixed clock time that a delay or an early arrival could throw off.

## There's No Verified "Standard" Drive Time — And That's Fine

You'll find plenty of travel sites quoting exact minutes and kilometers for routes out of Bari Airport. We'd rather be straightforward: driving times from the airport to specific Puglia towns depend heavily on where exactly you're headed, the time of day, and road conditions on any given trip, and we don't publish invented averages as if they were guaranteed figures. What's true in general is that Puglia is a region best explored by road — public transport between its smaller towns is limited, and a private transfer is often the most practical way to cover ground without juggling schedules.

If your itinerary includes a specific onward destination, ask your driver or booking team for a realistic estimate for that particular day and route rather than relying on a number you saw online. Traffic around Bari itself, especially near the city center and during peak periods, can add meaningfully to a trip that otherwise feels short on a map.

## Choosing Your First Stop

Puglia's main appeal is spread across a handful of distinct areas, and where you head first from the airport depends on what kind of trip you're planning:

- **Bari city** itself, if you want a day or two in the regional capital before moving on, with its old town and waterfront worth exploring on foot.
- **The Valle d'Itria**, home to [Alberobello](/blog/bari-airport-to-alberobello-complete-transfer-guide) and its trulli, along with Ostuni and smaller hill towns, generally considered the heart of inland Puglia.
- **The Adriatic coast**, including towns like Polignano a Mare, known for dramatic cliffside settings.
- **The Salento peninsula** further south, around Lecce and the coastline near Otranto and Gallipoli, for travelers planning a longer loop through the region.

Many visitors structure a Puglia trip as a loop starting and ending at Bari Airport, with a few nights in the Valle d'Itria and a few more down in Salento. If that's your plan, it's worth deciding before you land whether you want to go straight from the airport to your first overnight stop, or spend a night in Bari first to ease into the trip.

## Luggage and Group Size

Puglia trips often involve more luggage than a typical city break — swimwear, layers for cooler evenings inland, and sometimes golf clubs or bikes for travelers combining the coast with cycling through the countryside. Mentioning any unusual luggage at booking means the right vehicle is arranged in advance rather than sorted out at the curb.

- An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) comfortably suits 3 passengers with 2 suitcases, a good fit for couples or solo travelers.
- A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) handles up to 5 passengers and 4 suitcases, useful for families or anyone traveling with extra gear.
- An [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) seats up to 7 passengers with 6 suitcases, suited to larger groups or multi-generational trips.

## Flights, Delays, and Connections

A driver working from your flight number rather than a fixed pickup time will adjust automatically if your flight runs early or late. What's harder to absorb is a tight same-day connection on the other end — a train further south, a ferry, or a second flight. If your trip has you moving on again quickly after landing in Bari, build that buffer around the airport process itself rather than assuming the drive afterward can simply be shortened to compensate.

It's also worth flagging connecting flights at booking. A delay on an earlier leg of a multi-flight itinerary can shift your real arrival time in Bari even if your final ticket shows something fixed, and a driver who knows this in advance can plan around it more easily.

## The Return Trip Matters Too

It's easy to plan carefully for arrival and then give almost no thought to the departure leg until the last day of the trip, but the return transfer deserves the same attention. If you're flying out of Bari after touring the region, work backward from your flight's check-in and security requirements, and build in extra time if you're driving back from somewhere further out, like Salento or the Valle d'Itria, rather than treating the return as a mirror image of the arrival drive. Traffic patterns at different times of day aren't symmetrical, and a drive that felt easy in one direction can take longer in the other depending on when you're traveling.

If your trip ends with an early flight, it's worth discussing pickup timing with your driver well in advance, particularly if you're coming from a rural stay where leaving extra early is the safer choice.

## When to Visit

Puglia draws most of its visitors in the warmer months, roughly from late spring through early autumn, when the coastline and hill towns are at their most appealing and outdoor dining and sightseeing are comfortable. This is also when Bari Airport tends to see its heaviest traffic, both in terms of flight volume and the resulting arrivals crowd, so travelers visiting in peak season should expect busier terminals and build a bit more buffer into arrival day. Visiting in the shoulder seasons or winter generally means a quieter airport experience, though some rural accommodations and smaller-town attractions may have reduced hours outside the main season — worth checking directly with your accommodation rather than assuming everything runs year-round.

## A Simple Starting Checklist

Before you land, it helps to have settled a few basics: your flight number shared with your driver, a rough passenger and luggage count, and a decided first stop — even if that decision is simply "straight to the hotel in Bari before deciding anything else." None of this takes long to sort out, but having it settled before touchdown means your first hours in Puglia go toward actually seeing the region rather than figuring out how to get anywhere.
