Booking a private chauffeur in Sardinia involves a few more decisions than it might for a single-city trip. The island spreads its airports, coastline and interior across a lot of ground, and the right setup for a quick Costa Smeralda transfer looks nothing like the right setup for a week-long touring itinerary that covers several regions. This guide walks through the main choices — vehicle sizing, how you book the service, and what local road knowledge actually buys you — so you can match the booking to the trip rather than the other way around.

## Start With How Many Days You're Actually Covering

The single biggest factor in choosing a Sardinia chauffeur setup is whether you need a one-off transfer or a multi-day arrangement. A single airport pickup — say, landing in Olbia and heading straight to a Costa Smeralda hotel — is a simple, self-contained booking with a clear start and end point. A multi-day touring itinerary that moves between the coast, the interior and maybe a second airport is a different kind of planning exercise, and it's worth thinking about it as a whole trip rather than a series of separate transfers booked one at a time.

For multi-day touring specifically, it helps to have a rough sense upfront of:

- How many distinct stops or regions you want to cover
- Whether you're based in one place with day trips out, or moving to a new base every night or two
- Whether you're flying in and out of the same airport or ending somewhere different
- How much luggage the group is carrying, since this tends to grow on longer trips

None of this needs to be locked in with precision before you book, but having even a loose outline makes it easier for a chauffeur service to recommend the right vehicle and structure the days sensibly.

## Sizing the Vehicle to the Trip, Not Just the Group

It's tempting to size a vehicle purely by passenger count, but luggage and trip length matter just as much, especially in Sardinia where a touring itinerary often means packing for a beach stay, an inland day or two, and whatever formal occasion might be on the agenda.

- A couple or solo traveler with standard luggage fits comfortably in an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan), both suited to up to three passengers and two suitcases.
- A group of four or five, or a couple packing heavier for a longer stay, tends to be better served by a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), built for up to five passengers and four suitcases.
- Families or small groups of up to seven generally need an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), both built with room for six suitcases alongside seven passengers.

If the trip includes a stretch where the group might split — some people heading to a beach club while others go sightseeing inland — it's worth mentioning that at booking too, since it can affect whether one vehicle covers the whole trip or whether a second car makes more sense for part of it.

## Hourly, Point-to-Point, or Multi-Day — Which Booking Structure Fits

Chauffeur bookings generally come in a few different shapes, and Sardinia's spread-out geography makes the choice between them more consequential than it might be in a compact city.

**Point-to-point** booking covers a single leg — an airport to a hotel, one town to another — with a fixed pickup and drop-off. It's the simplest option and works well when your plan is already a series of clear, separate transfers rather than open-ended exploring.

**Hourly or by-the-day** booking keeps the driver and vehicle with you for a block of time, which suits a day of touring where you might want to stop at a few towns, linger somewhere longer than planned, or change the route as the day unfolds. This tends to be the better fit for exploring the Costa Smeralda's smaller towns or spending a day inland, where the value isn't just the drive itself but the flexibility to adjust along the way.

**Multi-day** arrangements string together several days of touring, sometimes with the same driver throughout, which can be worth asking about directly if your itinerary runs more than a couple of days and covers varied ground — coast one day, interior the next, maybe a transfer to a different base toward the end.

There's no single right answer here. A trip that's mostly fixed transfers between known points doesn't need the flexibility of an hourly booking, and a trip built around open-ended exploring doesn't get full value from a rigid point-to-point structure. Matching the booking type to what you actually plan to do is usually more useful than defaulting to whichever sounds simplest.

## Why Local Road Knowledge Matters More in Sardinia

Sardinia's roads vary a fair amount depending on where you are. The routes around the Costa Smeralda and the busier northeastern towns see heavier seasonal traffic, especially in summer. Coastal roads elsewhere on the island can be narrow and winding as they trace the shoreline, and the interior's roads are a different character again — quieter, but with mountain terrain that behaves differently from a flat coastal drive. A driver who already knows these patterns — which stretches slow down in the afternoon, which coastal towns get tight for parking, how the interior roads differ from the coast — tends to make a multi-day itinerary noticeably smoother than one assembled purely from a map or a GPS route.

This matters more on a touring trip than on a single transfer, since a multi-day plan touching several of these road types benefits most from a driver who can sequence the days sensibly rather than just following turn-by-turn directions.

## Thinking About the Season

Sardinia's chauffeur bookings shift noticeably with the season, and it's worth factoring that into how you plan a trip. Summer brings heavier traffic to the coastal roads and the towns around the Costa Smeralda specifically, which can affect how a day's itinerary is sequenced even with an experienced driver behind the wheel. Shoulder season — spring and early autumn — tends to be quieter on the roads and often more comfortable for inland touring, when the interior's hill towns and countryside are easier to enjoy without summer heat. None of this changes whether a chauffeur is the right choice, but it's a reasonable factor to mention when discussing an itinerary, since a driver familiar with the island can sometimes suggest timing adjustments — an earlier start to avoid midday traffic near a popular coastal town, for instance — that aren't obvious from an itinerary built without that local context.

## A Few Questions Worth Asking Before You Book

- Which airport (or airports) are involved, and is the trip one-way or a loop?
- How many days of touring, roughly, and in what order?
- What's the realistic passenger and luggage count, including anything picked up along the way?
- Is the itinerary fixed, or do you want room to adjust day by day?
- Is there a wedding, event, or business meeting with a fixed time that the rest of the schedule needs to work around?

Working through these before you book, even loosely, tends to produce a better match between the trip and the service than booking a generic transfer and hoping it flexes to fit.
