Florence and Pisa are about as well-connected as two Italian cities get. There's a fast, frequent regional train running between them, and the drive covers a short 85 km in around an hour. On a route this short and well-served, the real question for most travelers isn't how to get there — it's which way makes more sense for them specifically. This is a straight comparison, built to help you answer that rather than to sell you on one option over the other.

## Why This Comparison Exists on a Route This Short

A lot of transfer decisions in Italy are shaped by distance and complexity — a three-hour drive through mountains is a different proposition than a short regional hop. Florence to Pisa doesn't have that complexity. The train is genuinely good here: it's quick, it runs often throughout the day, and it connects two well-served stations in city centers that are easy to reach on foot or by taxi. That's exactly why this comparison is worth having honestly, rather than assuming a private transfer automatically wins just because it's more comfortable. On a route this short and this well-served, the train deserves real consideration, not just a mention before getting to the chauffeur pitch.

## The Case for the Train

The train works well on this route for a specific kind of traveler:

- **Solo travelers and couples with light luggage.** If you're carrying a backpack or a single rolling suitcase each, getting to the station, onto the platform, and off at the other end isn't a burden.
- **Travelers already based near both stations.** If your Florence accommodation is a short walk or tram ride from Santa Maria Novella, and your Pisa destination is close to Pisa Centrale, the door-to-door time difference between train and car narrows considerably.
- **Budget-conscious travelers.** The train is generally the more affordable option on this corridor, and for a short, simple trip between two city centers, that cost difference is a genuinely reasonable thing to prioritize.
- **Travelers who enjoy the experience itself.** Regional trains through Tuscany pass through pleasant countryside, and for some travelers, watching the landscape go by is part of the trip rather than something to optimize away.

If this description fits you, there's no real argument for paying for a private transfer on this specific route. The train does the job well, and the money is better spent elsewhere in your trip.

## The Case for a Private Chauffeur

The calculation shifts for a different set of circumstances, and it's worth being specific about what actually changes it:

**Groups and families.** A train ticket is priced per person, and a family of four or five with luggage becomes a more complicated and more expensive train trip than it first appears — multiple tickets, navigating a platform together, and keeping everyone and everything accounted for through a station transfer. A private vehicle is a single fixed arrangement regardless of group size within the vehicle's capacity, and it keeps everyone together for the whole trip.

**Luggage beyond a carry-on or two.** Dragging large suitcases through a station, onto a train, and off again at the other end is manageable but not pleasant, especially if there are stairs involved or if the train is busy. A private transfer removes this entirely — your bags go in the car once and come out once.

**A flight tied to the schedule.** If this trip is really about catching a flight at Pisa Airport, or you've just landed and are heading to Florence, the calculation changes significantly. A train requires you to work backward from a fixed departure time, with your own buffer for getting to the station and the walk at the other end. A private transfer builds the buffer in and adjusts to your actual flight time rather than a timetable. This is really a different decision than the general Florence-to-Pisa comparison — more detail on timing a transfer specifically around a flight is covered in the [Florence to Pisa transfer guide](/blog/florence-to-pisa-private-transfer-guide).

**Flexible or unusual timing.** Very early morning departures, late evening arrivals, or a schedule that doesn't line up neatly with train times all favor a private transfer, which runs on your schedule rather than a fixed timetable.

**Wanting a stop along the way.** If you'd like to see the Leaning Tower and Piazza dei Miracoli as part of the trip rather than as a separate outing, a private transfer can build that in. A train trip can't easily accommodate a detour.

**Simply wanting a door-to-door trip without a station in between.** Some travelers just prefer not to manage a station at all — a driver meets you at your hotel or accommodation and takes you directly to the other one, with no walk to a platform or wait for a connection.

## A Direct Side-by-Side

| Factor | Train | Private Chauffeur |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Solo travelers, couples, light luggage | Groups, families, heavy luggage |
| Cost | Generally lower | Higher, but fixed per vehicle not per person |
| Flexibility | Fixed timetable | Flexible to your schedule |
| Flight connections | Requires working backward from departure | Built-in buffer, adjusts to actual flight time |
| Door-to-door | No — station to station | Yes |
| Stops along the way | Not practical | Can be arranged |

## How to Actually Decide

Start with your group size and luggage. If it's one or two people with manageable bags and no flight pressure, the train is a sensible, well-established choice on this route — there's little reason to spend more for a transfer you don't really need. If you're traveling as a family or group, carrying more than light luggage, working around a flight, or simply want the convenience of door-to-door travel without a station in between, a private transfer earns its cost on this particular trip, even though the distance itself is short.

It's also worth being honest that this isn't an all-or-nothing choice for your whole trip. Plenty of travelers take the train for this short, simple hop and arrange private transfers for longer or more complex routes elsewhere in Italy, where the advantages of a chauffeur matter more. Choosing the train here doesn't commit you to it everywhere else, and vice versa.

## Vehicle Options If You Choose a Transfer

For most pairs or solo travelers choosing a private transfer, an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) comfortably fits up to 3 passengers with 2 suitcases. Families or those with more luggage often prefer a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), seating up to 5 passengers with 4 suitcases. Larger groups can use a [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) to stay together in one vehicle.

## Final Thoughts

Florence to Pisa is short enough and well-served enough by train that there's no single right answer here — it genuinely depends on who's traveling and why. The train suits light, flexible, budget-minded trips between the two city centers. A private transfer earns its place when groups, luggage, flight timing, or a preference for door-to-door travel are part of the picture. Knowing which category you fall into is really the whole decision.
