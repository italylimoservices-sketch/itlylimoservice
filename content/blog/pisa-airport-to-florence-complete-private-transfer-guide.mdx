Pisa Airport is Tuscany's main international gateway, but a large share of the travelers landing there aren't actually staying in Pisa at all — they're heading on to Florence, using PSA simply because it's the most convenient or affordable airport to fly into. If that's your situation, this is the guide built specifically for you: not a Pisa city guide, not a general airport overview, but a complete look at getting from PSA straight to Florence.

## The Journey in Numbers

The drive from Pisa Airport to Florence covers approximately 80 minutes under normal conditions. That's a meaningfully different figure from the Florence-to-Pisa city-to-city distance of 85 km and roughly an hour — the airport sits slightly outside central Pisa, and traffic patterns on the route into Florence can add a bit more time than the pure city-to-city figure suggests. Either way, you're looking at a bit over an hour in the car, which makes this one of the more comfortable regional transfers in Tuscany — long enough to relax, short enough not to feel like a slog after a flight.

## Why So Many Travelers Fly Into Pisa for Florence

Florence doesn't have a major international airport of its own with the same volume of long-haul and budget connections that Pisa offers, so it's common for travelers to book flights into PSA specifically because it's cheaper, more direct, or simply available on dates when Florence's own airport isn't. If that describes your trip, you're not alone, and the airport itself is well set up for this pattern — arrivals, baggage claim, and the exit to ground transport are all compact and quick to move through.

## What Arrival Looks Like When Florence Is Your Real Destination

Because your actual destination is an hour-plus away, a few things are worth thinking about differently than if you were simply staying in Pisa:

- **Luggage handling matters more.** A longer drive means more time in the car, so having the right vehicle for your bags from the start avoids any discomfort or awkward repacking.
- **Jet lag and fatigue are bigger factors.** If you've just come off a long-haul flight, an hour-plus drive on top of the flight itself is a different experience than a quick 10-minute hop into Pisa. A private transfer lets you simply sit back rather than navigate a train platform and connection.
- **Your actual drop-off point in Florence needs to be clear.** Florence's historic center has restricted traffic zones (ZTL), so a hotel address inside the old city may require your driver to know the specific access point rather than just the general area.

## Private Transfer vs. the Train for This Specific Trip

Florence and Pisa are connected by a fast, frequent regional train, and for travelers already in central Pisa, that's a perfectly reasonable option. But landing at the airport changes the calculation somewhat. Getting from the arrivals hall to Pisa Centrale station involves a short connecting trip of its own, followed by the train ride, followed by arrival at Florence's station and then onward to your actual accommodation. Each of those steps is manageable on its own, but added together, with luggage, after a flight, they introduce more points where something can go slightly wrong or simply take longer than expected.

A private transfer collapses all of that into one continuous trip: your driver meets you at arrivals, and the next time you get out of the car is at your hotel in Florence. For travelers with significant luggage, young children, or simply a strong preference not to manage a station connection right after landing, this matters more than the raw travel time comparison suggests.

## Booking This Route Around Your Flight

Since this transfer starts at the airport, flight timing drives most of the planning:

- **Share your flight number when booking** so your driver can track real arrival time rather than the scheduled one, and adjust pickup accordingly if your flight is early or delayed.
- **Allow for the full arrival sequence** — passport control, baggage claim, and walking out to arrivals — before the 80-minute drive even begins. This is especially worth padding if you're arriving from outside the Schengen area.
- **If you're arriving very early or very late**, confirm this at booking rather than assuming standard availability, since overnight or pre-dawn pickups are a different logistical ask than a midday arrival.

## A Stop at Piazza dei Miracoli Along the Way

Because the drive to Florence passes directly by Pisa, some travelers choose to break up the trip with a stop at the Leaning Tower and Piazza dei Miracoli before continuing on. This works well if you've landed with a comfortable amount of time before you need to be settled in Florence, but it needs to be arranged in advance rather than decided spontaneously, since it adds meaningfully to what would otherwise be a straightforward 80-minute drive. If your main priority is getting to Florence and resting after a long flight, it's reasonable to skip this and save Pisa for a separate day trip later in your stay — you'll still pass close enough to the tower to glimpse it from the road.

## Choosing the Right Vehicle

For this longer route, comfort over the full drive matters more than it would on a short hop. An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits solo travelers or couples with standard luggage, seating up to 3 passengers with 2 suitcases. A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) is a better fit for families or anyone with extra bags, seating up to 5 passengers with 4 suitcases, with more space to stretch out over the hour-plus drive. Groups traveling together can use a [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), which keeps everyone in one vehicle rather than splitting across multiple cars.

## Arriving in Florence

Once you reach Florence, your drop-off point matters as much as the drive itself. Hotels inside the ZTL zone, particularly in the historic core near the Duomo or Ponte Vecchio, often require specific access arrangements that a driver familiar with the city will already know how to handle. Sharing your exact accommodation address when booking lets your driver plan the approach properly rather than working it out on arrival.

## What to Expect for the First Half of the Drive

The first stretch of the route takes you out of the airport area and past the edge of Pisa itself before the road settles into the more open Tuscan countryside toward Florence. There's nothing complicated about this part of the drive, but it's worth knowing that the scenery shifts fairly quickly from airport infrastructure to the kind of rolling Tuscan landscape many travelers associate with the region — a pleasant way to start a trip if you're looking out the window rather than resting after a flight.

## Connecting Flights and Long Travel Days

If Pisa is a connecting point on a longer journey — say, a long-haul flight followed by a short regional hop, or vice versa — the 80-minute drive to Florence is a good opportunity to simply decompress. Unlike managing a train connection, which requires some attention even on a short trip, a private transfer lets you hand off that responsibility entirely. For travelers arriving after an overnight flight, this is often the part of the journey that makes the biggest difference to how the rest of the day feels.

## Groups Arriving on Different Flights

It's common for travel companions to arrive at PSA on separate flights, particularly for group trips where people are coming from different cities or countries. If this applies to you, it's worth discussing with whoever arranges your transport whether a single pickup once everyone has landed makes more sense than two separate transfers, since consolidating into one trip to Florence is often simpler and avoids one group member waiting alone at the hotel for the other to arrive.

## Comparing This Route to Other Florence Airport Options

Travelers researching this trip sometimes also consider Florence's own airport, but flight availability and pricing often make Pisa the more practical choice despite the extra driving time. Once that decision is made — and for many travelers it's made simply by which flights are available and affordable — the 80-minute drive from PSA becomes a fixed, predictable part of the trip rather than something to second-guess. It's a comfortable amount of time to settle in before arriving, not so long that it eats meaningfully into your first day in Florence.

## Settling in After the Drive

By the time you reach Florence, you've covered the flight, the arrival process at PSA, and an hour-plus drive — a reasonably full travel day, even if none of the individual parts were especially difficult. Many travelers use the drive itself as the first chance to properly relax since starting the journey, which is part of why a direct, door-to-door transfer tends to suit this particular route better than trying to piece together a station connection on top of everything else.

## Final Thoughts

If Florence is actually where you're going, this is the transfer that matters — not a Pisa city guide, and not a short airport hop. The roughly 80-minute drive from PSA is comfortable, predictable, and removes the need to manage a station connection after a flight. Share your flight details and Florence drop-off address when booking, and decide early whether a Leaning Tower stop fits your schedule or is better saved for another day.
