The right way to get around Bologna changes depending on what you're actually doing that day, and treating every trip the same way — always walking, always grabbing a taxi, always assuming a private transfer is overkill — tends to make at least some part of a visit harder than it needs to be. The city offers enough different ways to move that it's worth matching the method to the specific trip rather than defaulting to one habit for the whole stay.

**Getting around Bologna with ease** comes down to recognizing three distinct situations: when walking is genuinely the best option, when a taxi covers the gap efficiently, and when arranging a private transfer in advance saves more hassle than it costs.

## When Walking Is the Right Call

Bologna is, by most measures, an excellent walking city, and its porticoes are the reason why. An estimated 40 kilometers of covered arcaded walkways run through the historic center and beyond, shielding pedestrians from both rain and summer sun in a way few other Italian cities can match. For moving between the main squares, browsing the university quarter, or wandering from one church or market to the next, walking isn't just adequate — it's often the most enjoyable way to experience the city, and trying to arrange transportation for those short hops would be a waste of both time and money.

The caveat is luggage and distance. A pleasant ten-minute portico walk with a day bag is a different proposition with two suitcases in tow, and even a confident walker should factor in that some streets are cobbled or unevenly paved, which slows things down more than a flat map suggests.

## When a Taxi Is the Better Call

For a single unplanned trip — a late dinner reservation across town, a spontaneous decision to see one more sight before it closes, a quick hop when the weather turns — a taxi covers the gap without requiring any advance planning. Taxis make sense when the trip is short, the timing is flexible, and you're not trying to coordinate multiple stops or a tight schedule around it. They're a reasonable default for the kind of trip you decide to take twenty minutes before you take it.

Where taxis get less convenient is anything involving a fixed schedule you can't afford to miss, a group larger than a taxi comfortably holds, or repeated back-and-forth trips across a single day, where the cost and hassle of hailing a new car each time starts to add up.

## When a Private Transfer Is Worth Arranging

A few specific situations in Bologna are worth arranging ahead of time rather than leaving to chance.

**Airport transfers.** Bologna Airport (BLQ) sits close to the center, roughly 6 km away, with a typical drive of 15 to 20 minutes — though traffic, weather, and the time of day can extend that, so it's worth building in a margin rather than cutting it close. A pre-arranged [airport transfer](/airport-transfers/bologna) with flight tracking means no queue and no uncertainty after a long journey.

**Day trips beyond the city.** Bologna's location puts towns like Modena, Parma, and Ferrara within reach for a day out, though there's no single reliable travel-time figure worth quoting for any of them, since the actual drive depends on the specific route, the season, and the day's traffic. A [city-to-city transfer](/city-to-city-transfers) or a booked [chauffeur service](/chauffeur-service) for the day removes the need to coordinate train schedules or manage a rental car in unfamiliar territory.

**BolognaFiere events.** Bologna's exhibition center hosts major trade fairs throughout the year, and traffic and transportation demand around the venue rises sharply during those periods. Arranging a transfer in advance for fair dates avoids competing with a much larger volume of travelers all trying to reach the same place at the same time.

**Multi-stop business days.** A day that involves several meetings across the city, possibly combined with an airport departure at the end, benefits from an [hourly chauffeur](/hourly-chauffeur) who can wait between stops and adjust the schedule as the day actually unfolds, rather than requiring a fresh taxi search after every meeting.

## Corporate Travelers Have Their Own Version of This Problem

Business visitors to Bologna face a variant of the same walk-taxi-transfer decision, just compressed into a tighter schedule. A single meeting near the historic center is often an easy walk or a short taxi ride. A day with several meetings spread across the city, or a schedule that ends with a flight to catch, tends to work better with a [corporate chauffeur](/corporate-chauffeur) who can wait between appointments and adjust for a meeting that runs long, rather than hoping a taxi is available the moment a meeting wraps up. The same logic that applies to a family's airport transfer applies here — the more a day depends on precise timing, the more a pre-arranged vehicle earns its cost over a taxi hailed on the spot.

## The ZTL Complicates Casual Driving

The historic center operates under a ZTL, a restricted traffic zone limiting vehicle access, which is common across Italian cities. This matters less for pedestrians and taxi passengers than it does for anyone considering renting a car to get around the city itself — a rental is a poor fit for navigating the center and is really only useful for reaching towns outside Bologna independently. For getting around inside the city, walking, taxis, and pre-arranged transfers all sidestep the ZTL issue in a way that driving yourself doesn't.

## Weather and Season Change the Calculation

Summer heat can turn a comfortable portico walk into an uncomfortable one by early afternoon, and winter rain, while less of an issue under the arcades themselves, still affects how pleasant it is to be outside waiting for a taxi. Any driving time mentioned here, whether for the airport or a regional day trip, should be treated as a starting estimate rather than a fixed number, since traffic, weather, and road conditions all shift it in practice. Building a little slack into a day's plans, especially one involving multiple stops, tends to matter more than the exact minutes on paper.

## Planning a Full Day That Mixes All Three

A single day in Bologna often ends up using more than one of these methods, and that's fine — the goal isn't picking one approach for the whole trip, it's switching between them as the day's plans actually change. A morning spent walking the porticoes between the main squares might shift to a taxi for a lunch reservation across town, then to a pre-arranged transfer for an afternoon trip out to a regional town, before walking again once you're back in the center for the evening. Thinking through the day in advance, even loosely, makes it easier to decide which legs are worth arranging ahead of time and which can be figured out as you go.

## Matching the Method to the Moment

There's no single best way to get around Bologna — there's a best way for each specific trip within a visit. Walking the historic center under its porticoes, hailing a taxi for a spontaneous errand, and arranging a private transfer for the airport, a regional day trip, or a fair-week event are three different tools for three different jobs, and using the right one for each moment is what actually makes a Bologna visit feel effortless rather than a series of small logistics to solve.
