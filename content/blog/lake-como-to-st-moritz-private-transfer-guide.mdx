Lake Como doesn't have its own separately verified distance or drive-time figure for this specific journey, but since Como sits roughly on the way to that same Chiasso crossing, it's reasonable to expect the trip from the lake to run somewhat shorter than the full Milan figure. What isn't reasonable is putting a specific number on that difference without real data behind it, so treat the Milan figures as useful general context rather than a Como-specific promise.

## The Shape of the Drive

Whatever the exact timing turns out to be for your particular starting point on the lake, the shape of the journey is the same one described for Milan travelers: south to the Chiasso border crossing, then north and east through Switzerland before the road begins its climb into the mountains. That final climb is where the drive changes character most noticeably, trading motorway for mountain pass roads that wind higher with every kilometer, past alpine scenery that shifts from forested slopes to the more open, high-altitude landscape around the Engadin valley itself.

## Crossing Into Switzerland

Switzerland isn't part of the European Union, so this route involves a genuine international border crossing at Chiasso, with its own entry and exit procedures independent of travel within Italy or the wider EU. Travelers should carry valid identification and check the entry requirements that apply to their nationality before departure, rather than assuming the crossing works the same way as moving between two Italian regions. A private chauffeur manages the logistics of the drive itself, but the documentation is worth confirming in advance. For more detail on how these crossings generally work, see [international border crossing transfers](/international-border-crossing-transfers).

## Winter Driving on Mountain Roads

The final stretch into the Engadin valley is mountain driving, and mountain roads behave differently than the flat lake routes around Como. In winter, that means the possibility of snow or ice affecting the higher sections of the route, and travel times that can shift meaningfully from the general estimate depending on conditions on a given day. Seasonal closures on some mountain roads are also worth checking ahead of a winter trip, since routing can change if a particular pass isn't accessible. A chauffeur experienced with the route will plan around current conditions, but it's worth going into a winter booking with the expectation that flexibility matters more here than on a shorter, flatter drive.

## Milder Seasons, Still Mountain Roads

Outside winter, the route is generally more predictable, though mountain weather can still shift over the course of a single day even in summer, and the roads themselves remain narrower and more winding than open motorway regardless of the season. Spring and autumn sit somewhere in between — usually clear, but worth checking closer to your travel date rather than assuming a fixed year-round timing.

## The Altitude Change, and What It Means for You

Lake Como sits at a modest elevation, and St. Moritz sits considerably higher, in the heart of the Engadin valley. Some travelers notice the change, particularly with a fairly direct transition from lakeside to high mountain valley without much time to adjust gradually. It's not something to be alarmed about, but it's worth being aware of, especially if you're planning anything physically demanding on arrival day, and worth mentioning to your driver if anyone in your group has a known sensitivity to altitude changes.

## What to Pack for the Trip

What belongs in your luggage depends heavily on when you're traveling. Winter visitors heading to St. Moritz for its alpine skiing reputation typically bring ski equipment, heavier boots, and bulkier outerwear on top of ordinary luggage, all of which affects how much space the vehicle needs. Summer travelers pack lighter, but layers are still worth including given how much temperatures can shift with elevation over the course of a day, even when it's warm at lake level. Either way, flagging any bulky items like ski bags when you book helps in choosing the right vehicle for the trip.
