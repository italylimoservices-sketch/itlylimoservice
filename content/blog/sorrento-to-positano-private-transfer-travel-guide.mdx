Of all the Amalfi Coast towns reachable from Sorrento, Positano is usually the first one people mention. It's the closest of the well-known coast towns to Sorrento, geographically — closer than Amalfi town, and considerably closer than Ravello, which sits up in the hills above the water. That proximity, combined with Positano's reputation as the coast's most photographed town, makes it the natural first stop for a lot of visitors, and often the only one they manage if their time is limited.

## The Drive from Sorrento

There's no single, verified figure for exactly how long the drive from Sorrento to Positano takes, and any guide that gives you one precise number without caveats is oversimplifying. The road follows the coastline closely, with tight curves, limited overtaking room, and stretches that narrow down to barely two lanes as they pass through small settlements along the way. In light traffic, on a quiet morning outside peak season, it's a relatively quick hop. On a summer afternoon, with tour buses, rental cars, and scooters all sharing the same narrow stretch, the same drive can take considerably longer. Treat any time estimate as just that — an estimate, dependent on season, time of day, and conditions on a given trip.

What doesn't vary is the drive itself as an experience. This stretch of coast road is genuinely scenic, with views opening up over the water at various points, and a private chauffeur who knows the route can make the drive part of the day rather than just a means of getting from one place to another — slowing down at a viewpoint, timing the approach into Positano to avoid the worst of a traffic backup, or simply handling the switchbacks so you can look at the scenery instead of the road.

## Arriving in Positano

Positano's layout is part of what makes it famous, and also part of what makes arriving there by car more complicated than in most towns. It's built into a steep hillside, with pastel-colored buildings stacked above a small beach, and streets that were laid out long before cars were part of the picture. Vehicle access into the town center is limited, and through much of the year there are restrictions on which vehicles can enter certain areas, along with limited parking near the center itself.

In practice, this usually means a private transfer drops you at a point near the top of town or at a designated arrival area, from which the rest of Positano is explored on foot — down through the stepped lanes toward the beach and back up again, since the town is essentially built on a slope in both directions. This is normal for Positano and not a shortcoming of the transfer; even locals and long-term visitors navigate the town this way. A driver familiar with Positano will know the current drop-off arrangements and the most sensible point to leave you, which can change depending on the season and any local traffic restrictions in effect at the time.

It's worth packing accordingly. If you're arriving with substantial luggage — say, at the start of a multi-night stay in Positano rather than a day trip — factor in that the walk from a vehicle drop-off point to many hotels involves stairs, and some properties are only reachable on foot from certain directions. Confirming this with your accommodation in advance saves a difficult first ten minutes in town.

## Why Positano Works Well as a Day Trip

A lot of visitors based in Sorrento treat Positano as a half-day or full-day outing rather than an overnight stay, and the geography supports that. Being the closest major coast town to Sorrento makes it realistic to head over in the morning, spend several hours exploring the beach, the lanes, and the views, and return to Sorrento by evening without the trip dominating the entire day the way a visit to Ravello or a multi-town itinerary might.

That also makes Positano a common starting point for travelers who want a taste of the Amalfi Coast without committing to a longer, multi-stop day. If your time in the Sorrento area is limited to a few days, a focused Positano trip is often more satisfying than trying to rush through several coast towns in a single outing — you get to actually sit somewhere, have a coffee looking out over the water, and take in the town rather than ticking it off a list.

For travelers who do want to see more of the coast in one trip, Positano pairs naturally with a stop in [Amalfi town](/blog/sorrento-to-amalfi-private-transfer-routes-and-travel-tips) further along the same road, and we've written more generally about combining Sorrento with multiple Amalfi Coast excursions over a longer stay in a [separate guide](/blog/sorrento-and-amalfi-coast-private-chauffeur-travel-guide).

## Booking a Sorrento-to-Positano Transfer

A few practical points to consider when arranging this specific transfer:

- **One-way versus round trip.** Some travelers head to Positano for the day and return to Sorrento in the evening; others use Positano as a stop on the way further along the coast, or as a one-way transfer if they're staying there overnight.
- **Timing around crowds.** Positano gets busy in the late morning through mid-afternoon during peak season. An earlier departure from Sorrento, or a later one for an evening visit, can make for a noticeably calmer experience.
- **Luggage.** If you're moving accommodation rather than day-tripping, mention the amount of luggage when booking, since the walk from drop-off to many Positano hotels isn't always short or flat.
- **Group size.** A couple or solo traveler fits comfortably in a [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan), while families or small groups with more bags often prefer the extra space of a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv).

## What to Do Once You're There

Positano doesn't require much of an agenda, which is part of its appeal. Many visitors are happy simply walking down through the town toward the beach, stopping at viewpoints and small shops along the way, and spending time at the water before making the climb back up. The town's compact size means you can cover the center in a few hours without feeling rushed, though it's also easy to spend a full day there without running out of things to look at.

Boat trips are a popular add-on for visitors already in Positano, since the town's small harbor offers connections along the coast and out toward Capri for those who want to see the coastline from the water rather than the road. If that's part of your plan, it's worth building the extra time into your day and coordinating your return transfer to Sorrento around it, since a boat excursion can shift your schedule later than a straightforward land-based visit would.

Photography is another reason people build extra time into a Positano stop. The town's stacked, colorful buildings against the hillside are best appreciated from a few specific vantage points, some reached only by walking further up or down than the main path between the drop-off point and the beach. If this is a priority for you, mentioning it to your driver in advance can help with timing the visit around better light, particularly in the late afternoon.
