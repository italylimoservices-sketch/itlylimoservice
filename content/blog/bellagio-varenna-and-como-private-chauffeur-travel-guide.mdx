Lake Como isn't one destination so much as a handful of them arranged around the same body of water, and the three towns travelers ask about most — Bellagio, Varenna and Como — are different enough from each other that picking among them deserves more thought than picking a hotel by star rating. ## Three Towns, Three Different Lakes

It helps to start with what actually separates these places, since from a distance they can blur into a single idea of "Lake Como." Bellagio sits on the promontory where the lake's three branches meet, a genuinely distinctive position that gives it water views in multiple directions and a reputation as the pearl of the lake. Varenna sits across the water on the eastern shore, quieter and more residential, built around a lakeside walkway rather than a dramatic point of land. Como town occupies the lake's southwestern tip, the largest and most accessible of the three, with a real town center and rail connections back to Milan that the other two don't have.

None of these towns is a smaller or lesser version of another — they're genuinely different experiences that happen to share the same lake, and the right one (or combination) depends on what you're actually looking for from a lake visit.

## Bellagio: The One Most People Picture

When someone pictures Lake Como without having been there, there's a good chance they're picturing Bellagio without realizing it. Its position at the meeting point of the lake's branches makes for views that are hard to replicate elsewhere on the lake, and that reputation means it draws the largest share of day-trip visitors, particularly during summer. The tradeoff for that scenery is crowding — narrower streets that fill up quickly in peak season, and a pace that can feel considerably busier than the other two towns during the day's busiest hours.

Bellagio suits travelers prioritizing the classic Lake Como image, especially those visiting once and wanting to see the town most associated with the lake's reputation.

## Varenna: The Quieter Alternative

Varenna doesn't try to compete with Bellagio's dramatic setting, and that's precisely its appeal to a certain kind of traveler. Its lakeside walkway runs along the water past a smaller, calmer town than Bellagio's, and it draws a noticeably lighter crowd even during the lake's busier months. Travelers who've already done the more famous stops, or who simply prefer a slower pace over the highest-profile scenery, tend to gravitate toward Varenna once they know it's an option rather than an afterthought.

Varenna suits travelers who want to actually relax on the lake rather than move through a crowded, high-traffic town center, and it works particularly well as a base for a multi-day stay for that reason.

## Como Town: The Practical Choice

Como town won't show up on as many postcards as Bellagio, but it makes the strongest practical case of the three. Its position at the lake's southwestern tip means the shortest, most direct route from Milan, road connections that don't require the additional shoreline driving Bellagio and Varenna involve, and train service back to the city that neither of the other two towns offers directly. It's also the largest of the three, with a genuine city feel along parts of its lakefront and enough infrastructure that it works well as a standalone destination rather than only a jumping-off point.

Como town suits travelers who want the lake experience with the least amount of additional travel, or who'd rather base themselves somewhere with more everyday amenities than a smaller village.
