Rome is one of those cities that can make a short holiday feel like a journey through thousands of years of history. Around one corner, you can find ancient Roman ruins. Around the next, there may be a beautiful fountain, a lively piazza, a small Italian restaurant, or a quiet street that feels completely different from the busy tourist areas.

Often called the Eternal City, Rome is famous for its history, architecture, art, food, churches, museums, and unforgettable landmarks. It is also a city that rewards travelers who slow down a little. You can spend the morning visiting the Colosseum, have lunch with a plate of fresh pasta, walk through charming neighborhoods in the afternoon, and finish the evening with gelato near a historic piazza.

If you're planning your first trip, this Rome travel guide will help you understand what to see, where to go, what to eat, how long to stay, and how to get around the city.

## Why Visit Rome?

Rome offers a little bit of everything.

History lovers can explore ancient monuments and archaeological sites. Art lovers have world-famous museums and churches to discover. Food lovers can try traditional Roman dishes in local neighborhoods. Families can enjoy parks, piazzas, and attractions that bring history to life.

The best part is that many of Rome's highlights are relatively close to one another. You can walk between several attractions while enjoying the streets and architecture along the way.

Rome is also a great starting point for exploring other parts of Italy. Once you've enjoyed the city, you can plan day trips to places such as Tivoli, Frascati, or Ostia Antica.

Whether you're visiting for two days or staying for a full week, Rome has plenty to keep you busy.

## Best Things to Do in Rome

There are countless things to do in Rome, but some attractions should be near the top of your list.

### 1. Visit the Colosseum

No Rome trip feels complete without seeing the Colosseum.

This enormous ancient amphitheater is one of the city's most recognizable landmarks and one of the best places to begin exploring ancient Rome. Walking around the structure gives you a sense of how impressive Roman engineering was thousands of years ago.

Try to allow enough time to explore rather than simply stopping for a photo. If you're interested in history, a guided visit can make the experience much more interesting because you'll learn about the people, events, and architecture connected to the monument.

The Colosseum can get extremely busy, especially during popular travel periods, so planning your visit ahead of time is a smart move.

### 2. Explore the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill

The Roman Forum sits close to the Colosseum and is an essential stop for anyone interested in ancient Rome.

This archaeological area was once the center of political, religious, and public life in the Roman world. Today, you can walk among ancient temples, government buildings, columns, and ruins.

Palatine Hill is nearby and is also worth exploring. It offers a different perspective of the archaeological area and connects directly with the story of ancient Rome.

A visit to the Colosseum, Roman Forum, and Palatine Hill can easily take several hours, so avoid squeezing everything into a rushed schedule.

### 3. See the Pantheon

The Pantheon is one of Rome's most impressive ancient buildings.

Its huge dome and distinctive interior make it a must-see attraction in the historic center. One of the most interesting features is the opening at the top of the dome, known as the oculus.

The Pantheon is located in a lively part of central Rome, making it easy to combine with other attractions. After visiting, you can walk toward Piazza Navona, enjoy a coffee, or simply explore the surrounding streets.

### 4. Make a Wish at the Trevi Fountain

The Trevi Fountain is one of Rome's most famous landmarks and one of the most photographed spots in the city.

The fountain is especially beautiful when illuminated in the evening, but it can be crowded throughout the day. If you want a more relaxed experience, consider visiting early in the morning.

Remember that the Trevi Fountain is more than just a photo stop. Take a moment to admire the details of the sculptures and the surrounding architecture.

And yes, tossing a coin into the fountain is part of the tradition for many visitors.

### 5. Visit the Vatican Museums

The Vatican Museums are home to an enormous collection of art and historical objects.

For many visitors, the highlight is the Sistine Chapel, famous for Michelangelo's ceiling. However, there is much more to see throughout the museums.

Because the collection is so large, first-time visitors can easily feel overwhelmed. It's better to decide beforehand which sections interest you most and give yourself enough time to explore.

The Vatican area can become very busy, so advance planning is particularly useful here.

### 6. See St. Peter's Basilica

St. Peter's Basilica is another major attraction in the Vatican area.

The building is famous for its scale, architecture, artwork, and religious importance. Even travelers who aren't particularly interested in religious history often appreciate the artistic and architectural details.

When visiting churches in Rome and Vatican City, dress respectfully. Shoulders and knees may need to be covered, particularly when entering religious sites.

### 7. Spend Time at Piazza Navona

Piazza Navona is one of the most enjoyable squares in central Rome.

The large open space, fountains, surrounding buildings, cafes, and street atmosphere make it a great place to take a break while sightseeing.

It works especially well as part of a walking route that includes the Pantheon and nearby historic streets.

Rather than rushing from one attraction to another, grab a drink or coffee and enjoy the atmosphere for a while. That's often when Rome starts to feel less like a checklist of tourist attractions and more like a real city.

### 8. Explore Villa Borghese and the Galleria Borghese

If you want a break from busy streets, head toward Villa Borghese.

This large green area provides space to walk, relax, and enjoy a quieter side of Rome. Nearby, the Galleria Borghese is a popular destination for visitors interested in art.

The gallery is known for its impressive collection, while the surrounding park makes the area a good choice for a slower afternoon.

## Best Areas to Explore in Rome

Choosing the right neighborhood can make a big difference to your experience.

### Historic Center

Rome's historic center is ideal if you want to stay close to major attractions.

The Pantheon, Trevi Fountain, Piazza Navona, and many other landmarks are within walking distance of one another.

The area can be busy and accommodation may cost more, but the convenience is hard to beat for a first visit.

### Trastevere

Trastevere has a very different atmosphere.

Its narrow streets, old buildings, restaurants, cafes, and lively evening scene make it a favorite among travelers who want to experience another side of Rome.

It's particularly enjoyable around dinner time when the neighborhood starts filling up with locals and visitors.

Don't be afraid to wander without a strict plan. Some of the best moments in Trastevere come from simply exploring its streets.

### Monti

Monti is another interesting neighborhood near the Colosseum.

It has a more relaxed, local feel compared with some of the busiest tourist areas. You'll find cafes, restaurants, small shops, and streets that are pleasant to explore on foot.

Its location also makes it convenient for travelers who want to combine neighborhood exploration with visits to ancient Roman attractions.

### Prati and the Vatican Area

Prati is a practical choice for travelers planning to spend a lot of time around Vatican City.

The neighborhood has restaurants, shops, and accommodation options and generally feels more residential than the areas immediately around some major tourist landmarks.

It's also a useful base if your itinerary includes the Vatican Museums and St. Peter's Basilica.

### Testaccio

Food lovers may want to add Testaccio to their Rome itinerary.

The neighborhood is known for its connection to traditional Roman food and its more local atmosphere. It's a good place to get away from the main tourist routes and experience a different side of the city.

## What Food Should You Try in Rome?

A Rome trip isn't complete without trying traditional Roman food.

Here are a few dishes worth looking for:

### Carbonara

Roman carbonara is a classic pasta dish traditionally made with pasta, eggs, Pecorino Romano, and guanciale.

It's rich, simple, and incredibly satisfying.

### Cacio e Pepe

Cacio e pepe is another Roman favorite. It combines pasta with Pecorino Romano and black pepper to create a surprisingly flavorful dish from just a few ingredients.

### Amatriciana

Amatriciana is traditionally prepared with tomato, Pecorino Romano, guanciale, and chili.

It's another great example of how Roman cooking can turn a small number of ingredients into something memorable.

### Supplì

Supplì are fried rice croquettes, often filled with mozzarella. They're an easy snack when you're exploring the city.

### Roman Pizza

Roman pizza comes in different styles, so don't expect every pizzeria to serve the same thing.

Try a few different places during your stay and see which style you prefer.

### Gelato

And, of course, leave room for gelato.

A cold gelato after several hours of walking around Rome is hard to beat.

## How Many Days Do You Need in Rome?

The ideal length of a Rome trip depends on your interests.

### Rome in 2 Days

Two days can work if you're short on time.

Focus on the major highlights:

**Day 1:** Colosseum, Roman Forum, Palatine Hill, Monti, and an evening in the historic center.

**Day 2:** Vatican Museums, St. Peter's Basilica, Pantheon, Piazza Navona, and Trevi Fountain.

It will be busy, but you can see many of the city's biggest attractions.

### Rome in 3 Days

Three days is a much more comfortable option.

You can dedicate the first day to ancient Rome, the second to Vatican City and central Rome, and the third to neighborhoods, parks, food, shopping, and attractions you may have missed.

Having an extra day also means you don't need to rush through every stop.

### Rome in 4–5 Days

Four or five days gives you the chance to slow down.

You can explore neighborhoods such as Trastevere and Testaccio, visit additional museums, enjoy longer meals, and even take a day trip outside Rome.

For travelers who prefer a relaxed holiday rather than nonstop sightseeing, this is an excellent amount of time.

## Best Time to Visit Rome

Rome can be visited throughout the year, but each season has a different feel.

### Spring

Spring is a popular period because temperatures are generally pleasant for sightseeing and walking.

The city can become busy, so major attractions may require more planning.

### Summer

Summer brings long days and plenty of sunshine, but Rome can become hot and crowded.

If you're visiting during the hottest part of the year, start sightseeing early, take breaks during the afternoon, and keep water with you.

### Autumn

Autumn can be a comfortable time to explore Rome.

The weather is often suitable for walking, while the atmosphere can feel less intense than during peak summer periods.

### Winter

Winter is generally quieter than the busiest travel months.

You'll still be able to visit Rome's major attractions, and shorter queues can make sightseeing easier. Just remember that opening hours and attraction schedules can vary by season.

If you want a balance between comfortable weather and manageable crowds, consider the shoulder seasons and plan popular attractions in advance.

## Getting Around Rome

Walking is one of the best ways to experience Rome.

Many attractions are relatively close together, and walking allows you to discover streets and small squares you might otherwise miss.

However, Rome is large, and you won't want to walk everywhere.

The city has public transportation, including buses, metro services, and trains. Public transport can be useful for longer distances, especially when you're staying outside the historic center.

Taxis and private transportation are another option, particularly for [airport trips](/airport-transfers), families, groups, travelers carrying luggage, or visitors who simply want a more comfortable journey.

A good approach is to combine walking with public or private transportation rather than relying on only one option.

## Day Trips From Rome

If you're staying for four or more days, consider adding a day trip.

### Tivoli

Tivoli is one of the most popular day-trip options from Rome.

It's known for historic villas, gardens, fountains, and beautiful surroundings. A trip to Tivoli gives you a chance to step away from the busy streets of central Rome for a day.

### Ostia Antica

Ostia Antica is another interesting option for history lovers.

The ancient archaeological site provides an opportunity to explore Roman remains in a setting outside the city center.

It's especially appealing if you enjoyed the Colosseum and Roman Forum and want to see more ancient Roman history.

### Frascati

Frascati is located in the hills outside Rome and is known for its food, wine tradition, and relaxed atmosphere.

It's a good option for travelers who want a slower day away from the major attractions.

## Rome Travel Tips for First-Time Visitors

A few simple tips can make your trip much easier.

### Book Popular Attractions in Advance

The Colosseum, Vatican Museums, and other major attractions can get very busy.

Check official booking information before your trip and plan your sightseeing days around your confirmed entry times.

### Start Early

Rome's most famous attractions are often busiest later in the day.

Starting early can make sightseeing more comfortable and give you more time for other activities.

### Wear Comfortable Shoes

This one sounds obvious, but it's important.

You'll probably walk much more than you expect. Rome has plenty of uneven streets, stone surfaces, and stairs, so comfortable shoes are a must.

### Carry Water

Rome can get hot, particularly in summer.

Keep a bottle of water with you and take regular breaks during long sightseeing days.

### Dress Respectfully for Churches

Many churches and religious sites expect visitors to dress appropriately.

Keep your shoulders and knees covered when required, especially when visiting major religious sites.

### Don't Overpack Your Itinerary

Rome has too much to see in a single trip.

Trying to visit ten attractions in one day may leave you exhausted and unable to enjoy any of them properly.

Leave some space in your schedule for coffee, lunch, wandering, and unexpected discoveries.

### Keep an Eye on Your Belongings

As in any major tourist destination, stay aware of your belongings in crowded places and on busy public transportation.

A small amount of awareness can go a long way.

## Is Rome Expensive?

Rome can fit different travel budgets.

Accommodation prices vary considerably depending on the neighborhood, season, and type of property. Food also ranges from affordable casual meals to high-end dining.

You don't need to spend a fortune to enjoy the city. Some of the best experiences, such as walking through historic neighborhoods, visiting public squares, admiring architecture, and exploring the city's streets, cost little or nothing.

The biggest expenses are usually accommodation, major attraction tickets, dining, and transportation.

Planning ahead can help you control your budget.

## Is Rome Good for Families?

Yes. Rome can be a great destination for families, although you'll want to plan your days carefully.

Children may enjoy seeing the Colosseum, ancient ruins, fountains, large piazzas, and other attractions that make history feel real.

Don't plan full-day sightseeing without breaks. Mix major attractions with parks, food stops, gelato, and free time.

If you're traveling with young children or a larger family, private transportation can also make airport journeys and longer trips around the city easier.

## Rome for Couples

Rome is also a fantastic destination for couples.

You can spend the morning visiting historic landmarks, enjoy lunch at a small restaurant, walk through Trastevere, and finish the day with dinner or an evening stroll through the historic center.

For couples, the city is often at its best when you don't rush.

Leave some time to simply walk, stop for coffee, sit in a piazza, and enjoy the atmosphere together.

## Final Thoughts

Rome isn't a city you simply check off a list.

Yes, you should see the Colosseum. You should visit the Pantheon, admire the Trevi Fountain, explore the Vatican Museums, and walk through the historic center. But don't stop there.

Give yourself time to sit in a small piazza, eat a plate of pasta, wander through Trastevere, enjoy a gelato, or take a slower walk through the city after sunset.

That's where Rome really comes alive.

For first-time visitors, a three-day itinerary is a great starting point. If you have more time, add another day for neighborhoods, museums, food experiences, or a trip outside the city.

And when it comes to transportation, choose what makes sense for your trip. Public transportation and walking work well for many journeys, while a [private chauffeur](/chauffeur-service) or [Rome airport transfer](/airport-transfers/rome-fiumicino) can make airport arrivals, group travel, and longer journeys much easier.

With a little planning and enough time to enjoy the city rather than rush through it, Rome can easily become one of the most memorable stops on your Italy trip.
