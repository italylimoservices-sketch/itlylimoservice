Booking a driver for the Amalfi Coast is a slightly different decision than booking one for a city like Rome or Florence. The road connecting Positano, Amalfi and Ravello is narrow, winding and often congested in season, which means vehicle size, route familiarity and flexibility matter more here than they would for a straightforward city transfer. Here's how to think through the choice.

## Start With Vehicle Size, Not Preference

It's tempting to pick a vehicle based on comfort alone, but on the Amalfi Coast, size has practical consequences beyond legroom. The SS163 coast road has sections narrow enough that larger vehicles need more care to pass oncoming traffic, and the centers of Positano and Amalfi have tight turns and limited maneuvering room near common drop-off points.

- A solo traveler or couple is generally well matched to an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) — easiest to place at a hotel entrance or a tight viewpoint pull-off.
- Groups of four or five, or anyone with extra luggage, often prefer a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) for the added space without stepping up to a van.
- Families or small groups of six or seven typically need an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), though it's worth asking your driver how that size handles the tightest stretches of the itinerary, since it may affect routing or where exactly you're picked up and dropped off.

None of this means larger groups can't be accommodated comfortably — they routinely are — but it's a reason to be upfront about group size and luggage when booking, rather than deciding on vehicle type at the last minute.

## Ask About Local Road Knowledge Directly

"Local knowledge" can sound like a vague selling point, so it's worth asking about it in specific terms. A driver who genuinely knows the Amalfi Coast route can usually speak to things like: which times of day the road through Positano tends to back up, where a safe stop for photos actually exists versus where it just looks like one, and how the drive to Ravello's hillside differs from the coastal run to Amalfi town. A driver who can only answer in generalities may still get you there safely, but they're less likely to build in the small adjustments — timing, stop selection — that make the difference between a tense drive and a relaxed one.

It's a fair question to ask directly: how often does this service run the Amalfi Coast route, and does the same driver handle both airport transfers and full-day touring, or are those treated as separate specialties.

## Hourly vs. Point-to-Point: Match the Format to the Day

Amalfi Coast bookings generally come in two shapes, and picking the right one matters more here than for a typical city transfer.

A point-to-point transfer — say, Naples Airport to your Positano hotel, or Sorrento to Amalfi town for a fixed appointment — is straightforward and cost-effective when you genuinely know your start and end point and don't need stops along the way.

An [hourly chauffeur](/hourly-chauffeur) arrangement suits a day where you want to see more than one town, stop for photos, or leave room for a lunch that runs long. Given how much there is to see between Positano, Amalfi and Ravello, many visitors find that a single point-to-point transfer undersells what a day on this coast can actually offer, and that hourly hire is worth the difference for a full touring day.

If you're not sure which you need, it helps to sketch out the day in advance: is this a one-stop journey, or a day with three towns and several stops in between. That answer usually settles the format on its own.

## Build In Flexibility for Photo Stops and Detours

One of the genuine pleasures of this coast is that the views change constantly, and plenty of visitors want to stop somewhere the itinerary didn't originally plan for. A chauffeur who's used to this route tends to expect it — pulling into a safe overlook without needing much notice, or adjusting timing if a stop runs ten minutes longer than planned. This is worth raising when you book, especially if photography or a leisurely pace matters to you more than covering ground quickly. It's also a good reason to lean toward hourly hire rather than a fixed transfer if flexibility is a priority, since a point-to-point booking is generally timed around getting from A to B rather than accommodating detours.

## How Booking Confirmations Work

As with most private chauffeur bookings, an initial reservation on the Amalfi Coast usually captures the essentials — arrival details or start location, passenger count, luggage, and a general sense of the day's plan. More specific details, including exactly who's driving, are typically confirmed closer to the travel date rather than at the moment of booking. That's standard practice rather than a sign anything's unsettled, but it's worth building in a little lead time for an Amalfi Coast booking specifically, since summer demand on this stretch of coast is genuinely high and last-minute availability can be tighter than in a larger city. If you have a specific requirement — a child seat, an early morning start to beat traffic through Positano — it's worth stating it plainly at booking so it can be confirmed rather than assumed.

## Consider the Whole Stay, Not Just One Transfer

If your trip includes an airport transfer, a multi-town touring day and eventually a departure transfer, it's worth mentioning all of that at once rather than booking each piece separately as it comes up. Services that handle the Amalfi Coast regularly can often coordinate a multi-day stay more smoothly when they understand the full shape of it from the start — which airport or station you're arriving through, how many touring days you want, and where the trip ends. This also gives the service a chance to flag anything worth knowing in advance, such as whether a particular hotel's access road suits every vehicle size, before you're standing outside it with luggage.

This matters more on the Amalfi Coast than in a city with a conventional grid of streets, since a handful of hotels and villas sit down access roads that are themselves narrow, occasionally requiring a smaller vehicle for the final stretch even if the rest of your trip uses something larger. It's a reasonable thing to ask about directly when you book, particularly if your accommodation is in Positano or along one of Ravello's hillside lanes.

## Questions Worth Asking Before You Book

A short checklist tends to cover what actually matters for this route:

- How many towns do I actually want to see in a day, and does that call for an hourly arrangement rather than a single transfer?
- What vehicle fits my group and luggage, and does the driver have any notes on how that size handles the tightest sections of the route?
- Has this driver run the Amalfi Coast route regularly, and can they speak specifically to traffic patterns and viewpoint stops?
- If plans shift — a stop runs long, a restaurant reservation moves — how does the service handle that kind of change?

## Matching the Choice to Your Trip

There's no universally "right" choice here — a business traveler needing a single reliable transfer from Naples Airport to a coastal hotel has different needs than a family spending a full day touring three towns. What matters is being specific about your own trip rather than picking generically: describe the group, the luggage, the pace you want, and whether the day has one stop or several.
