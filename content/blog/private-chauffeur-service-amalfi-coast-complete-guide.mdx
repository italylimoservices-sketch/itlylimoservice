The Amalfi Coast looks the way it does in photographs because the road has nowhere else to go. It hugs a cliff face for roughly 50 kilometers between Vietri sul Mare and Positano, ducking through tunnels, wrapping around blind curves, and offering a sea view one moment and a rock wall the next. That single road, the SS163, is also how nearly everyone gets between Positano, Amalfi and Ravello, whether they're arriving by car, bus or private driver. Understanding how a **private chauffeur service on the Amalfi Coast** actually works — where trips usually start, what the road demands, and where a driver earns their keep — makes it much easier to plan a visit that doesn't lose half its charm to logistics.

## Getting to the Coast: Naples Airport and Sorrento as Starting Points

Naples International Airport (NAP) is the nearest major airport to the Amalfi Coast, and it's where most international visitors begin this leg of a trip. The airport sits close to central Naples — about 7 km away, usually a 15 to 20 minute drive in normal traffic — but the coast itself is another matter. A transfer from Naples Airport to Sorrento typically runs 60 to 75 minutes, and from there the roads narrow further as they continue toward Positano and Amalfi. Exact timing to Positano, Amalfi town or Ravello specifically depends heavily on traffic, season and which town you're headed to, so it's worth treating any quoted time as a reasonable estimate rather than a guarantee. Sorrento is the other common starting point. Many visitors base themselves there for a few nights and use it as a launch pad for coast excursions, which keeps each day trip shorter than driving the whole way from Naples each time. For visitors coming from Rome rather than flying into Naples, the drive to the Amalfi Coast is about 280 km and takes roughly 3.5 hours. That's a long day if you're trying to see the coast on arrival, so many travelers either break the journey with a night in Naples or Sorrento, or plan the Rome-to-coast drive as its own dedicated travel day. ## Why the Road Itself Changes the Calculus

The SS163 wasn't built for the volume of traffic it now carries. It's narrow enough in places that two buses meeting from opposite directions sometimes have to negotiate who backs up, and the curves are frequent and tight enough that a driver unfamiliar with the road has to work harder just to keep pace with local traffic, let alone enjoy the scenery. This is genuinely one of Italy's most scenic drives, but it rewards familiarity — knowing where the road opens up for a safe viewpoint stop, which stretches get congested at which times of day, and how to time a passage through Positano's center to avoid the worst of it.

This is the practical core of what a private chauffeur adds here: a driver who has done this drive many times over reads the road differently than a rental car driver seeing it for the first time. That translates into a calmer ride for passengers, better-judged stops, and fewer of the small stressful moments — a near-miss on a blind curve, a wrong turn into a one-way section — that can otherwise chip away at a day meant to be relaxing.

## Touring Positano, Amalfi and Ravello

Most visitors want to see more than one town, and the three main stops each offer something different. Positano is the postcard image — pastel houses stacked on the hillside — but it's also the most congested and the hardest to park in. Amalfi town is flatter, with the cathedral and a working harbor, and makes a natural lunch or midday stop. Ravello sits higher up, reached by a steep side road, and trades beach access for quiet gardens and long views over the coastline.

A private driver makes it realistic to see two or even three of these towns in a single day without the exhausting logistics of parking and re-parking a rental car in each one, or working around a bus or ferry timetable that may not line up with how long you actually want to spend somewhere. If a viewpoint catches your eye on the way, or a stop runs long, an [hourly chauffeur](/hourly-chauffeur) arrangement lets that happen without upending the rest of the day. A fixed point-to-point transfer works fine if your plan really is just getting from one specific place to another, but a multi-town day benefits from the flexibility of hourly hire.

## Choosing the Right Vehicle

Vehicle choice matters more here than on a typical city transfer, because the road itself has width constraints in places. A solo traveler or couple is usually well suited to an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan). Groups of four or five, or anyone with extra luggage, often do better in a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv). Families or small groups of six or seven typically need an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) — though it's worth knowing upfront that larger vehicles have less margin on the tightest curves and through the center of Positano, so a driver may plan the route slightly differently than for a sedan. ## Planning Ahead

Because both Positano and Amalfi get genuinely busy in peak season, and because the drive itself takes longer than the straight-line distance suggests, it's worth booking Amalfi Coast transportation with a little more lead time than you might for a city transfer, and being upfront when booking about how many towns you actually want to see in a day.
