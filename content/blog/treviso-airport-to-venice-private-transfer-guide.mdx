Book a flight to "Venice" through a low-cost carrier, and there's a reasonable chance you'll actually land at Treviso, a smaller airport well outside the city that catches plenty of first-time visitors by surprise. It's not a bad airport, and it's not a bad way to reach Venice — it just requires a slightly different plan than flying into Marco Polo, and knowing that before you book your onward transport makes the arrival far less stressful.

This guide covers what to expect with a **Treviso Airport to Venice transfer**, why it's a longer journey than most travelers assume, and how the final approach into the city still involves the same road-to-water handoff that applies everywhere else around Venice.

## Why You Might Be Flying Into Treviso

Treviso Airport is commonly used by budget and low-cost carriers serving the Venice region, which is why travelers searching for cheap flights to Venice often end up booking a Treviso arrival without fully realizing it until they're checking the map. It's a real, functioning airport with its own terminal and ground transport options — it's simply not the same airport as Marco Polo, and it sits considerably farther from the city.

If you booked your flight based on price rather than airport name, it's worth double-checking your itinerary now rather than being caught off guard on arrival day.

## How Far Is Treviso From Venice, Really?

This is the part worth being honest about: Treviso is noticeably farther from Venice than Marco Polo Airport is, and travel time depends heavily on traffic, road conditions, and the time of day. Rather than quoting a specific figure that might not hold up on your travel date, the more useful approach is to request a quote for your exact dates and flight times when arranging transport, so you get a realistic estimate rather than a generic number that may not reflect current conditions.

What's consistent regardless of exact timing is the structure of the journey — a road transfer to the edge of Venice, followed by a separate leg into the city itself.

## The Road Transfer: Treviso to Piazzale Roma or Mestre

Like every other transfer into Venice, a private car from Treviso Airport can only take you as far as the mainland. Venice's historic center is entirely car-free, so no vehicle — regardless of where it started its journey — can drive directly into the canal city. The two realistic road destinations are:

- **Piazzale Roma**, the point where mainland roads end and the historic center begins, useful if you're heading straight into the canal city
- **Venice Mestre**, the mainland part of Venice with its own train station and hotel options, useful if your accommodation is on the mainland side rather than on the islands

Which one makes sense depends entirely on where you're staying. A hotel in the historic center means Piazzale Roma is your road destination, with a water taxi or vaporetto covering the rest. A hotel in Mestre means your journey is actually simpler, since it stays on the mainland the whole way with no water crossing required.

## What to Expect at Treviso Airport

Treviso is considerably smaller than Marco Polo, which has upsides and downsides. On the plus side, it's generally quicker to move through after landing — shorter walks, less congestion, and a more compact terminal overall. On the downside, ground transport options are less extensive than at a major international airport, so travelers who haven't planned ahead can find themselves with fewer immediate choices than they'd expect.

This is exactly the kind of airport where having a driver already arranged makes a noticeable difference. Rather than working out an unfamiliar smaller airport's transport options while tired from a flight, a pre-booked pickup means you walk out of arrivals with your next step already decided.

Because the Treviso journey is longer than the Marco Polo route, comfort matters more, particularly for families or anyone arriving after an early or late flight. A standard [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) suits couples or solo travelers with modest luggage, while groups or families with more bags may prefer a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) or an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van), both of which offer considerably more room for the longer drive.

## How Treviso Compares to Marco Polo

It's a fair question: if Treviso is farther away, why do so many travelers end up flying into it? Usually it comes down to which airlines fly where, and low-cost carriers frequently price Treviso routes attractively enough that the extra distance is worth it for many travelers. There's nothing wrong with that trade-off — you just want to walk into it with accurate expectations about the ground transportation rather than assuming the journey will mirror a Marco Polo arrival.

The practical difference mostly comes down to time in the car and the importance of planning ahead. At Marco Polo, a last-minute taxi or an improvised plan is rarely a disaster given the airport's size and the volume of transport options nearby. At a smaller, more distant airport like Treviso, arranging things in advance carries more weight, simply because there's less transport infrastructure to fall back on if your plans change at the last minute.

## Packing and Timing Considerations

Because the journey from Treviso takes longer than from Marco Polo, it's worth thinking about the trip itself as part of your travel day rather than a brief afterthought. If you're arriving on an early morning or late-night flight, confirm that your transfer is booked to align with that specific time, since ground transport options can be more limited outside of peak hours at a smaller airport.

If your hotel is in the historic center, also build in time for the water taxi or vaporetto leg after you reach Piazzale Roma, particularly if you're arriving tired and would rather not rush the final stretch of a long travel day.

## If You're Continuing Beyond Venice

Some travelers use Treviso as an entry point before continuing on to other parts of the Veneto region, including [Verona](/destinations/verona). If that's part of your plan, it's worth mentioning your full itinerary when arranging transport, since a single planned journey tends to work out more smoothly than booking each leg separately as an afterthought.

## Planning Ahead Is the Real Advantage

Treviso isn't a difficult airport to fly into, but it does require a bit more planning than Marco Polo, both because of the extra distance and because it doesn't have the same volume of dedicated transport infrastructure built around it. The two things worth locking in before you fly are your road destination — Piazzale Roma or Mestre, depending on where you're staying — and, if you're headed into the historic center, how you'll cover the final water leg once the car ride ends.
