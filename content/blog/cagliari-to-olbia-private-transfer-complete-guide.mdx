Sardinia doesn't have one obvious "main airport" the way some Italian regions do. Cagliari, on the south coast, and Olbia, in the northeast, both handle significant international and domestic traffic, and plenty of visitors end up needing to move between the two rather than sticking to just one end of the island. A private transfer between them is a genuine cross-island drive, not a quick hop, and it's worth planning the day around that rather than assuming it's a short ride.

## Why Travelers Make This Trip

The most common reason people book a Cagliari to Olbia transfer is a multi-city itinerary. Sardinia is large enough, and its coastline varied enough, that a lot of visitors don't want to base themselves in just one region. A typical pattern is landing in Cagliari, spending time exploring the south — beaches around Villasimius, the city itself, maybe the Sulcis area — and then heading north to pick up a second leg of the trip around Olbia, the Costa Smeralda, or San Teodoro, before flying home from Olbia's airport instead of doubling back to Cagliari.

The reverse happens just as often: fly into Olbia, enjoy the northeast coast first, then transfer south toward Cagliari for a few final days or an onward flight. Either direction, the appeal of a private transfer over renting a car for just this one leg is straightforward — you get where you're going without having to return a rental at a different city than you picked it up, which on Sardinia can add unexpected fees and logistics.

Business travelers also use this route more than you might expect. Cagliari is Sardinia's administrative and commercial center, while Olbia is the gateway to the Costa Smeralda's events, yachting, and hospitality business. Someone with meetings or work in both areas often finds a private transfer more practical than flying between two airports that are both on the same island.

## Distance and Timing: Being Honest About What We Know

We're not going to give you a specific kilometer figure or a drive-time estimate for this route, because there isn't a single verified number worth repeating. What we can say is that Cagliari and Olbia sit at opposite ends of Sardinia — one in the south, one in the northeast — and the drive between them crosses a meaningful stretch of the island, largely through its interior rather than hugging the coast the whole way. That alone tells you this is a multi-hour journey, not something to squeeze into a tight afternoon window.

Actual travel time depends on the specific route taken, traffic through both cities (Cagliari in particular can be slow at peak times), weather, and seasonal road conditions. Sardinia's interior roads vary a fair amount in character — some stretches move quickly, others wind through hillier terrain — so even drivers who know the island well tend to give a range rather than a fixed number when asked how long it takes. ## Planning Your Day Around a Longer Drive

Because this is a longer transfer than most point-to-point trips on the island, it's worth treating it differently than a quick airport run. A few things matter more here than on a short hop:

- **Build in a buffer** if there's a flight, ferry, or hotel check-in deadline on the other end. A cross-island drive gives more opportunity for delays to accumulate than a 20-minute transfer would.
- **Plan for a comfort stop.** On a drive of this length, most travelers appreciate the option to pause along the way rather than pushing straight through. Mention this when booking so your driver can plan accordingly.
- **Decide whether you want a straight transfer or a scenic detour.** Some travelers treat this crossing as a chance to see a bit of Sardinia's interior or an unplanned coastal stretch, rather than purely as transportation from point A to point B. If that interests you, say so in advance rather than trying to add it spontaneously.

## Starting Points: Airport, City, or Somewhere in Between

Where exactly your transfer begins affects how the day is structured. If you're coming straight off a flight into Cagliari-Elmas Airport, your driver can meet you at arrivals and start the northbound leg immediately — convenient if Olbia or the northeast coast is your very next stop. If you're instead departing from a hotel or rental property somewhere in Cagliari after a few days in the south, the pickup point is that address rather than the airport, and it's worth confirming it clearly when you book, especially if it's in a part of the city with limited vehicle access.

The same logic applies at the other end: arriving into central Olbia, a hotel on the Costa Smeralda, or Olbia Airport itself are all different drop-off scenarios, and specifying the exact destination upfront avoids confusion on the day.

## Choosing the Right Vehicle for a Longer Journey

Comfort matters more on a multi-hour drive than it does on a short transfer, so it's worth thinking about vehicle choice a bit more carefully for this route. A couple or solo traveler with a reasonable amount of luggage typically does well in an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan), both comfortable for up to 3 passengers and 2 suitcases over a longer drive. If you're traveling with more bags than that — common on a trip that spans both ends of Sardinia — or if your group is a bit larger, a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) offers more room, seating up to 5 passengers with 4 suitcases.

Families or small groups making this crossing together, particularly if beach gear and extra bags have piled up over a multi-stop Sardinia trip, often prefer a [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), suited to up to 7 passengers and 6 suitcases. Given that this transfer tends to happen mid-trip or at a transition point between two halves of a Sardinia itinerary, it's worth being realistic about how much luggage has accumulated rather than assuming the vehicle category you'd choose for a short airport hop will have enough room.
