Touring Sardinia as a large family or a multi-generational group raises a different set of questions than a trip for two. It's not just about fitting everyone in one vehicle — though that's part of it — it's about keeping a group together over several days on an island where the distances between the coast, the interior, and the main towns are genuinely large. A loosely coordinated plan that works fine for a weekend city trip tends to come apart over a longer Sardinia itinerary with more people involved.

## Sizing the Vehicle for Your Group

The starting point is an honest passenger count, and it's worth counting everyone — grandparents, kids, anyone joining for part of the trip — rather than estimating. Sardinia's chauffeur fleet options scale in a few clear steps:

- A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) fits up to five passengers and four suitcases, which works for a smaller family group traveling without excessive luggage.
- An [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) seats up to seven passengers and carries up to six suitcases, a common fit for a larger family or a small multi-generational group.
- A [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) offers the same seven-passenger, six-suitcase capacity with a different level of finish, worth considering if comfort over longer drives is a priority for the group.

Where groups tend to miscalculate is luggage. A party of seven adults with standard luggage can fit the stated capacity on paper, but add beach gear, a stroller, or a few oversized bags and the same group needs either a larger vehicle or a second one. Giving a realistic account of luggage, not just headcount, when requesting a quote is what avoids finding this out on the day you load up the car.

## When One Vehicle Isn't Enough

Beyond a certain group size, splitting across two vehicles isn't a compromise — it's often the more comfortable option, even if the total passenger count would technically fit into one larger van. A group of ten or twelve people in a single vehicle for a full day of touring gets cramped quickly, especially with luggage and the kind of stops a Sardinia itinerary tends to involve.

Coordinating multiple vehicles for a multi-day Sardinia trip means thinking through a few specifics at the booking stage:

- **Total group size and how you'd like it split.** Families with young children often prefer to keep the same small group together in one car each day rather than reshuffling, and it helps to say so directly.
- **Whether the itinerary is identical for both vehicles or branches at points.** If grandparents want a slower pace with fewer stops while the rest of the group tours more actively, that's worth flagging early rather than assuming it'll sort itself out en route.
- **A shared schedule for pickup and key meeting points.** Even with two vehicles, the group usually wants to arrive at lunch or a hotel check-in together, which means timing needs to be coordinated rather than left to chance.

None of this is complicated to arrange, but it goes more smoothly when it's described clearly in the original booking request rather than worked out after vehicles are already assigned.

## Keeping a Group Together on Long Cross-Island Drives

Sardinia's internal distances are real — there's no verified fixed drive-time figure for any particular route here, since road conditions and season shift those numbers, but the distances between the coast and the interior, or between the island's main airport gateways and the towns people actually stay in, add up quickly. For a group traveling together, a long cross-island transfer raises its own coordination questions:

- **Rest stop timing.** A group with children, older relatives, or just more people in general benefits from agreeing roughly when and how often to stop, rather than one vehicle pushing through while the other wants a break.
- **Keeping vehicles within sight of each other**, where that matters to the group, is worth mentioning at booking — it's a reasonable thing to coordinate with the operator ahead of time rather than leaving to chance on the day.
- **Building in slack rather than a tight schedule.** A multi-vehicle group moving together is only as fast as its slowest stop, so an itinerary with some breathing room tends to hold up better than one planned to the minute.

## Multi-Generational Travel Has Its Own Logistics

A group spanning grandparents, parents, and young children usually has a wider range of needs than a same-age group of friends or colleagues. Mobility considerations for older relatives, car seat needs for the youngest travelers, and a pace that doesn't exhaust either end of the age range all belong in the conversation when planning transportation. Raising these specifics directly in your booking request — ages involved, any mobility needs, car seat requirements — gives the operator a real chance to plan the right vehicles and pacing, rather than discovering a mismatch once the trip is underway. ## Airport Arrival as a Group

Sardinia's three main airport gateways — Cagliari, Olbia, and Alghero — each serve a different part of the island, and a larger group arriving together has more to coordinate at that first step than a couple or a solo traveler would. If the group is flying in on separate flights, or at different times, it's worth deciding in advance whether everyone waits for the last arrival before setting off, or whether vehicles depart in stages as each flight lands. Either approach can work, but it helps the operator plan vehicles and timing correctly if the arrangement is clear from the start rather than figured out at the arrivals hall with a tired, jet-lagged group.

## Matching Pace to the Group, Not Just the Itinerary

A mixed-age or mixed-interest group often has different ideas about what makes a good day. Some members might want an active day of sightseeing with several stops, while others — particularly older relatives or families with very young children — may prefer a slower day with longer rest periods between stops. A private chauffeur arrangement can usually accommodate this better than a fixed group tour, but only if the group's actual preferences are communicated rather than assumed to align. It's worth having that conversation within the group before booking, so the itinerary submitted actually reflects what everyone wants rather than a compromise nobody quite agreed to.

## Planning a Multi-Day Itinerary as a Group

A group trip across Sardinia benefits from thinking about the whole itinerary up front, not just the first transfer. A few things worth deciding before booking:

- Which towns or regions the group wants to cover, and roughly how many overnight stops that implies
- Whether the pace should stay consistent across the whole trip or ease up partway through
- How luggage volume changes across the trip — [a separate consideration in its own right](/blog/sardinia-travel-with-luggage-private-transfer-tips) once beach stops and extra bags are involved
- Whether the group's airport arrival and departure line up with the same gateway, or involve flying into one of Sardinia's three main airports and out of another

## The Short Version

Touring Sardinia as a family or larger group comes down to sizing vehicles honestly, deciding early whether one vehicle or several makes more sense, and building enough flexibility into cross-island drives that the group doesn't fray apart over a long day. None of this is especially complicated, but it benefits from being thought through before the trip starts rather than improvised once everyone's already in the car.
