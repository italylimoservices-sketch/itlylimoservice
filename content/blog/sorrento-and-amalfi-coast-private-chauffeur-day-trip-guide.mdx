Not every visit to the Amalfi Coast needs to be a multi-day stay. If you're based in Sorrento and only have one day to spend on the coast itself, it's entirely possible to build a single, well-paced day trip that leaves in the morning, covers a meaningful slice of the coastline, and gets you back to your Sorrento hotel by evening. This guide lays out one way to structure that day — not a rigid script, but a realistic shape for how the hours tend to work out when a private driver handles the road and you handle the sightseeing.

## Why a Single-Day Itinerary Needs Structure

The Amalfi Coast road is narrow, winding, and often slow, especially through the middle of the day when traffic from cruise groups, coach tours, and other day-trippers builds up. Trying to see too much in one day without a plan tends to backfire — you either rush through towns without really experiencing them, or you end up stuck on the road for longer than expected and cut the day short. A structured itinerary, with a sense of what happens in the morning versus the afternoon, makes it much easier to actually enjoy the towns you stop in rather than just passing through them.

The itinerary below assumes a full day, departing Sorrento in the morning and returning that evening, with a private chauffeur handling the driving throughout so you're free to focus on the views and the stops rather than the road.

## Morning: Departing Sorrento Early

Leaving Sorrento earlier in the morning, well before the coast road fills up with midday traffic, sets the tone for the whole day. Not only is the drive itself easier with less traffic, but arriving in the coast's most popular towns before the bulk of the day's visitors gives you a noticeably calmer first stop.

Positano is the natural first destination on a westward route along the coast, since it sits closest to Sorrento. An early arrival means a quieter walk down through the town's stacked streets to the beach, time to take in the view before the crowds build, and the chance to grab a coffee without competing for table space. An hour to two hours here is generally enough for a first, unhurried look at the town — enough time to walk down to the water and back up, browse a few shops, and get a feel for the place, without turning the whole day into a single-town visit.

## Midday: Continuing to Amalfi

From Positano, the coast road continues on toward Amalfi town, the historic center that gives the whole coastline its name. This stretch of the drive is part of the experience itself — the road keeps close to the cliffside with the sea visible for much of the way — and arriving around midday works well for two reasons: it lines up naturally with lunch, and Amalfi's harbor and main square have enough going on by midday to feel lively without yet being overwhelmed.

Amalfi is worth a slower stop than Positano, both because there's more to see and because it makes a sensible place to pause for lunch before continuing. The Duomo di Sant'Andrea, the town's cathedral, sits above the main piazza and is worth the walk up its staircase, and the streets running back from the harbor are good for a browse — ceramics, limoncello, and local food shops line the main route through town. Plan for roughly two to three hours here, including lunch, which gives enough time to see the cathedral, walk the town center, and eat without feeling rushed.

## Afternoon: A Choice — Ravello or More Coastline

The afternoon is where a single-day itinerary benefits most from flexibility, and it's worth deciding this part of the day based on pace rather than trying to force in every possible stop.

One option is a detour up to **Ravello**, the hilltop town above Amalfi known for its gardens and sweeping coastal views. The road up involves more climbing and curves than the coast road itself, so it adds meaningfully to the day's driving time, but it also offers a different kind of experience — quieter, elevated, and centered on the views from Villa Rufolo or Villa Cimbrone rather than a beach or harbor. This works well if the morning and midday stops went smoothly and there's still a comfortable amount of daylight left.

The other option is to spend the remaining afternoon at a more relaxed pace back along the coast — a longer lunch, more time in Amalfi itself, or simply starting the return drive earlier to avoid the coast road's late-afternoon traffic buildup, which can be just as heavy as the midday peak. Neither choice is objectively better; it depends on how the earlier stops went and how much driving your group is comfortable with in a single day.

## Evening: The Return to Sorrento

Heading back to Sorrento in the late afternoon or early evening generally means retracing the same coast road, so building in time for potential slow stretches matters just as much on the return as it did on the way out. If dinner plans in Sorrento are part of the evening, it's worth giving the return drive a generous buffer rather than cutting it close, since the coast road doesn't reliably speed up just because the day is ending.

A private chauffeur familiar with the route can also adjust the return timing in response to how the day has actually gone — leaving a little earlier if Ravello ran long, or allowing more time in Amalfi if the group wants it, rather than sticking rigidly to a plan made before the day started. That flexibility is really the main advantage of doing this as a chartered day rather than a series of separately booked transfers.

## Rough Shape of the Day

Put together, a single-day version of this trip generally looks something like:

- **Morning:** Early departure from Sorrento, arrival in Positano, one to two hours exploring the beach and streets.
- **Midday:** Continue to Amalfi, two to three hours including lunch, the Duomo, and the town center.
- **Afternoon:** Either the climb up to Ravello for its gardens and views, or a more relaxed continuation along the coast.
- **Evening:** Return drive to Sorrento, timed with a buffer for coast road traffic.

This is a template, not a fixed script — some groups prefer to spend the whole day in just Amalfi and Positano without adding Ravello, while others prioritize Ravello over a longer stop in Amalfi. The point of chartering a private day rather than booking fixed point-to-point transfers is that the plan can bend as the day actually unfolds.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can you really see Positano, Amalfi, and Ravello all in one day from Sorrento?**
It's possible, but it makes for a full, driving-heavy day given the coast road's narrow, winding character. Many travelers prefer to treat Ravello as optional, choosing it only if the morning and midday stops go smoothly and there's comfortable time left in the afternoon.

**What time should I leave Sorrento for a day trip like this?**
An early morning departure, well before the coast road's midday traffic builds up, generally makes the whole day easier and gives you a calmer first stop in Positano.

**Is this day trip better with a private driver than a rental car?**
Given the narrow roads, limited parking in each town, and the value of being able to adjust the plan as the day goes, most travelers find a private chauffeur considerably less stressful than self-driving and hunting for parking in three different towns.

**What if I'd rather spend more than one day on the Amalfi Coast instead of a single day trip?**
That's a reasonable alternative, especially if you want a slower pace in each town. This itinerary is built specifically for a single day departing and returning to Sorrento; a multi-day stay allows more time in each place without the pressure of a return drive each evening.
