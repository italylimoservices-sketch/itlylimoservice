Alghero Airport serves northwestern Sardinia, and it's the natural arrival point for anyone heading into Alghero itself or exploring the coastline nearby. It's a quieter airport than Cagliari or Olbia, which generally makes for a more relaxed arrival experience — but a few of the same basics still apply, and it's worth planning for them before you land.

## A Smaller, Simpler Airport

Alghero Airport is noticeably smaller in scale than Sardinia's other two main airports, and for most travelers that translates into a more straightforward arrival. Fewer simultaneous flights generally means less congestion in the arrivals area and a quicker path from the gate to meeting your driver. That said, "generally" is the operative word — traffic through any airport can still vary by season and time of day, and a quiet midweek arrival in spring will look different from a busy Saturday in peak summer.

Drive times depend on exactly where in Alghero you're headed, the time of day, and seasonal traffic, so even a confirmed figure would really be a range rather than a single number anyway.

## From Landing to Meeting Your Driver

The sequence of events after your flight lands follows a familiar pattern regardless of which Sardinian airport you're using: deplaning, clearing passport control if you've arrived from outside the Schengen area, collecting any checked luggage, and walking out to the arrivals area. Given Alghero's smaller scale, this process is often quicker here than at a larger international airport, but it can still take longer than expected if multiple flights happen to land close together.

It's sensible to build a reasonable buffer into your arrival-day plans regardless, particularly if you have a hotel check-in time or dinner reservation lined up for your first evening in Alghero. A private driver tracking your flight number will be ready and waiting by the time you clear arrivals, which removes one variable from an otherwise unpredictable process.

## Sharing Your Flight Details

Giving your driver your actual flight number, rather than just a scheduled pickup time, allows real-time tracking so the pickup adjusts automatically whether your flight runs early or late. This matters even at a quieter airport like Alghero, since flight schedules shift for reasons that have nothing to do with the airport itself — weather elsewhere, air traffic delays, or a late departure from your origin city.

If you're arriving on a connecting flight, it's worth flagging that at booking too. A delay on an earlier leg can shift your real arrival time in Alghero even if your ticket shows a fixed time, and sharing that detail in advance means your transfer adjusts accordingly.

## Meeting Your Driver and Handling Luggage

Your driver will typically be waiting in the main arrivals area holding a sign with your name. Given Alghero's smaller terminal, this tends to be a simpler process than navigating a sprawling international arrivals hall — there's less ground to cover and fewer places a driver could be standing that you might miss.

Once you've connected, your driver handles loading your luggage into the car, taking care of a part of travel that's more tiring than it gets credit for, especially after a long journey or with more bags than hands. If you're bringing an unusual volume of luggage — extra bags for a longer stay, or bulkier items — mentioning it at booking means the right vehicle is already arranged rather than sorted out at the curb.

## Choosing the Right Vehicle

Matching your vehicle to your group size and luggage matters most on the arrival leg, since that's when you're typically carrying the most bags of the whole trip. An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits couples or solo travelers well, comfortably fitting 3 passengers and 2 suitcases. Families or anyone with more luggage should consider a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), which handles up to 5 passengers and 4 suitcases. Larger groups can book an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), each seating up to 7 passengers with 6 suitcases.

## If Your Flight Doesn't Land on Schedule

Both delays and early arrivals are normal, and a transfer arranged around your flight number should handle either without much fuss. A delay simply shifts the pickup time, while an early landing means a driver working from flight data should already be in position rather than catching up after the fact.

What a transfer can't do is compress a drive to make up for a tight same-day connection. If you have an onward ferry, train, or flight planned shortly after landing in Alghero, build that buffer around the airport process itself rather than hoping the trip into town can somehow be rushed.

## Arriving in Alghero's Historic Center

Alghero's old town has narrow streets and areas with restricted vehicle access, particularly around the historic core near the city walls and the harbor. A driver familiar with the area will generally know where a car can actually stop near your accommodation and where the final stretch involves a short walk with bags. Sharing your hotel address in advance lets your driver plan the most practical drop-off point rather than figuring it out on arrival.

## Seasonal Differences Worth Knowing About

Alghero draws a noticeable seasonal crowd, with far more flights landing in the summer months than during the quieter parts of the year. If you're arriving in peak season, it's worth assuming the airport will feel busier than its usual relaxed pace and allowing a little extra time for the arrival process. Outside the summer months, flights into Alghero tend to be fewer and more spread out, which generally makes for one of the calmer airport arrivals available anywhere on the island.

This seasonal shift doesn't change anything about how a private transfer works — your driver still tracks your flight number and adjusts the pickup accordingly — but it's useful context when deciding how much buffer to build into your first day, particularly if you're landing in July or August.
