A group of two can wander into Portofino without much planning. A group of ten — grandparents, teenagers, a couple of toddlers, assorted luggage — needs an actual plan, because the same narrow lanes and walk-in access that make Portofino charming for a couple can turn into a logistical puzzle for a larger party.

## The Challenge of Visiting Portofino as a Larger Group

Portofino's village center is compact and largely pedestrian. Vehicles reach the edge of the village, not the harbor itself, so everyone finishes the trip on foot over streets that are narrow, sometimes stepped, and not built to move a crowd quickly. For two or three people, that's a short, easy walk. For a larger group, a few things change:

- The group naturally spreads out at different paces — a grandparent walking carefully over uneven paving is not moving at the same speed as a teenager who's already three lanes ahead
- A bigger group means more total luggage, strollers, and bags to manage through the same narrow access points
- Coordinating where everyone meets back up, and when, matters more as the group gets larger, since Portofino's lanes aren't designed for a crowd to easily spot each other

None of this makes Portofino unsuitable for groups or multi-generational trips. It just means the day benefits from more deliberate planning than a couple traveling alone would need.

## Vehicle Sizing for Groups

The first practical decision is whether one vehicle covers the group comfortably or whether splitting across two makes more sense. A [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) or [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) seats up to seven passengers with room for up to six suitcases, which comfortably covers many extended families and small groups traveling together with a normal amount of luggage.

Beyond that passenger count, or with substantially more luggage than seats would suggest, two vehicles traveling together usually works better than trying to squeeze everyone and everything into one. It's worth saying so directly when requesting a quote — give an honest total headcount and a realistic sense of luggage, rather than rounding down, so the suggested vehicle (or vehicles) actually fit the group you're bringing.

## Coordinating Multiple Vehicles

When a group splits across two vehicles, a few details make the day run more smoothly:

- Request the same pickup time and location for both vehicles, so the group arrives in Portofino together rather than staggered
- Agree on a single return time in advance rather than leaving it loose, since reassembling a scattered group at the end of a visit is harder than it sounds
- Mention in your booking request that the vehicles are traveling together, so the operator can plan the pairing sensibly rather than treating them as two unrelated bookings

Traveling together through the village itself isn't something a vehicle can help with once everyone's on foot, but arriving and leaving together, at the same point and the same time, removes a layer of coordination that otherwise falls on whoever in the group is doing the organizing.

## Keeping a Group Together Once You're Walking

The vehicle access point is a natural anchor for a larger group, and it's worth treating it as one. A few habits help:

- Agree on a specific return time and location before everyone scatters toward the harbor, rather than assuming people will naturally regroup
- For members of the group who may not want to walk the full distance in and out — an older relative, for instance — consider whether they'd rather stay closer to the vehicle access point with a coffee while others explore further
- Don't assume mobile signal will make it easy to coordinate on the fly in the village's narrow lanes; agreeing on a plan in advance is more reliable than figuring it out once you've split up

## Pacing a Multi-Generational Group

Groups that span generations usually have a wider range of walking pace and stamina than a same-age group of friends, and Portofino's lanes and any walk up toward the hillside church and castle make that gap more noticeable than it might be elsewhere. Rather than expecting everyone to move together the whole time, it often works better to plan the visit with a natural split in mind: some of the group heading up toward the castle or further along the harbor, while others are happy settling in at a café near the vehicle access point. Agreeing on this in advance, rather than assuming everyone wants the same pace, tends to make the visit more enjoyable for both the more energetic members of the group and those who'd rather take it slowly.

Meals work the same way. Portofino's harbor-front restaurants aren't generally set up for very large parties without advance notice, so a bigger group may want to decide ahead of time whether to eat together at a reserved table, split into smaller groups, or push the meal to the afternoon stop if the day includes a nearby town as well. None of this needs to be rigid, but deciding roughly what the plan is before arriving beats improvising it in the middle of a crowded harbor front.

## Booking for a Group

The more accurate the details in your booking request, the better the plan that comes back. When requesting a quote for a larger party, it helps to include:

- The total number of passengers, including children and any infants who'll need a car seat
- An honest luggage count, especially if the trip includes an overnight stay rather than just a day visit
- Whether the group needs one larger vehicle or would be better served by two traveling together
- Any specific timing needs — an early arrival to beat the busier midday hours, for example, or a fixed return time tied to another part of the trip

## The Bigger Picture

Portofino rewards a group that's planned its visit a little more carefully than a couple might need to. The village itself doesn't change for a larger party — the same narrow lanes, the same walk-in from the vehicle access point — but getting the vehicle sizing, the coordination between multiple cars if needed, and the regrouping plan right in advance is what keeps a bigger trip feeling like a shared outing rather than a logistics exercise.
