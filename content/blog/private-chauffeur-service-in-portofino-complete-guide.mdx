Portofino is one of those places that photographs better than it drives. The harbor, the pastel buildings stacked above the water, the yachts lined up against the quay — all of it sits inside a village built for walking, not for traffic. A **private chauffeur service in Portofino** doesn't try to fight that reality. Instead, it handles everything around it: the arrival from Genoa or Milan, the drive along the Ligurian coast, and the stretch of road right up to the point where the village takes over and the car has to stop.

This guide walks through how a chauffeur service typically fits into a Portofino visit, from the first airport pickup to pairing the trip with nearby destinations like Cinque Terre or a Genoa cruise call.

## Getting to Portofino from Genoa or Milan

Most visitors reach Portofino by road, arriving either through Genoa's airport or overland from Milan. Genoa is the closer of the two and the more common gateway for a Ligurian coast trip, while Milan tends to be the choice for travelers combining Portofino with a broader northern Italy itinerary — Lake Como, Turin, or a longer loop through the region.

We're not going to attach a specific drive time to either route here. There's no verified distance or duration figure for Genoa-to-Portofino or Milan-to-Portofino on this site, and actual travel time depends on traffic, season, and the exact route taken, so any number you see elsewhere should be treated as a rough estimate rather than a promise. What a private chauffeur does change is the experience of that drive: a driver who knows the coast road handles the navigation, the parking considerations once you're close to the village, and the general pacing of the trip, so you're free to watch the coastline rather than a map app.

A pickup arranged in advance also removes the usual friction of arriving in a new country — no taxi queue, no rental car counter, no figuring out where to park once you're near a village with almost no street parking of its own. The driver meets you, handles luggage, and the trip starts from the moment you land.

## Why Portofino's Streets Change the Plan

Portofino's center is built around a tiny harbor, and the streets leading into it are narrow, often stepped, and largely closed to vehicle traffic. This isn't a quirk of the village — it's the whole point of it. The scale that makes Portofino charming to walk through is the same scale that makes it impractical, and often impossible, to drive a car all the way to the harbor itself.

In practice, this means any vehicle — chauffeured or otherwise — gets you to the edge of the village, not into its center. From there, the final stretch is on foot. A private chauffeur who already understands this layout can plan the drop-off and pickup point sensibly, rather than you discovering the restriction after arriving with bags in hand. It's a small detail, but it's the single biggest difference between a Portofino trip that goes smoothly and one that starts with confusion about where the car is actually allowed to go.

## Pairing Portofino with Cinque Terre

Portofino and Cinque Terre both sit on the Ligurian coast, and a fair number of visitors try to see both on the same trip. They're genuinely different experiences — Portofino is a single compact harbor village, while Cinque Terre is a string of five towns connected by trails, trains, and in some cases boats — so it helps to think of them as two separate days rather than one combined stop.

A private chauffeur can be useful for the transfer between the two areas, and for getting to whichever Cinque Terre town you plan to start from, since parking and vehicle access near those villages carry their own restrictions as well. We cover the practical side of a Cinque Terre visit in more depth on our [Cinque Terre destination page](/destinations/cinque-terre), and it's worth reading before building a combined itinerary, since the logistics of each place are different enough to plan separately.

## Pairing Portofino with a Genoa Cruise Call

Genoa is a working port as well as a historic city, and cruise passengers calling there sometimes use the day to see Portofino rather than staying in Genoa itself. This works reasonably well with a private chauffeur, since the timing of a cruise call is fixed and non-negotiable — missing the ship isn't an option — and a driver who understands that constraint can plan the day around getting back to the port with a sensible margin.

If your Portofino day is built around a cruise stop, it's worth being upfront about the ship's departure time when booking, since that detail shapes how much time can realistically be spent in and around Portofino versus on the road. ## Business Use Cases Around Portofino

Portofino isn't just a leisure destination. Its hotels and harbor have long attracted business travelers combining meetings in Genoa or Milan with a day or two of downtime on the coast, and some companies use the area for small executive retreats or client entertainment precisely because of its setting. For this kind of trip, a [corporate chauffeur](/corporate-chauffeur) arrangement works well — a fixed schedule, a professional vehicle, and a driver who treats pickup times as firm rather than approximate matters more here than the flexibility a leisure itinerary might want.

## Leisure Travel and Longer Italy Itineraries

For leisure travelers, Portofino often works best as one stop within a longer northern Italy or Ligurian coast trip rather than a destination on its own. A few days in Milan, a stretch along the coast through Portofino and Cinque Terre, perhaps a detour to Lake Como — a private chauffeur suits this kind of multi-stop itinerary because the same driver and vehicle can carry you between very different kinds of places without you needing to manage separate bookings for each leg. ## Choosing a Vehicle for the Trip

Vehicle choice for a Portofino visit depends mostly on group size and how much luggage the wider itinerary involves. A couple or solo traveler moving between an airport, a hotel near Portofino, and the village itself is usually well matched to an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan). A small group of four or five, especially one carrying extra luggage for a longer coastal tour, tends to fit better in a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv). Larger families or small groups generally need an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), both built to keep everyone and their bags together in one vehicle.
