Amalfi town gives its name to the entire coast, but it's worth remembering it's also just one town among many along that stretch of coastline, sitting further along the coast road from Sorrento than Positano does. For visitors planning a private transfer from Sorrento specifically to Amalfi town, there are a few practical differences worth knowing — in the drive itself, in how the town handles arriving vehicles, and in what a visit there tends to look like compared to its more photographed neighbor down the coast.

## The Route from Sorrento to Amalfi

The road from Sorrento to Amalfi runs along the same general coastal route that continues on to Positano and the other towns, but reaching Amalfi means covering more of it. There's no fixed, verified duration for this drive that applies consistently — it depends on traffic, the season, and conditions on the narrow sections of road that pass through the smaller villages along the way. A private chauffeur familiar with the route can make sensible calls about timing and pacing that a fixed schedule can't, but it's worth going in with the expectation that this is a longer, more involved drive than the shorter hop to Positano, not a quick errand.

The road itself is narrow for most of its length, cut into the cliffside with a series of tight curves and limited passing room. This isn't a route most visitors choose to self-drive, particularly if they're not used to mountain or coastal roads with blind corners and oncoming traffic sharing a lane that sometimes barely fits two cars. Being driven means you can actually look at the scenery — and this stretch of coast has plenty worth looking at — rather than focusing entirely on the road.

## Comparing the Drive to Positano

If you're deciding between Amalfi and Positano for a day trip, or trying to work out whether to fit both into one outing, it helps to know how the two differ. Positano sits closer to Sorrento along the same road, which makes it a shorter, more contained trip. Amalfi is farther along, so a transfer there typically means a longer drive in each direction, and a full day is often the more comfortable way to plan it rather than trying to squeeze the trip into a half-day window.

Some travelers do combine the two, treating the route from Sorrento through Positano and on to Amalfi as one continuous day, with stops in both towns. This works, but it makes for a fuller day on the road, and it's worth discussing the plan with your driver in advance so the stops, timing, and return trip are realistic rather than rushed. If your priority is spending unhurried time in a single town rather than covering ground, choosing one destination over the other tends to make for a more relaxed day.

## Parking and Vehicle Access in Amalfi

Amalfi town is more open than Positano — it sits along a harborfront rather than climbing a hillside from the water up, which makes it somewhat easier for vehicles to approach. That said, parking directly in the town center is still limited, especially during busier months, and a private transfer will typically drop you at a point near the main piazza or harbor area rather than attempting to navigate deep into the town's narrow interior streets.

From that drop-off point, Amalfi's center is compact and walkable, with the cathedral, main square, and harborfront all within a short distance of each other. Unlike Positano, where much of the town requires navigating stairs and steep lanes, Amalfi's core is comparatively flat, which some travelers — particularly those with mobility considerations or a lot to carry — find easier to manage.

## Luggage and Practical Considerations

If your visit to Amalfi is a day trip from a Sorrento base, luggage generally isn't a major factor — most visitors travel light for a day out. But if Amalfi is a stop on a longer coastal itinerary, or you're arriving with luggage to continue on to another town or to stay overnight, it's worth mentioning that upfront when booking. A driver can plan the drop-off point accordingly, and knowing your onward plans helps with realistic timing for the rest of the day.

A few other points worth considering when arranging this transfer:

- **Time of year.** Amalfi, like the rest of the coast, gets considerably busier in summer, and both the drive and the town itself feel different outside peak season.
- **Return timing.** If you're doing Amalfi as a day trip, agreeing on a rough return time in advance — with some flexibility built in — helps avoid rushing your visit or waiting around at the end of the day.
- **Combining with Ravello.** Amalfi sits close to Ravello, which is reachable by a short additional drive up into the hills, and some visitors add it to the same day rather than treating it as a separate trip.
- **Vehicle choice.** A [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits a couple or solo traveler well, while a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) offers more comfort and space for families or small groups covering a longer day on winding roads.

## What Sets Amalfi Apart from the Other Coast Towns

It's easy to lump all the Amalfi Coast towns together in planning, but Amalfi itself has a slightly different feel from Positano or Ravello. Where Positano is built almost entirely on a vertical hillside and Ravello sits high above the water with sweeping views out over the coast, Amalfi occupies flatter ground around a natural harbor, which historically made it a working port town rather than primarily a resort. That history shows up in the layout — a real town center with a working cathedral, shops that serve residents as well as visitors, and streets that feel a little less designed purely for tourism than some of its neighbors.

This doesn't mean Amalfi is less scenic — the approach along the coast road, with the town appearing around a bend backed by steep hills, is one of the more memorable moments of the drive. It just means the experience of walking around once you arrive is a bit different: less climbing, more browsing, and a center that's easier to cover on foot without the stair-heavy routes Positano requires.

## Making the Most of an Amalfi Visit

Because the drive to Amalfi takes longer than the hop to Positano, it's worth planning enough time in the town itself to make the trip feel worthwhile rather than rushed. Amalfi's cathedral and the surrounding streets reward a slower pace, and the harborfront is a natural place to stop for lunch and take in the setting before heading back. Some visitors also use Amalfi as a base for a short boat trip along the coast, which can be arranged locally if you have the extra time in your day.
