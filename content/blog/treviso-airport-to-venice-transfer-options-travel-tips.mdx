Landing at Treviso rather than Marco Polo isn't a mistake — plenty of low-cost carriers fly there specifically because it's cheaper to operate out of — but it does mean weighing a slightly wider set of options than a Marco Polo arrival requires, since Treviso sits farther from the city and has less transport infrastructure built up around it.

This guide compares the realistic **Treviso Airport to Venice transfer** options side by side: a private transfer, the public bus, and a combined bus-and-train route via Mestre, so you can pick based on your budget, luggage, and tolerance for connections.

## Why the Comparison Looks Different Than at Marco Polo

At Marco Polo, most comparisons come down to road versus water, since the airport sits close to the city and a water bus can run directly there. Treviso doesn't have that option — there's no direct water connection from Treviso, and the airport itself is noticeably farther from Venice, though travel time depends heavily on traffic and road conditions, so it's worth requesting a quote for your specific travel dates rather than relying on a fixed figure.

That distance changes the calculation. Instead of "car versus boat," the real comparison at Treviso is about how many separate legs and connections you're willing to manage after a flight, and how much that trades off against cost.

## Option One: Private Transfer

A private chauffeur picks you up at Treviso arrivals and drives you directly to your destination on the mainland side of Venice — either Piazzale Roma, if you're headed into the historic center, or directly to a Mestre hotel if that's where you're staying.

**Convenience:** This is the strongest point in its favor. One vehicle, one driver, no connections to manage, and pickup timed around your actual flight rather than a fixed schedule. After a longer journey than a Marco Polo arrival typically involves, not having to navigate an unfamiliar bus system or train platform makes a real difference.

**Cost:** Higher than public transport, as expected, but it scales well for groups and families, since the price covers the vehicle rather than each individual passenger.

**Best for:** Families, business travelers, groups with significant luggage, or anyone arriving on an early or late flight when public transport options may be limited or unavailable. You can arrange this through [airport transfers](/airport-transfers), specifying Treviso as your pickup point.

## Option Two: Public Bus to Piazzale Roma or Mestre

Direct bus services connect Treviso Airport to Venice, typically running to either Piazzale Roma or the Mestre area, offering the most budget-friendly way to cover the distance.

**Convenience:** Buses run on a set schedule rather than around your flight, so there's some waiting involved, and buses can be crowded during peak travel periods, particularly in summer. You'll also be managing your own luggage through boarding, the journey, and disembarking.

**Cost:** The cheapest option by a clear margin, which is a large part of its appeal for budget-conscious and solo travelers.

**Best for:** Travelers with light luggage, flexible schedules, and a tolerance for waiting on a bus timetable rather than a driver waiting on their flight.

## Option Three: Bus Plus Train via Mestre

A less direct but sometimes useful option is a shorter bus connection from Treviso into Mestre, followed by a train from Venezia Mestre station onward — either into Santa Lucia station in the historic center, or elsewhere in the region if Venice is one stop on a longer Italian itinerary.

**Convenience:** This is the option with the most moving parts. It works well if you're comfortable navigating two separate systems and have some buffer built into your schedule in case a connection runs late, but it's a poor fit for travelers who'd rather not think about timing two transfers back to back after a flight.

**Cost:** Generally comparable to or slightly more than the direct bus, depending on the train ticket, but still well under a private transfer.

**Best for:** Travelers already comfortable with regional trains, or those continuing beyond Venice itself to another city on the rail network, who can fold this leg into a longer train journey rather than treating it as a separate transfer.

## What to Expect at Treviso Itself

Treviso is considerably smaller and simpler than Marco Polo, with shorter walks and less congestion after landing. The trade-off is fewer ground transport options overall, so whichever choice you make from the table above, it's worth confirming details — schedules, pickup points, ticket purchase locations — before you fly rather than assuming the same volume of options you'd find at a major international airport.

## Don't Forget the Water Leg Into the Historic Center

Whichever option you choose, if your final destination is a hotel inside Venice's historic center rather than Mestre, remember that the car-free zone still applies. A private transfer or bus arriving at Piazzale Roma still requires a water taxi or vaporetto for the final stretch, and a train arriving at Santa Lucia station puts you inside the car-free zone directly, with the same water-based options for the rest of your route. None of the three options above eliminates this last leg — they only differ in how you reach the edge of it.

## Choosing the Right Vehicle If You Go Private

For travelers going the private transfer route, an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) suits couples or solo travelers comfortably, while a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) or [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) offers considerably more room for families or groups making the longer drive from Treviso.

## Timing Considerations Regardless of Which Option You Pick

Treviso's ground transport options thin out considerably outside normal daytime hours, which matters more here than at a larger airport like Marco Polo. If you're arriving on an early morning or late-night flight, a public bus or train connection may not be running at all, which effectively narrows your choice down to a private transfer regardless of your original preference. It's worth checking current schedules against your actual arrival time well before you fly, rather than assuming a bus or train will simply be waiting whenever you land.

This is also a case where booking a private transfer in advance carries more weight than it might at a busier airport. With fewer fallback options if a plan falls through, having a driver already tracking your flight number removes one of the more stressful variables from an otherwise straightforward arrival.

## Making the Call for Your Trip

If your priority is arriving with zero waiting and no connections to manage after a long flight, a private transfer is the clear choice, particularly for families or anyone arriving outside normal daytime hours when public options thin out. If budget matters most and you're traveling light, the direct bus gets the job done without much fuss. And if you're already planning to use trains elsewhere in Italy, the bus-plus-train route via Mestre can fold neatly into a longer rail itinerary rather than standing alone as an extra step.

Whichever path fits your trip, arranging the details in advance — through [Italy Limo Service](/chauffeur-service) if you're going the private route, or by checking current bus and train schedules if not — turns Treviso's extra distance from a source of stress into simply one more planned leg of your journey to Venice.
