Landing at Milan Malpensa and heading straight for Portofino is a common plan for travelers who want to start their Italy trip on the coast rather than in the city. It's also a longer first-day drive than most arrival transfers, so it's worth thinking through the arrival sequence itself before you even get to the question of the road ahead.

## Why Malpensa, and What That Means for This Trip

[Milan Malpensa](/airport-transfers/milan-malpensa) is Milan's larger international airport and the more common entry point for long-haul flights into the Milan area, which makes it a frequent starting point for travelers heading onward to the Italian Riviera. It sits outside the city center, in the opposite direction from Milan itself relative to where Portofino lies, so this transfer isn't simply "airport, then city, then coast" — it's a direct run from the airport toward Liguria that bypasses central Milan entirely if you don't need to stop there.

We won't put a specific number of hours on this drive. There's no verified, published timing for the Malpensa-to-Portofino route on this site, and the honest answer is that it's a genuinely long transfer — crossing a good stretch of Lombardy before reaching the Ligurian coast — with actual time depending on traffic, time of day, and conditions on the coastal roads near Portofino itself. Plan for a substantial first-day drive and you won't be caught off guard.

## Malpensa's Layout

Malpensa is a larger airport with more than one terminal, and which one you land in depends on your airline. It's worth confirming your terminal when you book your transfer, since that's where your driver will plan to meet you, and getting this detail right avoids any confusion in a terminal you've likely never navigated before. Sharing your flight number at booking also means your pickup is tracked against your actual landing time, so a delayed or early flight doesn't throw off the plan.

## What Happens Between Landing and Meeting Your Driver

After touchdown, there's a sequence most international arrivals go through before you actually reach the curb: deplaning, passport control if you're arriving from outside the Schengen area, baggage claim, and then the walk out to arrivals. Depending on how busy the airport is when you land, that process alone can take anywhere from 20 minutes to well over an hour. It's sensible to think of this as a separate chunk of time from the drive itself, rather than assuming the whole journey starts the moment your wheels touch the runway.

## Why This Matters More Than on a Short Transfer

On a quick airport-to-city hop, arrival processing delays are a minor inconvenience — you might wait an extra 20 minutes before a 15-minute drive. On a longer transfer like this one, where the drive itself is already a significant chunk of your day, stacking arrival delays on top of a long drive means your total door-to-door time can run considerably longer than you might initially assume. If you have a dinner reservation or a specific arrival time in mind for Portofino, it's worth building a generous buffer rather than planning tightly around when your flight is scheduled to land.

## Luggage for a Long First Day

Arrival day after an international flight is typically when you're carrying the most luggage of your entire trip — suitcases packed for the whole stay, plus whatever you've picked up for the journey itself. Combined with a long drive ahead, it's worth giving a bit more thought to vehicle space than you might for a short in-city transfer. If you're bringing extra bags for an extended stay, ski or golf equipment, or simply more cases than the group has hands for, mention it when you book so the right vehicle is already arranged.

## Choosing Your Vehicle

For couples or solo travelers, an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) comfortably seats 3 passengers with 2 suitcases, which is a reasonable fit for a long first-day drive. Families or small groups with more luggage often do better in a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), fitting up to 5 passengers and 4 suitcases with more room to settle in. For larger groups, an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) seats up to 7 passengers with 6 suitcases, useful if you're arriving as an extended family or group of friends starting an Italy trip together.

## Thinking About Jet Lag and Pacing

If you've just arrived on a long-haul flight into Malpensa, it's worth being realistic about how a long first-day drive sits alongside jet lag. Some travelers prefer to push through and arrive in Portofino the same day, ready to settle in and rest properly once they get there. Others find it more comfortable to treat the transfer as the main event of day one, without planning anything else — no dinner reservation, no sightseeing — so there's no pressure if the arrival sequence and drive together take longer than hoped. Either approach works; the key is deciding which one suits you before you land rather than figuring it out at the curb.

## The Drive Itself

Once you're on the road, the route runs from the airport across Lombardy and into Liguria, with the character of the drive changing noticeably as you approach the coast — faster, more direct roads further inland giving way to narrower, more winding coastal roads as you near Portofino. It's worth treating that final coastal stretch as its own phase of the journey rather than an extension of the fast inland driving that precedes it, since pacing naturally slows there.

## Arriving at Portofino

As with any arrival into the village, it's worth knowing that Portofino's harbor area restricts vehicle access. Your driver will bring you to the edge of the village, with the final approach to the harbor front and most hotels in the old center covered on foot. This applies to every form of road transport arriving in Portofino, not just private transfers, so it's simply part of the destination rather than anything specific to how you travel.
