## Getting to Lake Como in the First Place

There's no airport on Lake Como itself, so almost every visitor arrives through somewhere else first. Milan and Malpensa Airport are the most common starting points, and the route between them and the lake is a well-established one — [Milan to Lake Como](/routes/milan-to-lake-como) covers approximately 50 km and takes around an hour, whether you're going straight to a hotel or building the drive into a full day of touring with stops along the way. Traffic around Milan and the roads leading north can add to that, particularly in the late afternoon, so it's worth treating the hour as a reasonable baseline rather than a guarantee.

Bergamo Airport is the other option some travelers fly into, especially on budget routes from other parts of Europe. It sits roughly an hour or more from the lake area, though there isn't a single verified figure that holds up regardless of which town you're headed to, so it's worth requesting a quote for your specific route rather than assuming a fixed number. Either way, a [private airport transfer](/airport-transfers) that tracks your flight and has a driver waiting in arrivals removes the first, and often most stressful, logistical step of the trip — figuring out ground transportation after a long journey, with luggage in hand and no fixed plan yet in place.

## Touring the Lake's Towns

Como town, Bellagio, and Varenna are the three names that come up most in conversations about visiting the lake, and each has a distinct character. Como town sits at the southern end and is the easiest to reach directly, with a walkable center and a lakefront promenade. Bellagio, further up the lake, is often described as one of its most photographed towns, with steep lanes stepping down toward the water. Varenna, across the water from Bellagio, tends to feel quieter and less crowded, which is part of why visitors who include it are often glad they did.

Moving between these towns is where a private chauffeur changes the shape of the day the most. The lake's ferries run on their own schedule, and driving yourself into towns like Bellagio means dealing with narrow streets and limited parking, neither of which is particularly enjoyable on unfamiliar roads. Being driven between towns means the day is built around what you actually want to see rather than around a boat timetable or a hunt for a parking space in a village that wasn't really designed for cars.

## Extending a Trip to Switzerland

One detail that surprises some first-time visitors is how close the Swiss border actually is. Lugano, in the Italian-speaking part of Switzerland, sits approximately 35 km from Lake Como — around 45 minutes to an hour by road, crossing at Chiasso, though as with any drive, the exact timing depends on traffic and conditions at the border. Some travelers extend a Lake Como stay with a day or a few hours in Lugano, and a [private transfer across the border](/international-border-crossing-transfers) avoids the need to work out train connections or rental logistics for what's often just a half-day addition to the trip.

## Choosing the Right Vehicle

Vehicle choice for a Lake Como trip depends mostly on group size, luggage, and how much of the day involves narrow lakeside streets. A solo traveler or a couple is typically well suited to an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan), both built for up to three passengers and two suitcases, with the luxury sedan offering a more upscale interior for the same capacity. Groups of four or five, or trips involving more luggage than usual, tend to work better in a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), which handles up to five passengers and four suitcases. Larger groups — a family gathering, a wedding party, or a business delegation — typically need an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), both suited to seven passengers and six suitcases.

It's worth mentioning any specific requirements, such as a stroller or additional equipment, when booking rather than assuming they'll be sorted out automatically — these are the kinds of details worth confirming in advance rather than raising on the day.
