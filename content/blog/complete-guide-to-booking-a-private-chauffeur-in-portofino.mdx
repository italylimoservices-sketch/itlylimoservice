Booking a private chauffeur for Portofino is a fairly simple process once you know what information actually speeds it up. A request like "a car for Portofino next month" takes longer to turn into a real quote than one that answers a handful of specific questions upfront — where, when, how many people, and what the day is meant to look like.

This guide walks through what a booking request needs, how the process generally moves from quote to confirmation, and some timing advice for visiting during the busier months on the Ligurian coast.

## What a Booking Request Needs

A standard quote request is built around a short set of practical details. Having these ready before you start makes the whole process faster and reduces the number of follow-up questions.

**Pickup location.** This might be Genoa Airport, a hotel in Genoa or Santa Margherita Ligure, or another point along the coast. If your trip starts in Milan, it's worth naming that clearly as well, since the two cities sit at different points relative to Portofino.

**Destination.** For a straightforward visit, this is simply Portofino — though it's worth noting in the request that the vehicle will reach the edge of the village rather than the harbor itself, since the streets near the water are pedestrian. If your day includes other stops, naming them helps shape a realistic quote from the start.

**Date and time.** An accurate date matters everywhere, but it matters more during Portofino's busier months, when availability for specific vehicle types can tighten closer to the date. If the day starts or ends with a flight, factoring in the actual flight schedule rather than a rough guess helps avoid timing problems later.

**Number of passengers.** This is what determines vehicle class, so it's worth counting the full group rather than just the adults.

**Vehicle type.** If you already know you want a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van), or something else, specify it directly. If you're unsure, it's fine to leave this open and let passenger count and luggage guide the recommendation.

**Trip type.** Whether the booking is a one-way transfer, a round trip, or an hourly/full-day arrangement changes the logistics meaningfully. Portofino visits in particular often benefit from an hourly structure rather than a fixed point-to-point transfer, since the time spent in the village itself tends to run longer than expected. If your day is more open-ended, it's worth saying so in the request rather than defaulting to a rigid transfer.

**Special requirements.** This field gets skipped more often than it should. A child seat, a mobility consideration that affects how close to the village the car needs to get, extra luggage, or a specific stop you want included on the way — all of this belongs in the original request, not mentioned to the driver on the day, when there's far less room to actually plan around it.

**Contact details.** A name and a reliable way to reach you, so the quote and any follow-up questions can get to you without delay.

## From Quote to Confirmation

Once a request is submitted, the process generally follows a simple sequence:

1. **The request is reviewed** against the details provided — pickup, destination, timing, passenger count, and any special requirements noted.
2. **A vehicle and quote are proposed**, based on group size, luggage, and the structure of the trip. A single airport-to-Portofino transfer is a simpler proposal than a full day that also covers Santa Margherita Ligure or a Genoa pickup.
3. **Adjustments get worked out.** If the suggested vehicle or timing doesn't quite fit, or you want to shift from a fixed transfer to an hourly booking after thinking it through, this is the stage where that gets sorted before anything is locked in.
4. **The booking is confirmed**, with the specifics — driver, vehicle, pickup time and location — set for the trip.

Requests that already include passenger count, luggage, and any special needs tend to get a usable quote on the first pass, rather than needing a round of back-and-forth to fill in gaps.

## Booking for a Simple Visit Versus a Longer Day

The process looks a little different depending on whether you're booking a straightforward transfer to Portofino or a longer day that includes other stops. For a simple visit, naming the pickup point, Portofino as the destination, and a rough sense of how long you'd like at the village is usually enough. For a longer day — say, combining Portofino with Santa Margherita Ligure, Genoa, or a push toward Cinque Terre — it helps to describe the rough shape of the day in the request itself, rather than booking a single leg and figuring out the rest later. ## Timing Advice for Peak Season

Portofino's busiest period generally runs through the warmer months, when both demand for vehicles and general road traffic along the coast tend to increase. A few habits make a real difference during this stretch:

- **Book earlier rather than closer to the date.** Peak-season availability tightens as the travel date approaches, especially for specific vehicle types or longer bookings.
- **Build in extra time around the village itself.** Portofino tends to absorb more time than visitors expect, and a tightly scheduled day leaves less room for that.
- **Confirm details again closer to the trip.** Flight schedules and plans shift, and a quick reconfirmation closer to the date helps avoid last-minute surprises.
- **Expect parking and access points near the village to be busier.** This is less about the booking itself and more about setting realistic expectations for how the day will feel once you arrive.

Outside peak season, booking tends to be more flexible, with more room to adjust dates or vehicle choice closer to the trip itself.

## A Few Points That Come Up Mid-Process

A handful of practical questions tend to surface after the initial quote goes out:

- **Uncertain final pickup details.** If your hotel isn't booked yet, naming the general area — Genoa, Santa Margherita Ligure, or wherever you're staying — is enough to get a workable quote started.
- **Adding a stop after booking.** Plans shift, and most adjustments are straightforward if raised with reasonable notice rather than the morning of the trip.
- **Combining a transfer with a tour day.** A request that's really "pick us up in Genoa, spend the day in Portofino and Santa Margherita Ligure, then drop us back" is a normal way to structure the day — it just helps to describe it as one continuous plan rather than separate, disconnected legs.
