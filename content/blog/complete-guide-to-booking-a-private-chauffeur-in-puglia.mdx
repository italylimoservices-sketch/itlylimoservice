Booking a private chauffeur in Puglia comes down to a handful of practical details, and having them ready before you start the request makes the whole process faster. A vague message — "need a car in Puglia for a week" — takes longer to turn into a usable quote than one that answers the questions that actually matter: where, when, how many people, and what kind of trip it is.

## What a Booking Request Actually Needs

A standard quote request is built around a short, specific set of fields, and each one exists for a practical reason.

**Pickup location and destination.** This can be an airport — Bari or Brindisi, Puglia's two main gateways — a hotel, a villa or masseria, or just a town name if the exact address isn't settled yet. For a touring day rather than a point-to-point transfer, naming the town or towns you want to visit works fine in place of a single fixed destination.

**Date and time.** An accurate date matters year-round, but it matters more during busy periods, and a realistic time — factoring in flight schedules if the trip starts or ends at an airport — helps avoid timing problems later in the process.

**Number of passengers.** This is what actually determines which vehicle fits the group, so it's worth counting everyone traveling, including children, rather than just the adults in the party.

**Vehicle type.** If you already know you want a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van), or something else from the fleet, you can specify it directly in the request. If you're not sure, leaving this open is fine — passenger and luggage counts will guide what gets suggested back.

**Trip type.** Whether the booking is one-way or round-trip changes the logistics meaningfully, especially for a multi-day Puglia visit, so this needs to be clear from the start. A round-trip request also asks for a return date and time, which is worth settling even loosely so the schedule can be planned around it properly.

**Special requirements.** This field gets skipped more often than it should be. Car seats for children, extra or oversized luggage, mobility needs, a specific stop you want included along a route, or anything else particular to your trip belongs here — not mentioned to the driver on the day, when there's far less room to actually plan for it.

**Contact details.** A name and a way to reach you, by phone or email, so the quote and any follow-up questions actually get to you.

## From Quote to Confirmation

Once a request is submitted, the process generally moves through a simple sequence:

1. **The request is reviewed** against the details provided — pickup, destination, timing, passenger count, and any special requirements noted.
2. **A vehicle and quote are proposed**, based on group size, luggage, and the nature of the trip. A single airport transfer is a simpler proposal than a multi-day tour spanning several Puglia towns.
3. **Adjustments get worked out.** If the suggested vehicle doesn't quite fit, or the itinerary needs refining — adding a stop in Alberobello, adjusting a pickup time, splitting a large group across two vehicles — this is the stage where that happens, before anything is locked in.
4. **The booking is confirmed**, and the specifics — driver, vehicle, pickup time and location — are set for the trip.

A request with more detail upfront tends to need fewer rounds of back-and-forth. One that already specifies passenger count, luggage, and any special needs usually gets a usable quote on the first pass, rather than requiring a follow-up just to fill in gaps.

## Booking a Tour Versus a Simple Transfer

The process looks slightly different depending on whether you're booking a straightforward transfer — airport to hotel, or one town to another — or a touring day involving multiple stops, sightseeing time, and a more flexible schedule. For a touring day, it helps to describe roughly what you're after: specific towns, a particular site like the trulli in Alberobello or the old town in Ostuni, and whether the pace should leave room for lingering or stay tighter. ## Booking for Families and Larger Groups

Families traveling with young children, and larger or multi-generational groups, benefit from giving the fuller picture upfront — total passengers, whether the group splits across more than one vehicle, ages of any children, and car seat needs. Child seat availability should always be confirmed directly at booking rather than assumed. ## Timing Advice for Peak Summer Season

Puglia's busiest travel months are generally through the summer, when demand for vehicles and regional road traffic both tend to pick up, particularly around the coast and the more popular inland towns. A few habits make a real difference during this period:

- **Book earlier rather than later.** Peak-season availability tightens as the date gets closer, particularly for specific vehicle types, multi-day plans, or larger groups needing more than one vehicle.
- **Build extra time into airport transfers.** Summer traffic around Bari and Brindisi can run heavier than other times of year, so a tight connection window carries more risk than it would in a quieter season.
- **Confirm timing again closer to the date.** Flight schedules and plans shift, and a quick confirmation shortly before travel heads off any last-minute surprises.
- **Expect summer heat to affect pacing.** Midday heat is a real factor in how comfortable walking through a historic center like Lecce or Ostuni feels, which is worth building into when you schedule stops on a full day out.

Outside peak summer, booking tends to be more flexible, with more room to adjust dates or vehicle choice closer to the actual trip.

## Luggage and Vehicle Fit, Specifically

Puglia's old towns — Alberobello, Ostuni, and Lecce especially — often involve a walk from wherever a vehicle can realistically stop to wherever you're actually staying, since narrow historic streets limit vehicle access. That's worth factoring into vehicle choice: a group bringing noticeably more luggage than a quick trip would suggest is better served naming that clearly in the special requirements field, so a vehicle like the [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) (up to five passengers, four suitcases) or the larger [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) (up to seven passengers, six suitcases) gets proposed appropriately rather than something that turns out tight once bags are loaded. ## Common Questions That Come Up Mid-Process

A few practical points tend to surface after the initial quote goes out, and knowing them ahead of time can save a round of back-and-forth:

- **Changing the itinerary after booking.** Plans shift, especially on a longer trip — a town gets added, a timing moves. Most adjustments are straightforward as long as they're raised with reasonable notice rather than the morning of.
- **Multiple pickup points in one day.** If a day involves collecting different people at different times, or stopping at more than one property, describe that sequence clearly rather than listing one pickup and destination and explaining the rest later.
- **Uncertain final addresses.** If a villa or masseria isn't confirmed yet at the time of the request, naming the general area or nearest town is enough to get a workable quote started — the exact address can follow once it's booked.
- **Combining a transfer with a touring day.** A request that's really "pick us up at the airport, then spend the afternoon visiting a couple of towns before reaching the accommodation" is a normal kind of day to plan for — describe it as one continuous day rather than two disconnected legs.
