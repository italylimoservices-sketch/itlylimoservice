Sorrento's historic center is a beautiful place to walk and a genuinely inconvenient one to drag a suitcase through. Cobbled streets, sudden narrow turns, and in places limited vehicle access all mean that the distance between where a car can actually stop and where your hotel room is can be longer than it looks on a map. None of this is a reason to avoid the town — it's simply worth knowing before you arrive, so the luggage part of your trip doesn't become the stressful part.

## Why Sorrento's Streets Complicate Luggage

Much of central Sorrento — the streets around Corso Italia, the lanes near Piazza Tasso, and the older quarter closer to the Marina Grande side — was laid out long before cars existed, and some of it has limited vehicle access as a result. A hotel that's a five-minute walk from the main piazza might still mean your driver can only get within a block or two of the entrance, particularly if there's a pedestrian zone, a market, or simply a lane too narrow for a full-size vehicle. This is common in historic Italian town centers generally, not unique to Sorrento, but it catches travelers off guard when they've pictured a door-to-door drop-off with several bags.

The practical upshot: a private transfer still gets you and your luggage to Sorrento reliably, but it's worth confirming your exact hotel address and being ready for the last short stretch to potentially involve a bit of walking, especially with a suitcase that doesn't roll well on cobblestones.

## Luggage Handling From Vehicle to Hotel

For most hotels, particularly those with their own entrance on a road a vehicle can actually reach, luggage handling from car to lobby is straightforward. For properties tucked into the narrower parts of the historic center, there may be a short walk involved for the final stretch — something worth asking about when you book, particularly if you're traveling with more bags than you can comfortably carry yourself, or with mobility considerations that make an unexpected walk with luggage a real problem rather than a minor inconvenience.

Giving an honest count of your luggage, not just your passenger count, when you submit a quote request is what allows the right vehicle to be suggested in the first place. A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) comfortably handles up to 5 passengers and 4 suitcases, and an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) extends that to up to 7 passengers and 6 suitcases — useful to know if your trip involves more bags than people, which happens more often than travelers expect.

## Day Trips That Add to Your Luggage

Sorrento and the surrounding area are known for a handful of regional products that tempt most visitors into buying more than they arrived with — limoncello, local ceramics, and leather goods among them. None of this is a problem for a private transfer, but it's worth thinking about if your day trip itinerary includes shopping stops, since what starts as a day bag can come back as something considerably bulkier. If you know in advance that a day trip will likely include picking up a few boxes of ceramics or bottles of limoncello, mentioning it as part of your special requirements when booking helps ensure the vehicle has room for it, rather than discovering a squeeze at the end of the day.

This is particularly relevant for trips combining sightseeing with shopping — say, a half-day out to [Positano or Amalfi](/blog/positano-amalfi-and-ravello-private-chauffeur-day-trip-guide) — where the return leg of the journey often has more cargo than the outbound one.

## Packing Advice for the Peninsula Specifically

A few practical notes that come up often for travelers moving around the Sorrentine Peninsula with luggage:

- Soft-sided bags or duffels handle cobblestones and narrow stairwells better than hard-shell suitcases with small wheels
- If your hotel is deep in the historic center, ask ahead whether the final approach involves stairs or a pedestrian-only stretch
- Keep anything fragile — ceramics, bottled limoncello — in a separate bag you can carry yourself rather than packed loosely with clothing
- Consolidate day-trip purchases into one bag at the end of the day rather than several loose items, which makes loading and unloading the vehicle faster and less prone to something getting left behind

## Multi-Stop Transfers With Luggage

If your trip includes a transfer that covers more than one stop — picking someone up along the way, or a brief errand before heading to your final destination — it's worth flagging this at booking rather than mentioning it to the driver once you're already in the vehicle. The same goes for any transfer where you know in advance that luggage will be added partway through, such as a family member joining after a separate flight. Structuring this into your original request, using the special requirements field, gives the operator a chance to plan the right vehicle and timing from the outset.

## Departure Day Considerations

Departure day tends to compress the luggage question into a smaller window, particularly if you're catching an early flight out of Naples. Confirming your total luggage count again ahead of a departure transfer — especially if you've picked up ceramics, wine, or other bulky souvenirs during your stay — helps avoid a last-minute surprise about whether everything actually fits. A booking made with an accurate count from the start rarely runs into this problem; it's the trips where luggage changed significantly after the original booking that tend to need adjusting.

## The Practical Takeaway

Sorrento's charm is tied up in the same narrow, historic streets that make luggage handling slightly more involved than at a modern hotel with underground parking. It isn't a serious obstacle, but it rewards a bit of upfront honesty — an accurate pickup address, a real luggage count, and a heads-up about any shopping you expect to do along the way. With that in hand, a private transfer handles the rest.
