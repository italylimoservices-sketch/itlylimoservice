## Why Santa Margherita Ligure Draws Travelers

Santa Margherita Ligure has a different feel from its more famous neighbor, Portofino. It's a proper town rather than a small harbor village, with a palm-lined seafront, a working beach scene, and enough restaurants, shops, and hotels to function comfortably as a base for several days rather than just a quick stop. That combination — real town infrastructure alongside genuine Riviera charm — is a big part of why it's become such a popular choice for travelers who want to experience this stretch of coast without basing themselves somewhere as compact and in-demand as Portofino itself.

The town's waterfront promenade is lined with pastel buildings, and its harbor has a mix of fishing boats and pleasure craft that gives it an authentic, lived-in feel rather than a purely polished resort atmosphere. There's also a notable villa and public gardens near the center of town, giving visitors a green, shaded option when the midday sun gets strong along the water.

## Santa Margherita as a Base for Exploring

One of the clearest advantages of staying in Santa Margherita Ligure is location. From here, [Portofino](/destinations/portofino) is a short and scenic trip away, making it entirely realistic to base yourself in Santa Margherita and visit Portofino for an afternoon or an evening rather than trying to find accommodation directly in Portofino itself, where options are limited and in high demand. The reverse is also true for travelers curious about [Cinque Terre](/destinations/cinque-terre) or [Genoa](/destinations/genoa) — Santa Margherita's position along the coast makes it a workable midpoint for day trips in either direction, with a private chauffeur handling the legwork between stops.

This is part of why many travelers treat Santa Margherita less as a single-day destination and more as a comfortable home base for a few nights, using it to explore the surrounding coastline at a relaxed pace rather than packing everything into one rushed day.

## What to Do Once You Arrive

Santa Margherita rewards an unhurried visit. A walk along the seafront promenade, time spent at one of the town's beach clubs or public beach areas, and a wander through the compact historic center cover the basics without requiring a packed schedule. The town's harbor is worth lingering at, particularly in the late afternoon when the light softens and the boats are coming in for the evening.

Because Santa Margherita doesn't have the pedestrian-only constraints that a place like Cinque Terre does, it's a more conventional coastal town to navigate — walkable, with a center that's easy to explore on foot, but without the staircases and car-free villages that reshape how you get around in other parts of the Ligurian coast.

## Practical Notes

A few points worth keeping in mind when planning this transfer:

- **Seasonal crowds affect the experience more than the drive.** Santa Margherita gets busy during the summer high season, and while that doesn't necessarily change your transfer time dramatically, it does affect parking and pedestrian congestion once you're in town.
- **The town works year-round, unlike some smaller coastal spots.** Because it has genuine year-round infrastructure, Santa Margherita stays functional and pleasant outside of peak summer months too, which is worth considering if you're trying to avoid the busiest crowds.
- **Luggage is rarely an issue.** Unlike Cinque Terre's pedestrian villages, Santa Margherita's hotels are generally reachable by car, so a private transfer can typically take you to your accommodation directly rather than requiring a walk with your bags.
