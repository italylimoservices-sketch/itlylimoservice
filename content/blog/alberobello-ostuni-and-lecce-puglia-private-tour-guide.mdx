Alberobello, Ostuni, and Lecce together cover a wide stretch of Puglia's character — trulli-dotted countryside, a hilltop white city, and a baroque southern capital. Unlike a quick single-day loop near Bari, this combination spans real distance across the region, and the honest way to plan it is as a multi-day itinerary rather than a rushed one-day dash between three unrelated towns.

## Why These Three Towns, and Why Not in a Single Day

It's worth being direct about this up front: Alberobello, Ostuni, and Lecce are spread further apart than a typical single-day Bari-area loop, such as combining Alberobello with Polignano a Mare. Lecce in particular sits well south, deep into the Salento peninsula, and treating all three towns as a single rushed day trip tends to leave you with little more than a windshield tour and three rushed photo stops. This guide takes a different approach, building the three towns into a longer trip — whether that's two or three days, or stops along a longer Puglia itinerary — so each town gets the time it actually deserves.

## A Suggested Sequence

One practical way to structure this as a multi-day trip:

**Day one: Alberobello.** Start inland with Alberobello's trulli districts, ideally in the morning before the town fills with day visitors. This gives you time to wander Rione Monti and the quieter Rione Aia Piccola properly, rather than rushing through on the way to somewhere else.

**Day one or two: Ostuni.** From Alberobello, Ostuni makes a natural next stop — close enough in character and setting to feel like a continuation of the trip, rather than requiring a long haul. Whether you push on to Ostuni the same afternoon or treat it as the start of day two depends on how much time you want in Alberobello and how your overnight stays are arranged.

**Final day(s): Lecce.** Save Lecce for last, both because it sits furthest south of the three and because its baroque architecture and lively, youthful atmosphere make a satisfying final stop rather than a rushed middle stage. Lecce rewards at least a full day, and arguably more, given the density of its historic center.

This sequence isn't the only workable order — some travelers prefer to start in Lecce if that's where their flight or accommodation puts them, working backward toward Alberobello. The point isn't a fixed script; it's spreading the three towns across enough time that none of them feels like an afterthought.

## Alberobello: The Trulli Towns

Alberobello's trulli — conical, whitewashed limestone structures with distinctive grey corbelled roofs — are unlike anything else in Italy, concentrated densely enough across Rione Monti and Rione Aia Piccola that the town has UNESCO World Heritage status. Beyond wandering the two main districts, look for the Trullo Sovrano, one of the few two-story examples, and Casa d'Amore, notable as the first trullo built with mortar rather than the traditional dry-stone technique. A few unhurried hours here, ideally before the midday crowds build, gives you a genuine feel for the town beyond its postcard image.

## Ostuni: The White City

Ostuni sits on a hilltop a short distance from the Adriatic, and its nickname — the White City — comes from the whitewashed buildings that cascade down the hill in tight, layered tiers, a look that's striking from a distance and atmospheric once you're inside the old town's maze of narrow lanes. The cathedral, with its distinctive rose window, anchors the highest point of town, and wandering the surrounding streets — many too narrow for cars — gives Ostuni a quieter, more residential feel than some of Puglia's busier stops. Ostuni also sits close to the coast, and if your schedule allows, the surrounding countryside of olive groves and the nearby beaches add another dimension to a stay here beyond the hilltop town itself.

## Lecce: Puglia's Baroque Capital

Lecce is often called the "Florence of the South," a nickname that oversells the comparison but gets at something real: this is a city dense with elaborate baroque architecture, carved from a local golden limestone that takes on a warm glow in the right light. The Basilica di Santa Croce is the standout example, with an extraordinarily ornate facade that rewards slow, close looking rather than a quick glance. Piazza del Duomo, the well-preserved Roman amphitheater sitting below street level in the city's main square, and the lively streets around Piazza Sant'Oronzo all add to a city that feels both historic and genuinely lived-in, with a large student population keeping its bars and piazzas busy well into the evening. Lecce is also a sensible base for exploring further into Salento, including destinations like [Otranto](/blog/bari-to-otranto-private-transfer-travelers-guide) to the east.

## Practical Notes for a Multi-Day Trip

- **Plan overnight stays rather than forcing everything into day trips.** Given the distances involved, a multi-day trip with accommodation in or near Ostuni or Lecce tends to work better than commuting back to a single base each night.
- **Lecce deserves more than a half day.** Its historic center is dense enough with baroque architecture that a rushed visit undersells it badly.
- **Ostuni rewards an early or late visit.** The hilltop town's narrow lanes offer welcome shade later in the day during summer, while mornings bring softer light for the white buildings.
- **Comfortable shoes matter in all three towns.** Trulli lanes, hilltop cobblestones, and Lecce's historic paving all involve uneven surfaces and plenty of walking.
- **Confirm your route structure in advance with your chauffeur.** Because this is a longer, multi-stop trip rather than a single transfer, agreeing on the overall plan — including overnight points — ahead of time makes each day's logistics simpler.
