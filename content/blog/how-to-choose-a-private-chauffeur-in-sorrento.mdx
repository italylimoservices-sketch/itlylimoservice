Booking a private chauffeur in Sorrento involves a few more decisions than picking the first option that shows up in a search. Vehicle size, how the booking is structured, and whether the driver actually knows the Amalfi Coast roads you're likely to use all affect how the day goes. Here's what actually matters when you're comparing options.

## Start With Group Size and Luggage

The most common mistake is booking a vehicle that's technically big enough but tight once luggage is factored in. A sedan rated for three passengers becomes uncomfortable fast if all three are also traveling with a full-size suitcase. Before booking, it helps to count both people and bags honestly:

- **Solo travelers or couples** with standard luggage typically fit well in an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) — up to three passengers and two suitcases.
- **Groups of four or five**, or two people with extra bags, usually need a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) — up to five passengers and four suitcases.
- **Families or small groups of six or seven** generally require an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) — both built for up to seven passengers and six suitcases.

If your trip includes an Amalfi Coast day trip with camera bags, beach gear or shopping picked up along the way, it's worth sizing up rather than assuming a tight fit will work out.

## Point-to-Point vs. Hourly Booking

Sorrento trips generally split into two types of booking, and knowing which one fits your day avoids paying for flexibility you don't need or being boxed into a fixed route when you actually wanted to stop.

**Point-to-point transfers** work well for a defined trip with a clear start and end — an airport pickup, a transfer to Naples or Rome, or a one-way trip to a specific hotel. You know where you're starting, where you're ending, and roughly when.

**Hourly bookings** make more sense for an open-ended day, particularly an Amalfi Coast excursion where you might want to stop in Positano for an hour, continue to Amalfi town, and then decide on the way back whether Ravello is worth the detour. Paying for the driver's time rather than a single route gives you the freedom to change the plan mid-trip without renegotiating anything.

A [chauffeur service](/chauffeur-service) arrangement can combine both — a point-to-point airport transfer on arrival, followed by an hourly day trip later in the stay.

## Local Road Knowledge Actually Matters Here

This is more relevant in Sorrento than in most Italian cities. The roads connecting Sorrento to Positano, Amalfi and Ravello are narrow, cliffside, and built for a different era of traffic than what actually uses them in peak season. A driver who works this coast regularly knows where the tour buses tend to bottleneck, which stretches are worth timing around, and where a decent photo stop actually exists versus where stopping isn't practical or safe.

This isn't just about comfort — it's about not losing half a day to traffic that a locally experienced driver would have routed around or timed differently. When you're comparing chauffeur options, it's reasonable to ask directly whether the driver regularly covers the Sorrento-Amalfi Coast route, rather than assuming any licensed driver in the region knows these roads equally well.

## What a Booking Confirmation Should Include

Before you commit, a clear booking should give you:

- Pickup location and time, with some allowance for flight delays if it's an airport transfer
- Vehicle type, matched to your group size and luggage
- Whether the booking is point-to-point or hourly, and what happens if the day runs long
- A way to reach the company or driver if plans shift

Driver details typically get confirmed closer to the travel date rather than at the moment of booking, which is normal — but the vehicle type and the structure of the trip should be clear upfront, not left vague.

## Booking for Multi-Stop Days

If your Sorrento stay includes more than one kind of trip — say, an airport transfer on arrival, a Capri ferry connection mid-week, and an Amalfi Coast day near the end — it's worth discussing the whole stay at once rather than booking each piece separately. It often means one point of contact for the trip rather than several disconnected reservations, and it gives the company a clearer picture of what the days actually look like so they can plan vehicle and driver availability accordingly.

## When to Book

Because Sorrento isn't on Italy's high-speed rail network, more travelers rely on private transportation here than in cities with easy train access, which means vehicles and experienced drivers can be booked up during peak season. Booking a week or more ahead for standard transfers, and earlier for an Amalfi Coast day trip during the busiest summer weeks, gives you a better chance of getting the vehicle size and timing you actually want.

## Comparing Providers

Not every company offering transportation around Sorrento operates the same way, and it's worth looking past price alone when comparing options. A few questions tend to separate a straightforward booking from one that turns into a source of stress partway through the trip:

- Does the confirmation clearly state the vehicle type, or is it vague about what will actually show up?
- Is there a direct way to reach the company if a flight is delayed or plans change on short notice?
- Does the provider operate across the wider region — Naples, the Amalfi Coast, Rome — or only within Sorrento itself, which matters if your trip involves more than one leg?
- Are hourly and point-to-point pricing structures explained clearly, so you know what you're booking before you commit?

A provider that answers these clearly upfront is usually easier to work with once you're actually on the ground and plans shift, which they often do on a multi-stop Italian trip.

## Airport Pickup Specifics

If your booking starts with a Naples Airport pickup, it's worth confirming a few specifics beyond just the pickup time. Ask whether the driver tracks your flight number directly, so a delay doesn't leave you waiting curbside or the driver waiting unnecessarily. Ask where exactly the meeting point is — arrivals halls at busy airports can have multiple exits, and a clear, agreed meeting spot avoids the awkward process of trying to spot each other in a crowd. These details are small, but they're usually the difference between a pickup that feels seamless and one that starts the trip with unnecessary friction.

## Communicating Changes Mid-Trip

Italian coastal trips rarely go exactly to plan, and a good chauffeur arrangement should be able to absorb some change without falling apart. If an Amalfi Coast day trip runs long because Ravello turned out to be worth more time than expected, or a Capri ferry gets rescheduled, having a direct line to your driver or the company — rather than a call center or a generic booking platform — makes it much easier to adjust in real time. This is one more reason local, Sorrento-based experience tends to matter more than booking through a large, impersonal platform with no regional presence.

## A Quick Checklist

Before confirming a booking, it helps to have answered:

- How many passengers and how much luggage, realistically
- Point-to-point or hourly, based on how fixed or open the day is
- Whether the driver has direct experience on the Sorrento-Amalfi Coast road
- What the pickup and confirmation process looks like

Getting these right upfront is most of what separates a smooth Sorrento chauffeur experience from one that runs into avoidable friction.
