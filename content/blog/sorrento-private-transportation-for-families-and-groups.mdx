Larger groups traveling together in Sorrento run into a specific problem that solo travelers and couples rarely think about: keeping everyone together. A multi-generational family, a group of friends splitting a villa, or an extended family reunion trip all share the same challenge once it's time to move from the hotel to a restaurant, a beach club, or a day trip along the coast — how do you get eight, ten, or fifteen people to the same place at the same time without splintering into three separate taxis and losing half the group at some point along the way.

## Why Group Size Changes the Planning

A couple or a small family can generally improvise. If someone wants to walk instead of ride, or the group needs to split up for an hour, it's a minor inconvenience. With a larger group, especially one that includes grandparents, young children, and everyone in between, the calculus is different. A missed connection point means someone standing alone on a narrow Sorrento street with no clear way to reach the rest of the group. The larger the party, the more that coordination becomes the actual logistics problem of the trip, not the sightseeing itself.

This is where planning private transportation as a group, rather than each smaller family unit booking separately, tends to pay off — both in terms of actually staying together and in avoiding the situation where three cars leave the hotel three minutes apart and three cars arrive at the destination in a different order than anyone expected.

## Matching Vehicles to Group Size

Vehicle sizing for a group isn't just about fitting bodies into seats — luggage, day bags, and the general comfort of a multi-hour trip all matter more with a larger, mixed-age group. For a family or group of up to about five, a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) is often enough, comfortably handling 5 passengers and 4 suitcases. Once a group grows beyond that — six or seven travelers, or five travelers with a genuinely large amount of luggage — an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) becomes the more sensible option, with room for up to 7 passengers and 6 suitcases.

For groups larger than a single van comfortably fits, the honest answer is that you'll need more than one vehicle, and that's where coordination — not just vehicle choice — becomes the actual planning task.

## Coordinating Multiple Vehicles for a Day Trip

A large family or group heading from Sorrento to the Amalfi Coast for the day is a common scenario where more than one vehicle is genuinely needed. The coastal road connecting Sorrento to towns like [Positano and Amalfi](/blog/sorrento-to-positano-private-transfer-travel-guide) is narrow and often slow, particularly in peak season, and there's no dependable fixed drive time to quote for it — it varies with traffic, season, and the exact towns involved, so it's worth planning the day around flexibility rather than a tight schedule.

When a group needs two or more vehicles for a trip like this, a few things make the day go more smoothly:

- Requesting all vehicles for the same booking, with the full group size and any splits noted in your special requirements, rather than submitting separate quote requests that a coordinator has to piece together afterward
- Agreeing on a shared departure time before the day starts, rather than assuming everyone's ready at the same moment
- Picking a specific, named meeting point at the destination — a piazza, a hotel entrance, a restaurant — rather than a vague "we'll find each other"
- Building a little slack into the schedule for a stop, a bathroom break, or someone needing extra time, since a group's pace is set by its slowest member, not its fastest

None of this is complicated, but it's the sort of thing that's much easier to arrange in advance than to sort out on the morning of the trip.

## Keeping a Group Together Once You're Moving

Even with vehicles matched to the group's size, staying together during the day takes a bit of intentional planning. If your group splits across two vehicles, agreeing on a rough itinerary in advance — which stops happen together and which are optional — helps avoid the awkward situation of one carload waiting an hour for the other to catch up at a restaurant. It's also worth deciding ahead of time whether the group plans to return to Sorrento together at a fixed time, or whether some members might want to stay out later while others head back early; flagging that flexibility as part of your booking makes it easier for the return trip to actually accommodate it, rather than assuming everyone leaves at once.

## Multi-Generational Trips Specifically

Trips that include grandparents alongside young grandchildren carry their own version of this challenge. An older traveler may need a bit more time getting in and out of a vehicle, or prefer not to walk long stretches on uneven ground, while a younger child may need a stroller, a car seat, or simply more patience built into the schedule. Flagging both ends of that range when you book — rather than assuming a standard itinerary works for everyone — helps the trip actually match the group taking it. If a child seat is needed for part of the group, confirm it directly at booking, since availability should never be assumed.

## Booking as a Group Versus Booking Separately

It's tempting, especially for a large family trip split across multiple households, to let each family or couple book their own transportation independently. This usually works fine for arrivals, where everyone might be landing at different times anyway. For a shared day trip, though, booking as a single group — even across multiple vehicles — tends to produce a smoother day, since the operator has visibility into the whole group's plan rather than piecing together several unconnected bookings that happen to be going to the same place.

When you [request a quote](/chauffeur-service) for a group trip, giving an accurate total headcount, a realistic luggage estimate, and a clear note about whether the group needs to travel together or can be split sensibly across vehicles gives you the best chance of a day that runs the way you actually pictured it.

## The Bigger Picture

Group and family travel around Sorrento isn't fundamentally different from traveling as a couple — it just multiplies the number of small decisions that need to be made and agreed on in advance. Getting vehicle sizing right, coordinating multiple cars when a group requires them, and setting clear meeting points and timing all matter more with ten people than with two. Handled with a bit of planning, a large group trip out of Sorrento can move as smoothly as a smaller one — it just takes slightly more thought before the day begins.

An [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) comfortably fits up to 7 passengers and 6 suitcases. Larger groups will need more than one vehicle, coordinated together.

**Should a large family book one shared transfer or separate ones per household?**
For a shared day trip, booking as a single group request — even across multiple vehicles — tends to produce better coordination than several unconnected bookings heading to the same place.

**How do multiple vehicles stay together during a day trip to the Amalfi Coast?**
Agreeing on a shared departure time, a specific named meeting point at the destination, and a rough shared itinerary before the day starts is the most reliable approach, since there's no fixed drive time to plan around precisely.

**Can child seats be arranged for part of a larger group?**
Yes, but this should be confirmed directly at the time of booking rather than assumed, particularly for multi-generational trips where only some travelers need one.
