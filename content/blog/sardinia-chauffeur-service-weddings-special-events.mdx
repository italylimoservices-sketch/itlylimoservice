A Sardinia wedding comes with a logistics problem most other Italian destination weddings don't face in quite the same way: three possible airports, often spread far enough apart that which one a guest lands at depends almost entirely on where the venue sits. Cagliari in the south, Olbia in the northeast near the Costa Smeralda, and Alghero in the northwest each serve a different part of the island, and a guest list that converges from different countries, or even just different parts of Italy, often ends up scattered across two or even all three on arrival day. This guide covers how transportation logistics for a Sardinia wedding or special event typically come together.

## Figuring Out Which Airport Serves Your Venue

The first planning question for wedding transportation in Sardinia isn't about vehicles or timing — it's about geography. A venue near the Costa Smeralda or elsewhere in the northeast points most guests toward Olbia. A venue closer to Cagliari or the south points toward that airport instead. A venue in the northwest, near Alghero, puts guests through a smaller but still functional gateway with its own Catalan-influenced old town nearby. Some venues sit in a position where more than one airport is a realistic option depending on where a given guest is flying from, which adds a layer of choice that a mainland wedding usually doesn't require.

We won't attach a specific drive time to any route between these airports and a venue, since there's no verified figure for Sardinia's roads on this site and actual travel time depends on the exact location, the season, and traffic — but establishing early on which airport (or airports) most of the guest list will realistically use is the foundation everything else gets planned around.

## Staggering Arrivals Instead of Scrambling on the Day

Destination weddings rarely have every guest arrive at once, and Sardinia weddings are no exception — guests tend to land across a wide window, from several days before the event to the morning of, depending on their own travel plans and how much of a Sardinia trip they're building around the wedding itself. Rather than treating each arrival as an isolated pickup to arrange reactively, it generally works better to build a rough arrival schedule ahead of time: who's landing when, at which airport, and roughly how many people need to move from that airport to the venue or to their accommodation.

This kind of staggered approach lets a chauffeur service plan vehicle allocation sensibly instead of trying to cover a cluster of unpredictable pickups all happening close together. It also means a couple planning the wedding doesn't have to personally manage each guest's transportation on the day — the schedule, once built, runs largely on its own.

## When Guests Split Across Multiple Airports

For weddings where the guest list realistically splits across two or three of Sardinia's airports — some flying through Cagliari, others through Olbia or Alghero depending on where they're coming from — the transportation plan needs to account for each group separately rather than assuming a single pickup point covers everyone. This is more common than it might sound, especially for weddings with an international guest list where flight availability and cost often dictate which airport a given guest ends up using, regardless of which one is technically closest to the venue.

Coordinating this usually means:

- Confirming which airport each guest or guest group is using as early as possible in the RSVP process
- Grouping arrivals by rough time windows at each airport rather than planning pickups one by one
- Allowing some buffer for flight delays, particularly on international connections, so a late arrival doesn't throw off a tightly scheduled pickup

A wedding party moving between a hotel and the venue over the course of the wedding weekend — rehearsal dinner, ceremony, reception, departure — benefits from the same kind of advance planning, just scaled to a smaller group than the full guest list.

## Matching Vehicles to the Wedding Party and Guests

Vehicle needs for a Sardinia wedding usually span a range, from the couple's own transportation to group transfers for larger blocks of guests. An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits a couple or a small bridal party moving between getting-ready locations and the ceremony. For slightly larger groups — parents, a small group of close family — a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) covers up to five passengers with room for luggage. For moving larger blocks of guests between a hotel and the venue, an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) handles up to seven passengers per vehicle, which tends to be the practical choice when a sizable share of the guest list is staying at the same accommodation and needs to arrive together.

Weddings with a larger guest list sometimes need several vehicles running on a loop between the accommodation and the venue rather than a single large transfer, particularly if the ceremony and reception times don't line up neatly with everyone's readiness. Planning this as a coordinated shuttle schedule, rather than individual pickups, tends to keep things running smoothly on the day itself.

## Beyond Weddings: Other Sardinia Events

The same logistics apply to other kinds of private events held in Sardinia — a milestone celebration, a corporate retreat with a social component, or a multi-day gathering that brings people in from different directions. Any event where guests are arriving through more than one of the island's airports, or where a group needs to move together between a hotel and a venue more than once over a weekend, benefits from the same kind of advance coordination described above. ## Accounting for the Season and the Island's Pace

Most Sardinia weddings happen in the warmer months, which also happens to be when the island's coastal roads and smaller towns see the most visitor traffic. A wedding weekend planned for peak summer benefits from building a little extra time into the transportation schedule, particularly for any transfer that passes through a busier town or along a popular coastal stretch, since the same drive can take noticeably longer in August than it would in a quieter month. This is worth mentioning to whoever is coordinating transportation, since a driver already familiar with the seasonal patterns can plan around them more easily than a schedule built without that context.

## What to Share When Planning Wedding Transportation

Working out Sardinia wedding transportation generally goes more smoothly with a few pieces of information gathered ahead of time:

- Roughly how many guests are attending and which airport(s) they're likely to use
- The venue location and how far it sits from each relevant airport
- The wedding weekend's schedule, including any events beyond the ceremony and reception that require transportation
- Whether accommodation is centralized at one property or spread across several

None of this needs to be finalized months in advance, but having even a rough outline early makes it much easier to build a transportation plan that actually matches the guest list, rather than assembling one reactively as arrivals start coming in.

## Bringing a Scattered Guest List Together

A Sardinia wedding's biggest transportation challenge isn't the drive itself — it's the fact that guests often aren't all arriving through the same door. Three airports, a wedding weekend that might stretch across several days of arrivals, and a venue that only some of those airports serve conveniently all add up to a logistics puzzle that benefits from being planned deliberately rather than handled on the fly.
