Landing at Naples Airport is the easy part. What actually determines how smoothly your first day in Italy goes is everything that happens in the hour or two after you step off the plane — clearing the terminal, finding your driver, loading the car, and getting your bearings on where exactly you're headed along the coast. This guide is about that planning window, not the drive itself.

## Build a Realistic Buffer Around Your Flight Time

Most travelers underestimate how long it takes to actually leave an airport after the wheels touch down. Naples Airport (NAP) is compact by international standards, which helps, but you still need to walk from the gate, pass through passport control if you're arriving from outside the Schengen area, and collect checked luggage before you reach the arrivals hall.

On a good day this might take 30 to 45 minutes. On a busier day, with a full flight and a slow bag carousel, it can stretch closer to an hour. None of this is unique to Naples — it's true at most airports — but it matters more here because whatever you've booked for later in the day (a hotel check-in, a dinner reservation, a boat trip) needs to account for both this arrival buffer and the drive itself.

A private transfer helps with the uncertainty on one end: if you share your flight number when booking, your driver can track the flight and adjust pickup timing for a delay, so you're not paying for a no-show driver or scrambling to arrange transport if your plane is late. That said, no service can shorten how long passport control or baggage claim takes on a given day, so it's worth giving yourself margin rather than planning a tight connection to anything time-sensitive on arrival day.

## Finding Your Driver at Naples Airport

Naples Airport has a single, relatively small terminal, which makes meeting a pre-arranged driver more straightforward than at a sprawling international hub. Typically you'll find your driver waiting just outside the arrivals area holding a sign with your name, and the exact meeting point is usually confirmed in your booking details ahead of time.

A few practical points worth knowing before you land:

- Keep your phone charged, or at least have access to your booking confirmation, in case you need to reach your driver or double-check the meeting spot.
- If your flight is delayed and you haven't been able to notify anyone, a driver who's tracking your flight will typically already know and adjust accordingly.
- Have your phone's roaming or a local SIM sorted if you think you'll need to make a call from the terminal — airport WiFi isn't always reliable enough to depend on.

If you're arranging your own onward transport rather than a pre-booked transfer, the taxi rank and shuttle pickup points are both a short walk from arrivals, though this is where things get less predictable — more on that in a companion piece comparing your travel options.

## Luggage: What to Expect on the Coast Road

This is worth thinking through before you land, not after. The Amalfi Coast towns — Positano, Amalfi, and Ravello — sit along a narrow, winding stretch of road, and several hotels in these towns have limited or no vehicle access right up to the front door. It's common for a driver to drop you as close as the road allows, with a short walk, some stairs, or a local shuttle covering the final stretch to your actual room.

This isn't a flaw in the transfer — it's simply how these towns are built into the hillside. But it does mean:

- Pack with the assumption that you (or a porter) might need to carry your own bags for a short final stretch.
- A soft duffel or a bag with sturdy wheels that can handle uneven stone steps is more practical than an oversized hard-shell case.
- If you're staying somewhere with genuinely difficult access, it's worth checking with your hotel directly about their arrival process, since some send their own staff to meet guests at a fixed drop-off point.

None of this is a reason to worry, but knowing it in advance means you're not caught off guard, tired and jet-lagged, dragging a heavy suitcase up a flight of stone steps in the Positano heat.

## Choosing Which Coast Town Based on Your Hotel

One decision that's easy to overlook when booking a transfer is being specific about your destination town, rather than just saying "the Amalfi Coast." Positano, Amalfi, and Ravello are all part of the same coastline, but they sit at different points along it, and the practical experience of arriving in each is genuinely different.

**Positano** is the most visually dramatic — built almost vertically into the hillside — and also the town with the most limited vehicle access near the center. If your hotel is in the upper part of town, expect some walking or a shuttle from the drop-off point.

**Amalfi town** itself is comparatively easier for a vehicle to reach, with a larger central piazza close to the waterfront. If your hotel is directly in the town center, drop-off tends to be simpler than in Positano.

**Ravello** sits higher up, off the main coast road, which means the drive from Naples Airport includes an additional uphill stretch beyond the coastal route. It's quieter and less crowded than the beachfront towns, but worth knowing the drive takes a bit longer to reach.

There's no single verified travel time from Naples Airport to the Amalfi Coast as a whole, because it depends heavily on which of these towns you're actually headed to, along with traffic, season, and road conditions on the day. Positano is generally reached first coming from Naples, while Amalfi and especially Ravello add further time along the coast road. ## What to Confirm Before Landing

A short checklist worth going through a day or two before you fly:

- Your driver has the correct flight number and terminal, so they can track any delay.
- Your exact drop-off point is confirmed — the name of your hotel, or a landmark if your accommodation is up a stepped lane with no vehicle access.
- You know roughly how far the final stretch on foot might be, so you're not surprised.
- If you're arriving very late at night or very early in the morning, your hotel knows to expect you, since some smaller coast properties have limited front-desk hours.

## Final Thoughts

The Amalfi Coast rewards a bit of upfront planning, and arrival day is really where that planning pays off. Getting the buffer around your flight right, knowing where you'll meet your driver, and being realistic about the last stretch of the journey to your specific hotel all matter more than the exact minute-by-minute timing of the drive itself.
