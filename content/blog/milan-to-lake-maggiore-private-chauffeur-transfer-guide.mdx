## Why Lake Maggiore Is Worth the Detour

Lake Maggiore stretches along the border between Lombardy and Piedmont, with a character that's a little different from Lake Como's — broader, calmer in places, and centered around the town of Stresa and the Borromean Islands just offshore. It's a well-known destination in its own right, popular for its lakefront promenades, gardens, and the small islands you can reach by boat from the shore.

Because it sits further from Milan than Lake Como and doesn't have quite the same profile among first-time visitors, Lake Maggiore often ends up being the quieter, more relaxed option for a day away from the city — which is part of its appeal.

## How Far Is Lake Maggiore From Milan

Lake Maggiore makes a comfortable day-trip distance from Milan, similar in spirit to the kind of lake excursion many visitors already plan around Lake Como, though the exact drive time will depend on traffic, your specific starting point in the city, and which part of the lake you're heading to. Rather than quoting a fixed figure, it's more useful to think of it as a trip that comfortably fits into a full day out and back, with plenty of time left over once you arrive.

A private chauffeur takes the guesswork out of that journey. Instead of working out train connections or driving yourself through unfamiliar roads, you're picked up in Milan and dropped directly at the lakefront, with the return trip already arranged for whenever your day winds down.

## Stresa: The Natural Base for a Lake Maggiore Day

Stresa is the town most visitors use as their starting point on Lake Maggiore, and for good reason — it sits directly on the water, has a walkable lakefront promenade, and is the main departure point for boats out to the Borromean Islands. A chauffeur can drop you right along the lakefront in Stresa, which means your day starts with a walk along the water rather than a search for parking in a town that gets busy, especially in warmer months.

Stresa itself is worth some time before or after the islands — its promenade, gardens, and lakeside cafés make for an easy place to slow down, whether that's first thing in the morning or as a way to close out the day before heading back to Milan.

## The Borromean Islands

The Borromean Islands are the main draw for most visitors to this part of the lake, known for their gardens and palazzos set just offshore from Stresa. Reaching them involves a short boat crossing from the town, which is a straightforward add-on to a day that starts with a chauffeur drop-off in Stresa itself.

Because the islands are reached by boat rather than by road, this is one part of the day where the car isn't directly involved — but having a driver waiting back in Stresa for whenever you return from the islands means you're not trying to coordinate a return transfer on the spot after a day that's already run its own pace.

## Beyond Stresa: The Wider Lake Maggiore Shoreline

While Stresa is the natural starting point, the lake itself stretches well beyond that single town, with a shoreline that includes smaller villages and stretches of quieter countryside further along the water. Most first-time visitors don't need to venture much beyond Stresa and the islands to get a genuine feel for the lake, but travelers who've already done a Stresa day and want a slightly different experience on a return visit may find it worth asking a chauffeur about other points along the shoreline.

A private driver has the flexibility to adjust the route on the day itself, which is useful if the weather, the crowds in Stresa, or simply your own curiosity points you toward exploring a little further along the water than originally planned.

## The Best Time of Day to Visit

Like most lake destinations near Milan, Lake Maggiore tends to be at its calmest earlier in the day, before the boat crossings to the islands and the lakefront promenade in Stresa fill up with visitors. Leaving Milan in the morning generally gives you the most comfortable window in Stresa itself, with the option to extend into the afternoon once the initial rush has settled.

A chauffeur can help time the departure from Milan around this, aiming to arrive in Stresa before the town gets busiest rather than in the middle of the day when both the promenade and the boat queues tend to be at their most crowded.

## Structuring the Day So It Doesn't Feel Rushed

A day trip to Lake Maggiore works best when it isn't packed too tightly. Between the drive out, time in Stresa, a boat crossing to the islands, and the drive back to Milan, there's enough happening in one day without trying to add extra stops along the way. Building in some flexibility — an extra hour in Stresa if the weather's good, or an earlier return if it isn't — tends to make for a more relaxed experience than a fixed itinerary planned down to the minute.

This is one of the advantages of arranging the trip privately rather than around a fixed tour schedule or public transport timetable. The day can shift around what's actually happening rather than around someone else's departure time.
