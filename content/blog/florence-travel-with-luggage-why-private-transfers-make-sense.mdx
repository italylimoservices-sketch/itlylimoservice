Florence was not built with suitcases in mind. Its streets were laid centuries before anyone imagined a traveler wheeling a 23-kilo case over cobblestones, and no amount of clever packing changes the fact that the city's most charming features — narrow lanes, worn stone paving, tight doorways — are exactly the things that make hauling luggage through it genuinely tiring. **Traveling with luggage in Florence** is a different experience from traveling light, and it's worth planning for accordingly.

This guide looks specifically at the luggage problem across a Florence visit — not just one arrival point, but the whole stretch of moving bags through the city — and why a lot of travelers end up preferring direct, door-to-door transport once they've dealt with the alternative.

## Florence's Historic Center Wasn't Designed for Wheels

The historic center of Florence is largely paved in cobblestones — attractive underfoot when you're strolling with a coffee, considerably less so when you're dragging a suitcase behind you. Wheels catch on the gaps between stones, cases tip more easily than they would on smooth pavement, and what should be a quick walk from a taxi drop-off to your hotel entrance can turn into a slow, bumpy slog.

Sidewalks in many parts of the center are also narrow, sometimes barely wide enough for two people to pass, let alone two people each pulling a suitcase. Combine that with the pedestrian traffic in popular areas near the Duomo or along the main shopping streets, and moving luggage through the city center becomes an exercise in patience as much as logistics.

## Stairs Show Up More Often Than You'd Expect

Florence Santa Maria Novella, the city's main train station, is centrally located, which is genuinely convenient — but getting from a platform to street level, or navigating a station that's often busy and crowded, still means stairs, uneven flooring, and crowds to weave through. Once you're outside, many historic buildings, including hotels converted from older palazzi, have their own steps at the entrance, narrow stairwells, or lifts too small for a large case. None of this is a design flaw; it's just what comes with staying in a city built long before modern accessibility standards existed. It does mean luggage-heavy travel here requires a bit more planning than it might in a newer city.

## Why This Matters More for Some Travelers Than Others

A solo traveler with a single backpack can move through Florence without giving any of this a second thought. The calculation changes considerably for:

- Families traveling with children, where an adult's hands are already full without also managing suitcases
- Groups with several pieces of luggage each, multiplying the total bulk to move at once
- Travelers with mobility considerations, for whom cobblestones and stairs are more than an inconvenience
- Anyone arriving after a long flight or train journey, when the patience for navigating narrow streets with bags is already low
- Business travelers moving between a hotel and meetings with equipment or luggage in tow

If any of these describe your trip, the "just walk it" plan that works fine for a light packer can turn into the most exhausting part of your day.

## The Case for Door-to-Door Transport

The appeal of a private transfer in Florence isn't really about the distance covered — most trips within or into the city are short by any measure, generally 5 to 20 minutes depending on where you're going and traffic conditions. It's about what happens at each end of that trip. A taxi or shuttle typically drops you at a general point, leaving you to cover the last stretch, cobblestones and all, on foot. A private chauffeur transfer, by contrast, is built around the door-to-door leg specifically — your luggage is handled, and the car takes you as close to your actual entrance as the historic center's layout allows.

That last detail matters. Florence's historic center includes restricted traffic zones, similar to other Italian city centers, which can limit exactly where a vehicle is able to stop. A driver familiar with the city knows these restrictions and the practical routes around them, which is a meaningful advantage over guessing your way through unfamiliar streets with bags in tow.

## Public Transport and Luggage Don't Always Mix Well

Florence's bus network and the shuttle connections to the airport can work well for travelers packing light, but they're a harder sell once luggage enters the picture. Boarding a bus with a large suitcase during a crowded stretch of the day means finding somewhere to wedge it that doesn't block the aisle or other passengers, and getting off at the right stop with bags in hand, in an unfamiliar city, adds a layer of stress that a lighter packer simply doesn't encounter. None of this makes public transport a bad option — it's often perfectly fine for shorter hops — but it's worth being honest about where the friction shows up.

## Practical Ways to Reduce the Luggage Burden

Even with the best transport arrangements, a few habits make a real difference in Florence specifically:

- Pack lighter than you think you need to, since you'll likely be lifting your own bags at some point regardless of how you travel
- Choose luggage with sturdy, larger wheels if you know you'll be covering any cobblestone ground
- Confirm whether your hotel has an elevator, and how close a vehicle can realistically get to the entrance
- Keep valuables and essentials in a smaller bag you can carry separately if you do end up walking a stretch
