Ostuni announces itself from a distance — a hill town of whitewashed buildings stacked up a rise above the surrounding countryside, visible well before you arrive at its gates. It's known simply as "the White City," and that nickname is doing honest work, because the whole historic center really is painted in shades of white, originally for practical reasons tied to heat and hygiene rather than purely for effect. This guide focuses on planning the trip from Bari — timing, what to expect from the hilltop center, and what to bring.

## Why Ostuni Looks the Way It Does

The old town sits at the top of a hill in the Murgia dei Trulli area, with newer development spreading out below it on flatter ground. From a distance, the effect is striking: a solid mass of white against the green of the surrounding olive groves, which stretch out for a considerable distance in every direction and are themselves part of what gives this part of Puglia its character.

**The historic center** is a maze of narrow, winding streets and steep staircases, built this way originally for defensive reasons — a layout meant to confuse invaders is now simply an atmospheric place to get pleasantly lost for an afternoon. Nearly every surface is whitewashed, from building facades to the steps themselves, and the effect under strong midday sun is genuinely bright, almost dazzling in places.

**The cathedral**, sitting near the top of the hill, is a Gothic structure that stands out from the surrounding whitewash with its stone facade and rose window, and the piazza in front of it offers sweeping views over the countryside and, on a clear day, toward the coast in the distance.

**The views themselves** are a major part of Ostuni's appeal. Because the town sits elevated above its surroundings, various points around the old town — terraces, gaps between buildings, the area near the cathedral — offer long views over olive groves toward the Adriatic.

## What to Pack and Expect

Because Ostuni's charm is largely about walking its hilltop streets, a few practical considerations go further here than in a flatter town:

- **Comfortable, sturdy shoes.** The old town's streets are a mix of smooth, worn stone and genuine staircases, and some sections have real gradient. Flat sandals are workable but supportive shoes make the walk more comfortable.
- **Sun protection.** The whitewashed surfaces reflect a lot of light, and the hilltop position means less shade than you might find in a denser, taller old town. A hat and sunscreen matter more here than in many other stops.
- **Water.** Especially if visiting in warmer months, the climbing involved in exploring the center means it's worth carrying water rather than relying on finding a shop at the right moment.
- **A camera with some charge left.** The combination of whitewashed architecture and long countryside views makes this one of the more photogenic stops in the region, and it's easy to use up a phone battery faster than expected.

## How Much Time to Set Aside

A half-day covers the core of Ostuni reasonably well — a walk up through the old town, the cathedral and its piazza, and time to wander the maze of whitewashed streets. A full day allows for a slower pace, more time taking in the views, and the option of a meal somewhere with an outlook over the olive groves, which several restaurants in the upper town offer.

Given the honest uncertainty around drive time discussed above, a full day gives considerably more breathing room than trying to squeeze Ostuni into a tight half-day round trip from Bari.

## The Countryside Around Ostuni

Ostuni's appeal isn't confined to the hilltop old town itself. The surrounding countryside is part of what gives the area its character — rolling land covered in olive groves, some containing trees that are genuinely old, alongside masserie, the fortified farmhouses historically used to manage agricultural estates in this part of Puglia. Many of these masserie have been converted into accommodations or restaurants, and travelers with extra time sometimes build in a stop at one as part of a longer day, rather than treating Ostuni purely as a town to walk through. This countryside setting is also part of why the views from the old town feel so expansive — there's genuinely a lot of open land to look out over, stretching toward the coast in the distance.
