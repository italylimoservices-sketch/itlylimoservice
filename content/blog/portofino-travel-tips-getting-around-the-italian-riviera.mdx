Getting around Portofino and the stretch of the Italian Riviera around it is a different experience from navigating a larger Italian city. There's no metro system, no sprawling road network to master, and the village at the center of it all restricts vehicles near its own harbor. For first-time visitors, that combination raises a reasonable question: what's actually the best way to move between these places, and when does it make sense to drive yourself versus relying on public transport, a boat, or a private chauffeur?

## Start With Portofino's Own Limits

Before anything else, it helps to understand that Portofino's village center has restricted vehicle access near the harbor. Whatever your plan for reaching the area, it will involve parking or being dropped off outside the immediate center and walking the final stretch in. This isn't a quirk to work around so much as a basic fact of how the village operates, and it shapes every other decision about getting there.

Santa Margherita Ligure, the nearest larger town to Portofino, doesn't have this same restriction and functions as a more practical base for anyone who wants easier parking, more lodging options, or simply a calmer town to return to at the end of the day.

## Self-Driving: When It Makes Sense

Renting a car and driving yourself can work well for travelers who want full independence and are comfortable with narrower, winding coastal roads that don't always behave like a flatter inland route. It suits a trip with a flexible schedule and no fixed deadlines, where the occasional slow stretch of road or a parking search near a popular stop isn't a serious problem.

Where self-driving becomes more of a burden is exactly where Portofino's restrictions kick in — you'll still need to find parking outside the village center and walk in, so the independence a rental car offers largely disappears once you're close to the destination itself. Self-driving also asks more of a traveler already managing an unfamiliar itinerary, a different side of the road in some cases, and road conditions that can shift with season and weather along this part of the coast.

## Public Transport: A Reasonable Option for Some Trips

There are public transport connections along this stretch of the Riviera, including trains serving the larger towns in the region and local buses that run toward Portofino itself from Santa Margherita Ligure. For a solo traveler or a couple with light luggage and no fixed schedule to keep, this can be a perfectly workable way to get around, particularly for the final approach into Portofino where driving isn't much of an advantage anyway given the restricted access.

The trade-off is the usual one with public transport anywhere: fixed schedules, potential crowding during busier travel periods, and less flexibility if your plans change during the day. It also doesn't solve the luggage problem for travelers arriving with a full set of bags, whether for a longer stay or a cruise-related visit, since stations and bus stops aren't always close to where you actually need to be.

## Traveling by Boat

Boat travel is a notable feature of this coastline, and ferry connections along the Riviera are a genuinely popular way to see Portofino's harbor from the water or to connect it with neighboring towns during the right season. This can be a worthwhile addition to a trip, particularly for the view of the coastline it offers that a road transfer can't replicate. That said, boat schedules depend on the season and operating conditions, and they're worth treating as a complement to your ground transportation plan rather than a full replacement for it, since they won't necessarily align with your actual arrival or departure logistics, hotel location, or luggage needs.

## When a Private Chauffeur Is Worth It

A private chauffeur tends to make the most sense in exactly the situations where self-driving and public transport both show their limits: groups or families with luggage, travelers on a schedule tied to a cruise ship's departure, anyone combining Portofino with a longer itinerary across multiple towns, or simply visitors who'd rather not manage parking, timetables, or unfamiliar roads on a trip meant to be relaxing.

The advantage isn't just comfort in the abstract. It's the flexibility of not being locked to a fixed departure time, the convenience of door-to-door pickup rather than a walk to a station, and a driver who already knows where vehicles can and can't go near Portofino's center, which removes a layer of guesswork that first-time visitors often don't anticipate. ## Orientation for First-Time Visitors

A few general points worth keeping in mind for anyone new to this part of the Riviera:

- **Distances are often shorter than they look on a map, but timing still varies.** There's no verified, fixed driving time between Portofino and its neighboring towns that can be quoted confidently here — actual timing depends on traffic, season, and road conditions, so it's worth treating any estimate as a starting point rather than a guarantee.
- **Parking near Portofino is limited and seasonal demand is real.** Expect to park outside the center regardless of how you arrive, and expect that demand for the closest spots increases substantially during busier months.
- **Santa Margherita Ligure is a practical base.** For travelers splitting time across this area, basing yourself there rather than trying to stay directly in Portofino's small village tends to be more comfortable, with easier access to transport options in all directions. Our [day trip guide](/blog/portofino-and-santa-margherita-private-chauffeur-day-trip-guide) covers how to pair the two towns in a single day.
- **Weather along the coast can shift plans.** Coastal conditions here can behave differently from inland Italy, and it's worth building some flexibility into any day that depends on clear weather, particularly if a boat trip or a walk along the lighthouse path is part of the plan.
- **If Genoa is part of your trip, plan the two legs separately.** Genoa is a larger city with its own pace and sights, and pairing it with Portofino works best when each gets a realistic amount of time rather than being squeezed into the same rushed day. ## Choosing the Right Approach for Your Trip

There isn't a single correct way to get around this part of the Riviera — the right choice depends on your group size, luggage, schedule, and how much flexibility matters to you. A solo traveler with a loose schedule and light bags might be perfectly happy with a mix of walking, local buses, and the occasional boat. A family arriving with a cruise's worth of luggage, or a couple on a tight timeline tied to a ship's departure, will generally find more peace of mind in a private transfer that removes the guesswork entirely.
