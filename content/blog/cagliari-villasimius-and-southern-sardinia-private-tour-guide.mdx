Southern Sardinia packs a genuine variety of experiences into a relatively compact corner of the island — Cagliari's city life and history, Villasimius's beaches and harbor town feel, and, for travelers with more time, Costa Rei's quieter stretch of coast further along. Combining these into one trip is a popular way to see the south properly, and this guide covers how that sequencing tends to work.

## The Three Stops, Briefly

**Cagliari** is Sardinia's capital, a hillside city overlooking its own gulf, with a historic citadel quarter, a working port, and a mix of city life that's genuinely different from the beach towns further along the coast. It makes a natural base for the start or end of a southern Sardinia trip, both for its own sights and its airport and port connections.

**Villasimius** sits southeast of Cagliari, known for a string of excellent beaches, clear water, and a town center with its own harbor, shops, and restaurants. It has more of a self-contained resort-town feel than Cagliari, while still offering enough going on in the evenings that it doesn't feel purely beach-and-sleep.

**Costa Rei**, further along the coast past Villasimius, is a longer, more spread-out stretch of dune-backed beach with a quieter, more low-key character. It's covered in more depth in our [Cagliari to Costa Rei transfer guide](/blog/cagliari-to-costa-rei-private-transfer-what-to-know-before-you-go), but it's worth knowing as an option if your itinerary has room for a third stop.

## Why This Works as a Loop

Cagliari, Villasimius, and Costa Rei sit along a reasonably logical line heading southeast from the capital, which makes them easier to sequence than destinations scattered across different parts of the island. That said, we don't have verified drive times for any of these legs — actual travel time depends on the route, traffic around Cagliari, and the season, particularly during busier summer months when coastal roads near popular beaches see more traffic. Treat any schedule here as a reasonable structure to adapt, not a fixed countdown.

A sensible shape for the trip looks something like:

1. **Start in Cagliari** for a day or two, covering the city itself before heading toward the coast.
2. **Move to Villasimius** for the beach-focused portion of the trip, staying a few nights to actually use the beaches rather than rushing through.
3. **Continue to Costa Rei**, if your schedule allows, for a quieter stretch further along the coast — optional, but a natural extension if you have the time.
4. **Return to Cagliari** for departure, whether that's a flight, a cruise connection, or onward travel elsewhere in Sardinia.

This order avoids backtracking — each leg moves further from Cagliari rather than zigzagging between stops.

## Budgeting Time for Each Stop

**Cagliari** rewards at least a full day, ideally two, if you want to see the Castello district, the cathedral, the archaeological museum, and get a feel for the city beyond a quick drive-through. It's also worth some time simply walking the Marina quarter and the waterfront, which gives a different sense of the city than the hilltop citadel does.

**Villasimius** works best with a multi-night stay rather than a day trip, given how much of its appeal is simply time spent at its beaches. A single day lets you see one or two beaches and the town center; a longer stay lets you actually relax into the pace of the place, which is arguably the point of visiting a beach town like this at all.

**Costa Rei**, if you add it, deserves its own stretch of nights rather than being squeezed in as an afternoon stop, given both its distance from Cagliari and its character as a slower, more spread-out destination. ## Day-Trip vs. Multi-Stop Stay

Not every traveler needs to move hotels for this itinerary. Some options worth weighing:

- **Base in Cagliari, day-trip to Villasimius.** This keeps luggage in one place and works for travelers who want city amenities as a home base, accepting a longer day each time they head to the coast.
- **Base in Villasimius, day-trip into Cagliari.** This flips the previous option, prioritizing beach time and treating Cagliari as a single city-focused outing.
- **Move between all three.** The most thorough option, giving each stop its own dedicated nights rather than treating any of them as a rushed add-on.

There's no universally correct choice here — it depends on whether you'd rather minimize how often you pack and unpack, or maximize time actually spent at each destination.

## When to Go

Southern Sardinia's peak season runs through the summer, when Villasimius and Costa Rei's beaches are at their most crowded and roads along this stretch of coast see noticeably more traffic than during quieter months. If your trip falls in July or August, it's worth assuming longer drive times than you might expect and booking accommodation well ahead, since this part of the island draws heavy demand during the busiest weeks.

Late spring and early autumn offer a gentler alternative — warm enough for the beach on many days, with a calmer pace in both Villasimius and Cagliari. Some seasonal businesses along the coast reduce their hours or close outside the main summer window, so it's worth checking ahead if your trip falls in the shoulder season.

## Practical Tips for the Trip

A few smaller details tend to smooth out a multi-stop trip like this:

- **Pack light enough to move between hotels comfortably**, since this itinerary involves more luggage handling than a single-destination stay.
- **Book accommodation in Villasimius and Costa Rei early for summer travel**, as rooms in both towns fill up well ahead of peak season.
- **Keep your itinerary loosely flexible**, especially around how many nights you spend at each stop, since it's common to want more beach time than initially planned once you're actually there.
- **Confirm your Cagliari pickup and drop-off points clearly**, whether that's the airport, the cruise port, or a hotel, so the start and end of the trip run smoothly regardless of how the middle unfolds.
