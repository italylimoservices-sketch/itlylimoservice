Genoa, Portofino, and Santa Margherita Ligure sit close enough together that combining all three into a single private tour is a genuinely popular way to see this stretch of the Italian Riviera. It's a busier day than a simple two-stop trip, so this guide focuses specifically on how to sequence three stops rather than two, and what trade-offs come with trying to fit all of them into one outing.

## Why Three Stops Instead of Two

Travelers who want to go beyond a straightforward Genoa-and-Portofino day often add Santa Margherita Ligure to the mix, since it sits right along the way between the two and offers a different pace from either — a proper seafront town, as opposed to Genoa's urban density or Portofino's compact harbor. If you've read our guide to the simpler [Genoa and Portofino day trip](/blog/genoa-and-portofino-private-chauffeur-day-trip-guide), think of this as the fuller version: more variety, but less time to linger at each individual stop.

## Sequencing the Day

A sensible order for a three-stop day generally runs in one direction along the coast rather than backtracking:

**1. Morning in Genoa.** Start the day with a few focused hours in Genoa's historic center — the *caruggi* lanes, the cluster of Renaissance palaces along streets like Via Garibaldi, and the waterfront. Because this is one stop among three today rather than the day's main focus, it's worth deciding in advance on a couple of priorities here rather than trying to see everything the city offers.

**2. Midday stop in Santa Margherita Ligure.** Reaching Santa Margherita next makes sense geographically, since it sits along the route out from Genoa before the final approach to Portofino. It's also a sensible place to pause for lunch — a seafront promenade and a reasonably level, walkable center make it an easier midday stop than Portofino's smaller, often busier harbor area. A relaxed hour or two here, including a proper meal, sets a good pace for the rest of the day.

**3. Afternoon in Portofino.** Save Portofino for the back half of the day. Its small harbor, the piazzetta, and the paths up toward Castello Brown or out to the lighthouse at Punta del Capo are best enjoyed without the pressure of a long list of other stops still ahead. Because vehicle access into the village center is limited, a chauffeur will typically drop you near the edge of Portofino, leaving the harbor itself to be explored on foot.

**4. Return to Genoa.** The day wraps with the return drive to Genoa, timed around whatever your evening plans require — a cruise departure, a hotel check-in, or an onward journey.

This order isn't the only workable sequence, and a chauffeur familiar with the route can adjust it based on your arrival time, a cruise schedule, or simply which of the three stops interests you most. But starting in Genoa, pausing in Santa Margherita, and finishing in Portofino tends to flow more naturally than jumping back and forth along the coast.

## Being Honest About Time

Three stops in a day is more ambitious than two, and it's worth being upfront about that trade-off. We're not going to publish a specific drive-time or distance figure for any leg of this route — there's no verified data that would hold true across different travel dates, traffic, and season, and a precise-sounding number that ignores those variables wouldn't be an honest one to give you. What we can say in general terms is that adding a third stop to a day that already includes Genoa and Portofino means less time at each individual place than a simpler two-stop trip would allow.

If your priority is depth — properly wandering Portofino's harbor, or sitting for an unhurried lunch in Santa Margherita without checking the clock — a two-stop day might suit you better. If your priority is breadth, seeing the range this part of the coast offers in a single outing, three stops is a reasonable and popular way to do it. ## Building in Buffer

Because travel time along this coast varies with traffic and season in ways that can't be predicted precisely, the most useful thing you can do when planning a three-stop day is leave room for things to run long. That might mean treating the Santa Margherita stop as flexible — shorter if the morning in Genoa ran over, longer if everything's moving smoothly — rather than locking every stop to a fixed window. A private chauffeur can adjust the plan in real time as the day unfolds, which is one of the real advantages over a fixed-schedule group tour with set stop times.

## Starting Early Helps More Than It Seems

Of everything that affects whether a three-stop day feels comfortable or rushed, an early start probably matters most. Beginning before the coastal road and both Portofino and Santa Margherita fill up with other visitors buys back time that would otherwise be lost later in the day, particularly during busier months. It also gives you a cushion: if Genoa's morning runs a little long, an early start means that overrun eats into midday slack rather than pushing Portofino into a rushed, late-afternoon scramble.

## What to Cut if the Day Runs Long

It's worth deciding in advance what gives if the day runs behind. Many travelers find it easier to shorten the Santa Margherita stop than to rush through Genoa's historic center or cut Portofino short, since Santa Margherita's promenade is a pleasant walk whether you spend thirty minutes or two hours there, while both Genoa and Portofino have a more fixed set of sights that reward a minimum amount of time. Deciding this before the day starts avoids a stressful debate mid-afternoon about what to skip.
