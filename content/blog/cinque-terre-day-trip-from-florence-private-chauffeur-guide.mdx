Once you've decided a private chauffeur is how you're getting from Florence to Cinque Terre, the next question is what to actually do with the day once you arrive. This isn't about weighing transfer options — that decision is covered in our piece on [whether a private transfer is worth it](/blog/florence-to-cinque-terre-private-transfer-is-it-worth-it). This is about the itinerary itself: which villages to prioritize, in what order, and when to realistically call it and head back.

## Start With an Honest Time Budget

There's no verified figure for exactly how long the drive from Florence to the coast takes — it depends on traffic, weather, and where exactly you start from, and anyone quoting you a precise number without those caveats is guessing. What matters for planning purposes is that it's a longer drive than Florence's closer Tuscan day trips, which means your usable time in Cinque Terre itself is the middle chunk of a long day, bookended by two drives.

That reality should shape your expectations from the start: this is not a day where you casually tour all five villages at a relaxed pace. Most travelers do better treating it as a day to see two or three villages properly, rather than rushing through all five and remembering none of them clearly.

## A Suggested Village Order

Because the five villages sit along a single stretch of coast — Monterosso, Vernazza, Corniglia, Manarola, and Riomaggiore, running roughly northwest to southeast — a chauffeur typically drops you at one end, often near Monterosso or La Spezia depending on which gateway suits your route, letting you work your way along the coast using the local train between stops rather than backtracking.

**If dropped near Monterosso:**

1. **Monterosso** first. It's the largest and most spread-out of the five, with the most beach-town feel and the easiest walking of the group, which makes it a reasonable village to start with while you're still fresh.
2. **Vernazza** next, a short hop by the regional train. Many travelers consider it the most photogenic of the five, with its small harbor and the castle above it, so it's worth giving it real time rather than rushing through.
3. **Corniglia** if time allows. It sits higher above the water than the others, reached by a climb from the train station, and tends to be quieter than Monterosso or Vernazza.

**If dropped near La Spezia:**

1. **Riomaggiore** first, the closest village to La Spezia and a good starting point for working northwest along the coast.
2. **Manarola** next, a short train ride away, known for its colorful houses stacked above the harbor — a village many people recognize instantly from photos even before visiting.
3. **Vernazza** if your pace allows, pushing further along the coast for the most visually striking of the five before turning back.

Either direction works. What matters more than the exact starting point is picking a direction and committing to it, rather than bouncing back and forth between villages and losing time to the regional train's schedule.

## Why Two or Three Villages, Not Five

It's tempting to think you can simply ride the regional train to all five villages in a single day, since the train itself doesn't take long between stops. In practice, the limiting factor isn't the train — it's everything around it: walking from the station up into each village center, spending real time once you're there, finding somewhere to eat, and accounting for the stairs and elevation change that are part of every village's layout.

Add in the drive from Florence at the start and the return drive at the end, and a day that tries to cover all five villages tends to leave you rushing through each one without actually experiencing any of them. Two villages, explored properly, generally beats five villages seen in passing.

## A Realistic Hour-by-Hour Shape

Exact timing depends on your departure time and the drive itself, but a typical structure looks something like this:

- **Morning:** Depart Florence early. An early start matters more on this trip than on closer Tuscan excursions, since it directly determines how much usable time you have on the coast.
- **Late morning:** Arrive at your gateway town, meet the regional train or begin walking into your first village.
- **Midday:** Explore your first village — this is a good point for lunch, since villages get busier and hotter as the day goes on.
Italian coastal towns in peak season can be crowded by early afternoon, so seeing your first stop before the crowds build is worth prioritizing.
- **Early afternoon:** Move to your second village by regional train, allowing time to walk, take in the views, and not feel rushed.
- **Late afternoon:** A third village if your pace allows, or extra time at your second stop if you'd rather not rush — this is the point in the day to make that call honestly rather than forcing a third stop that leaves you drained.
- **Early evening:** Head back to your gateway town for the return drive to Florence, building in some buffer since this is the point in the day travelers most often run later than planned.

## When to Turn Back

The honest answer: earlier than feels natural. The return drive to Florence is itself a meaningful chunk of the day, and arriving back late after an already long day isn't an enjoyable way to end it. A useful rule of thumb is to decide your latest departure time from the coast before you even leave Florence that morning, and treat it as a hard stop rather than something to push back "just a little" once you're having a good time in the villages.

If you find yourself tempted to squeeze in a fourth village or stretch the day later than planned, it's worth remembering that an overnight stay on the coast is a common alternative for travelers who want more time, rather than trying to force a rushed day trip to cover more ground than it comfortably can. ## Practical Notes for the Day

- Wear proper walking shoes — every village involves stairs and uneven surfaces, and this isn't a day for sandals.
- Carry water and a light layer; the coast can be breezier than Florence, and shade is limited on some stretches.
- Keep your chauffeur informed of your rough plan for the day so pickup timing at the end has some flexibility built in.
- Confirm with your driver which gateway town works best for pickup and drop-off before the day begins, since La Spezia and Levanto sit at opposite ends of the coastal stretch.

## Making It Work

A Cinque Terre day trip from Florence rewards a bit of discipline: pick a direction along the coast, commit to two or three villages rather than chasing all five, and set a firm time to turn back before the day even starts. Arranging the transport through a [private chauffeur service](/chauffeur-service) takes care of the long drive on both ends, leaving your energy for the villages themselves rather than the logistics of getting to them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many Cinque Terre villages can I see in one day trip from Florence?**
Most travelers realistically manage two or three villages properly, given how much of the day is already spent on the drive to and from Florence. Trying to fit all five tends to mean rushing through each one.

**Which Cinque Terre village should I visit first?**
It depends on which gateway town your chauffeur drops you at. If you start near Monterosso, that village's easier walking makes a good first stop. If you start near La Spezia, Riomaggiore is the natural starting point.

**What time should I plan to leave the coast to get back to Florence?**
Decide your latest departure time before you even leave Florence that morning, and treat it as fixed. It's easy to lose track of time in the villages and end up starting the return drive later than planned.

**Is it better to see fewer villages properly or try to see all five?**
Seeing two or three villages properly generally makes for a more satisfying day than rushing through all five, especially once you factor in the walking, stairs, and train connections between stops.
