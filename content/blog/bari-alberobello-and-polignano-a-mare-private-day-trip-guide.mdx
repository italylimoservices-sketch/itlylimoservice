Alberobello's trulli and Polignano a Mare's cliffside streets are two of the most photographed sights in Puglia, and both sit close enough to Bari that combining them into a single day trip is one of the most common requests we see. This guide lays out a workable order for the day, along with an honest look at how to budget time when there's no verified distance or drive-time figure to lean on.

## Why This Combination Works

Alberobello and Polignano a Mare offer genuinely different experiences, which is part of why pairing them makes for a satisfying day rather than two similar stops. Alberobello is inland, built around its extraordinary concentration of trulli — the conical, whitewashed stone huts with grey corbelled roofs that give the town its UNESCO-listed character. Polignano a Mare is coastal, a town perched dramatically on limestone cliffs above the Adriatic, with a historic center that opens suddenly onto sea views at nearly every turn. Doing both in one day gives you a genuine sense of Puglia's range: traditional rural architecture in one stop, dramatic coastline in the other.

## Sequencing the Day

A sensible approach is to treat this as a loop out from Bari rather than a straight line, visiting the inland stop first and the coastal stop second, or vice versa, depending on where your day starts and what time you want to see each town at its best.

**Option one: Alberobello first, Polignano second.** Starting inland means you see Alberobello's trulli districts in the cooler part of the morning, before the streets fill with day-trip crowds, then head toward the coast for Polignano in the afternoon, timing your arrival for golden-hour light over the cliffs and the water — a genuinely good look for this particular town.

**Option two: Polignano first, Alberobello second.** This works well if your accommodation sits closer to the coast, or if you'd rather have your sea views and cliffside lunch earlier in the day and spend the afternoon in Alberobello's narrower, shadier lanes once the midday sun has passed its peak.

Neither order is objectively better — it depends on your starting point, the season, and whether you'd rather beat the crowds in Alberobello or catch better light in Polignano. A private chauffeur familiar with both towns can help you decide based on your actual travel dates.

## What to See in Alberobello

- **Rione Monti** — the larger and more concentrated trulli district, with narrow lanes winding between hundreds of the distinctive conical structures, many now housing small shops.
- **Rione Aia Piccola** — a quieter trulli district with fewer tourist shops, giving a more residential sense of what these neighborhoods actually feel like to live in.
- **Trullo Sovrano** — one of the few two-story trulli in town, worth a look for anyone curious about the construction style beyond the typical single-story huts.
- **Casa d'Amore** — notable as the first building in Alberobello constructed with mortar rather than the traditional dry-stone method, marking a shift in the town's building history.

## What to See in Polignano a Mare

- **The historic center's cliffside streets** — simply wandering the old town's narrow lanes, which repeatedly open onto unexpected sea views, is most of the appeal here.
- **Lama Monachile** — the small cove beach wedged between cliffs right at the edge of the old town, one of the most photographed spots in Puglia.
- **The clifftop viewpoints** — several spots around town offer sweeping views along the coastline, particularly striking with the afternoon light.
- **A seafood lunch** — Polignano's position on the water makes it a natural lunch stop, and a relaxed meal here is a good way to break up the day.

## When to Turn Back

Given that this is a three-point day with real but unquantified driving between each stop, it's worth deciding in advance what happens if the day runs long. A few honest guidelines:

- **If you're running behind by midafternoon, consider shortening whichever town you've already seen enough of**, rather than rushing the final stop just to say you visited it.
- **Polignano is the stop most worth protecting for good light**, since its cliffside views genuinely change character between harsh midday sun and the softer light of late afternoon. If a choice has to be made, many travelers prefer to arrive in Polignano later rather than earlier.
- **Don't push the return to Bari too late if you have an early start the next day.** This is a full day on the road, and arriving back exhausted undercuts the value of what you just saw.
