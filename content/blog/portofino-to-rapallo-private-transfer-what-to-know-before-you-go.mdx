## Where Rapallo Fits

Rapallo sits along the same stretch of Ligurian coastline as Portofino and Santa Margherita Ligure, a bit further along from Portofino than Santa Margherita Ligure is. It's a larger, more workaday town than either of the other two — less polished for tourism, with a genuine year-round community rather than a place that exists primarily around visitors. That's part of its appeal for travelers who want a look at how people actually live on this coast, rather than another dose of boutique shopping and harborside photo ops.

## How Rapallo Differs From Santa Margherita Ligure

Travelers often lump Rapallo and Santa Margherita Ligure together as similar alternatives to Portofino, but they have a noticeably different feel once you're actually there.

**Santa Margherita Ligure** leans more upscale and resort-oriented, with a palm-lined seafront, a refined villa and gardens, and a town center that still caters heavily to visitors, even if it's less intensely so than Portofino itself.

**Rapallo** feels more like a real Italian town that happens to be on a beautiful stretch of coast. It has its own long seafront promenade and a notable medieval castle sitting right at the water's edge, built out on what was once a small rock in the sea, which gives the town a distinctive silhouette from the water. Rapallo is also a cable car departure point for Montallegro, a sanctuary set on a hill above town with views back down over the bay — a different kind of excursion than anything available directly from Portofino or Santa Margherita Ligure.

**The pace is different, too.** Rapallo doesn't carry quite the same density of boutique shops and high-end restaurants that defines Portofino's piazzetta, which some travelers find a relief rather than a downside. If Portofino and Santa Margherita Ligure both start to feel like the same experience after a day or two, Rapallo offers something with a bit more local texture.

## Why Make the Trip

For travelers who've already spent time in Portofino and Santa Margherita Ligure, Rapallo is a sensible next stop precisely because it offers a change of pace rather than more of the same. It's also a reasonable base in its own right for travelers who want to be near Portofino without paying Portofino or Santa Margherita Ligure prices, since Rapallo tends to be a more affordable option for accommodation while remaining within easy reach of the more famous towns nearby.

The castle and seafront alone are worth an unhurried afternoon, and the cable car up to Montallegro is a worthwhile detour if you're interested in views over the bay that you won't get from sea level in Portofino.

## What to Expect Day-to-Day

Rapallo has a working marina alongside its promenade, a central old town with some genuinely old architecture tucked behind the waterfront, and a market scene that feels more oriented toward residents than toward tourists. It's a good place to get a sense of ordinary Ligurian coastal life, which can be a useful contrast if the rest of your trip has been built around more rarefied destinations.

Restaurants in Rapallo also tend to be a bit easier on the wallet than in Portofino specifically, which matters if your trip has included a few pricier meals already and you're looking for a more relaxed, less expensive evening without sacrificing the coastal setting.

Rapallo's history also runs deeper than its current role as a quiet alternative to its more famous neighbors suggests. The town has long functioned as a stopping point along this coast, and its waterfront and old town reflect centuries of that role, rather than having been built up primarily for modern tourism the way parts of Portofino have been. That history is part of what gives the town its distinct, lived-in feel compared to the more curated atmosphere nearby.

**Traffic through Santa Margherita Ligure affects this route.** Since the road between Portofino and Rapallo generally passes through or near Santa Margherita Ligure, congestion there in peak season can add time to a trip that would otherwise be straightforward.
