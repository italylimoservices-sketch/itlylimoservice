Sardinia doesn't organize itself around one city the way some Italian destinations do. Italy's second-largest island spreads its appeal across three separate gateways, a glamorous northeastern coastline, a quieter south, and an interior that most visitors never see at all. A **private chauffeur service in Sardinia** is built around that spread-out geography — moving between airports, coastal resorts and inland towns without forcing a trip to revolve around a single base. This guide walks through the main pieces: arriving, touring the coast and interior, the Costa Smeralda specifically, and the business and leisure situations where a private driver tends to make the most sense.

## Three Airports, Three Different Trips

Sardinia has no single main entry point. Cagliari, in the south, is the island's capital and one of its busiest airports, serving travelers heading to the southern coast or starting a loop around the island. Olbia, in the northeast, is the primary gateway for the Costa Smeralda and the glamorous stretch of coastline around Porto Cervo. Alghero, in the northwest, has its own airport and a old town shaped by centuries of Catalan influence, and tends to serve travelers focused on the northwest coast rather than the island as a whole.

Which airport you land at really does shape the trip. A visitor flying into Olbia for a week at a Costa Smeralda resort has a very different itinerary from someone landing in Cagliari to explore the south and interior, or someone arriving in Alghero to spend a few days around the northwest before working their way further along the coast. We won't put a specific drive time on any route between these points — there's no verified figure for Sardinia's roads on this site, and actual travel time depends on the road taken, the season, and how much traffic builds up around the bigger towns in peak summer — but a private chauffeur arranged in advance can meet you at any of the three, which matters more here than it might in a country with one obvious hub.

Some visitors also use two different airports on the same trip — flying into one and departing from another after a loop around the island, rather than doubling back to the same point. A chauffeur booking can be arranged either way, for a straightforward one-way arrival or for a longer route that ends somewhere else entirely.

## The Costa Smeralda and the Rest of the Coast

Ask most people what they associate with Sardinia and the Costa Smeralda usually comes up first — the stretch of coastline near Olbia known for Porto Cervo and a certain kind of glamour that draws a particular crowd each summer. It's a real and well-known part of the island's identity, and a meaningful share of chauffeur bookings in Sardinia involve getting visitors to and around that area specifically.

But the Costa Smeralda is only one piece of a much longer coastline. The island's edges run in nearly every direction, with beaches, cliffs and smaller towns scattered well beyond the northeast corner. A trip that treats the Costa Smeralda as a base for day exploring, rather than the entire itinerary, tends to show a different side of Sardinia — quieter coves, less crowded towns, and a slower pace that contrasts with the resort scene around Porto Cervo. A private driver who already knows the coastal roads can move between these areas without the group needing to work out the route themselves, which matters on an island where road quality and traffic patterns shift noticeably between the well-traveled northeast and the quieter stretches elsewhere.

## Sardinia's Interior Is Easy to Overlook

Most first-time visitors plan a Sardinia trip entirely around the coast, which is understandable given how much of the island's reputation rests on its beaches. But the interior holds a different kind of appeal — mountain villages, older traditions, and a landscape that has very little in common with the resort towns an hour or two away. It's not a part of the island that gets the same tourist traffic, and public transport options thin out quickly once you're away from the coastal towns, which is part of why a private driver tends to be the more practical way to see it on a trip that also includes coastal time.

A day or two spent inland, paired with the rest of a coastal itinerary, gives a fuller sense of what Sardinia actually is beyond the postcard image. It's not necessary for every visitor, but it's worth knowing the option exists before assuming the whole trip has to stay near the water.

## Business Travel to Sardinia

Sardinia isn't only a leisure destination. Cagliari in particular sees a share of business travel tied to its role as the island's administrative and economic center, and Olbia picks up some of the same traffic connected to the Costa Smeralda's commercial side. For business trips, the priorities shift away from scenic flexibility and toward punctuality, a quiet professional vehicle, and a driver who treats a pickup window as fixed rather than approximate. ## Choosing a Vehicle for a Sardinia Trip

Vehicle choice comes down mostly to group size and how much luggage a multi-day stay involves. A couple or solo traveler moving between an airport, a coastal hotel and a few day trips is usually well matched to an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan), both suited to up to three passengers and two suitcases. A group of four or five, especially with the extra bags a longer coastal stay tends to bring, generally does better in a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), built for up to five passengers and four suitcases. Larger families or small groups usually need an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), both built for up to seven passengers with room for six suitcases — a common choice when a family or wedding party is moving together.

## What to Expect When Booking

Booking a Sardinia chauffeur usually starts with which airport you're using, how many people and bags are traveling, and a general sense of the plan — a single transfer, a longer touring itinerary, or a wedding weekend with guests arriving from different directions.
