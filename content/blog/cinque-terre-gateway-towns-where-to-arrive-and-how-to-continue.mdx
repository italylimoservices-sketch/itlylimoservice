Anyone planning a Cinque Terre trip runs into the same question fairly quickly: if cars can't drive into the villages, where exactly does a car take you? The answer is a handful of "gateway towns" — places on the edge of the pedestrian zone where road transportation realistically ends and the on-foot or on-train portion of the visit begins. Understanding how these gateway towns work is the foundation for planning almost any part of a Cinque Terre trip, whether you're arranging a private chauffeur, driving yourself, or just trying to figure out the logistics in advance.

## Why Gateway Towns Exist in the First Place

Monterosso, Vernazza, Corniglia, Manarola and Riomaggiore were built directly into the cliffs and terraces of the Ligurian coast, long before cars existed. Many of the connecting paths between and within the villages are staircases, narrow lanes, or terraced walkways rather than roads wide enough for vehicle traffic. That's not a modern restriction imposed on visitors — it's simply how the villages were constructed, and it's a large part of why they've kept their character over the centuries rather than being reshaped around car access the way many towns eventually were.

Because of that, a few towns at the edges of this stretch of coast have become the practical points where road transportation and the pedestrian, train-connected villages meet. These are generally referred to as gateway towns, and which one makes sense for your trip depends mostly on where you're coming from and what kind of visit you're planning.

## La Spezia: The Southern Gateway

La Spezia sits at the southern end of the Cinque Terre area and is a real, working cruise port — it's the main arrival point for cruise passengers visiting the region, and transfers connected to cruise arrivals are typically arranged through the [cruise port transfers](/cruise-port-transfers) service rather than a standalone destination page, since La Spezia doesn't have its own page on this site. Beyond cruise traffic, La Spezia is also a reasonable arrival point for travelers driving up from the south, including day trips from [Florence](/destinations/florence) or [Pisa](/destinations/pisa).

From La Spezia, the regional train line that runs along the coast connects onward to each of the five villages, making it a practical base to continue from by rail rather than by road.

## Levanto and Monterosso: The Northern Approach

On the northern side, Levanto and Monterosso serve a similar function for travelers approaching from that direction. Monterosso is itself the northernmost of the five Cinque Terre villages and has enough road access at its edges to function as an arrival point, while Levanto sits just beyond it and connects into the same regional train line. Travelers arriving from the north, or looping back after visiting other parts of Liguria, often use one of these as their point of entry into the area rather than approaching via La Spezia.

## How You Actually Continue From a Gateway Town

Once you've reached a gateway town by car, there are generally two ways to continue into the villages:

- **By regional train.** This is the most common way visitors move between Monterosso, Vernazza, Corniglia, Manarola and Riomaggiore over the course of a day. It's a well-established, real piece of local infrastructure, though this site doesn't carry specific details on schedules, frequency or ticket pricing — those are worth checking closer to your travel dates since they can change.
- **On foot.** Sections of the coastal trail connecting the villages are walkable, and walking between adjacent villages is a popular way to see the cliffside scenery that makes the area famous. Trail conditions and sections open to walkers can vary, including occasional closures for maintenance or weather, so it's worth treating any specific trail as something to confirm locally rather than assuming full access.

Most visitors end up using some combination of both — training to the far end of their planned route and walking a scenic section or two along the way, rather than committing to one method for the entire day.

## Choosing the Right Gateway for Your Trip

For a day trip from Florence or Pisa, the gateway town you arrive at is really a function of which villages you most want to prioritize and how your return drive is arranged. There's no single "correct" gateway town for everyone — a private chauffeur or driver familiar with the area can help structure the route in a sensible order based on your specific priorities, even though this site doesn't publish a fixed drive time or distance for any of these routes.

For cruise passengers, the gateway is effectively decided for you — your day starts and ends at La Spezia, since that's where the ship is docked, and the train network handles the movement between villages during the hours you have ashore.

## A Note on Parking at the Gateway Towns

Travelers who are self-driving rather than using a private chauffeur should know that parking at gateway towns, particularly La Spezia during peak season, can be limited relative to demand. Day-trippers arriving by car from Florence, Pisa, or elsewhere in the region all converge on a relatively small number of parking areas near the train stations and port facilities, and arriving later in the morning can mean a longer search for a spot or a longer walk once parked. This is one of the more practical reasons some travelers prefer a private chauffeur for this leg specifically — being dropped directly at the gateway town avoids the parking search altogether, and the return pickup doesn't depend on remembering where you left the car or hoping a spot is still available at the end of a long day.

## Why the Gateway Concept Is Worth Understanding Even If You're Not Driving

Even travelers arriving entirely by train — say, coming from elsewhere in Italy on the national rail network rather than by road at all — benefit from understanding the gateway-town structure, since it explains why Cinque Terre's own regional line exists as a connector rather than a through-route from major cities. La Spezia and Levanto both sit on larger rail lines that connect to the rest of Italy, with the Cinque Terre regional service effectively branching off to link the five villages together. Knowing this helps make sense of why so many itineraries, whether arranged by private chauffeur, rental car, or train pass, end up routing through the same handful of towns regardless of how travelers start their day.

## Combining Gateway Towns With a Longer Itinerary

Some visitors use more than one gateway town in a single trip — arriving from the south via La Spezia, for instance, and continuing north to Levanto or beyond afterward rather than doubling back. This kind of routing is worth discussing directly with whoever arranges your transportation, since it changes where the vehicle needs to be at the end of the day compared to where it dropped you off in the morning. It's a reasonable way to see the area without retracing your steps, but it does require clearer planning upfront than a simple round trip from a single gateway.
