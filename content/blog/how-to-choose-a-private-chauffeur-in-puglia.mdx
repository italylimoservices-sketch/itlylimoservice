Choosing a private chauffeur for Puglia isn't quite the same decision as picking one for a single city visit. Puglia spreads its main draws — Bari, the trulli towns, Ostuni, Lecce, the coastline — across a region rather than concentrating them in one place, and the roads connecting them vary a fair amount: coastal stretches, inland countryside lanes, and narrow historic centers that weren't built with cars in mind. Getting the choice right means thinking through a few practical questions before you book, rather than treating every chauffeur booking as interchangeable.

## Start With the Shape of Your Trip

Before anything else, it helps to be clear on what kind of trip you're actually taking, because that shapes most of the other decisions:

- **A single arrival transfer** — landing at [Bari Airport](/airport-transfers) or docking at the cruise port, then heading straight to a hotel.
- **A multi-day touring itinerary** — moving between the trulli towns, the coast, and historic centers over several days.
- **A business trip** — a fixed schedule built around meetings, likely centered on Bari.
- **A wedding or event** — coordinating your own arrival alongside a group of guests, possibly through two different airports.

Being upfront about which of these you're planning, rather than booking a generic transfer and hoping it flexes to fit, makes the rest of the process go more smoothly.

## Vehicle Sizing for Multi-Stop Touring

Puglia trips often involve more stops than a typical city visit, and vehicle choice matters more here than it would for a quick point-to-point transfer, mainly because of luggage and the number of stops in a single day.

- **Solo travelers or couples** are usually well served by an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) — both handle up to three passengers and two suitcases comfortably.
- **Groups of four or five**, or smaller groups with extra luggage, tend to do better in a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), which seats up to five passengers with room for four suitcases.
- **Families or small groups of six or seven** generally need an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), both built for up to seven passengers and six suitcases.

If your itinerary mixes a few nights in Bari with day trips inland to the trulli towns and a stretch of coastal touring, it's worth mentioning the full plan at booking so the same vehicle and driver can be confirmed for the whole trip rather than switching partway through.

## Hourly vs. Point-to-Point: Which Fits Your Day

Chauffeur bookings generally come in two shapes, and picking the right one for each part of a Puglia trip matters.

**Point-to-point** works well for a single, defined journey — an airport or port transfer, or a transfer from one town to another with no stops planned along the way. It's simple and suits trips where the start and end points are fixed and known in advance.

**Hourly or full-day booking** makes more sense for a day with multiple stops, some flexibility in timing, or a plan that isn't fully fixed — a day touring Alberobello's trulli district with stops at a few points, or a coastal day where you might want to linger somewhere longer than planned. Because Puglia rewards this kind of flexible movement between small towns, a lot of touring days end up suited to an hourly arrangement rather than a single fixed transfer.

A multi-day Puglia trip often mixes both models: point-to-point for the airport legs at either end, and hourly or full-day bookings for the touring days in between. Working this out at the time of booking, rather than trying to convert a point-to-point transfer into a flexible day after the fact, saves back-and-forth later.

## Local Road Knowledge Matters More in Puglia Than It Looks

Puglia's roads aren't uniform, and this surprises some first-time visitors who expect a smaller region to be simpler to navigate than it turns out to be. Coastal routes near Ostuni or further along toward the southern tip of the heel behave differently from the inland roads winding through olive groves and trulli countryside, and historic town centers — Lecce's old town especially, but plenty of smaller towns too — often have narrow lanes with limited or restricted vehicle access.

A chauffeur who drives these roads regularly brings something a GPS alone doesn't: a sense of which inland roads turn into little more than a track between olive groves, where parking near a historic center is actually workable, and how to sequence a day's stops so you're not doubling back across the region. This matters more on a multi-stop Puglia itinerary than it would in a single compact city, simply because a poorly planned route can eat hours out of a day meant to include several different towns. That said, for confident drivers comfortable with unfamiliar roads and happy to navigate on their own, a rental car remains a reasonable way to see the region — the right choice really comes down to how much you value having someone else handle the driving and the route-planning.

## Understanding Booking Confirmations

When you book a chauffeur in Puglia, the initial confirmation usually covers the basics — pickup location, date and time, passenger count, luggage, and vehicle type. More specific details, such as which driver is assigned, tend to firm up closer to the travel date rather than at the moment of booking, so don't be surprised if a confirmation email doesn't spell out every detail right away.

For a multi-day or multi-stop itinerary, it helps to lay out the full plan at booking rather than booking each leg separately. An arrival transfer, a day of touring the trulli towns, a day along the coast, and a departure transfer can often be coordinated as one continuous arrangement rather than several disconnected bookings, which tends to make timing and vehicle continuity easier to manage.

If anything about your trip is non-standard — an early flight, a cruise ship schedule to track, a specific meeting point, a car seat, or a request to break a long day into shorter legs — flag it when you book. Confirming details in advance is far more reliable than assuming they'll be handled as a default.

## A Few Questions Worth Asking Before You Book

- Does the booking cover both Bari and a secondary gateway if your trip starts at one and ends at the other?
- Is the vehicle sized for your actual luggage, not just your passenger count?
- Is a multi-day itinerary being coordinated as one arrangement, or booked leg by leg?
- Are there any non-standard requests — a car seat, an early departure, a ship or flight schedule to track — that need confirming rather than assuming?

## Thinking About the Whole Trip, Not Just One Leg

It's easy to focus the booking decision entirely on the airport transfer, since that's usually the first thing arranged. But the bigger decision — and the one that matters more for how the trip actually feels — is whether the touring days in between get the same thought. A chauffeur booked only for arrival and departure, with the middle of the trip left to a rental car or ad hoc taxis, can end up feeling disjointed, especially once you're trying to connect a morning in Alberobello with an afternoon in Ostuni on roads you haven't driven before.

If budget is a factor, it's worth weighing a chauffeur for the parts of the trip where timing and comfort matter most — the airport legs, a wedding day, a day with several must-see stops — against a rental car for a looser, more spontaneous stretch later in the trip. This kind of mixed approach is common enough that it's worth raising directly when you book, rather than assuming the choice has to be all one or the other.
