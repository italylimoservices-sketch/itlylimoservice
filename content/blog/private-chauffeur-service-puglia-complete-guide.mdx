Puglia doesn't announce itself the way some Italian regions do. There's no single postcard view that sums up the "heel" of the Italian boot — instead there's Bari's old town and harbor, the conical-roofed trulli houses scattered through the countryside around Alberobello, the whitewashed lanes of Ostuni, the baroque stonework of Lecce, and a long coastline that alternates between rocky cliffs and quiet beaches. Getting a real sense of the region usually means covering ground between these places rather than settling into one of them, and that's where a private chauffeur service tends to make the most difference. This guide walks through the main pieces — arriving through Bari, touring the trulli towns and coast, and using a chauffeur for business or a wedding — so you have the full picture before planning a Puglia trip around it.

## Arriving Through Bari

Bari is Puglia's regional capital and, for most visitors, the natural entry point to the region. It works both as an airport gateway and as a cruise port, which means a private chauffeur arrangement can be built around either kind of arrival. Our [Bari destination page](/destinations/bari) covers this in more detail, including how airport and cruise transfers typically connect into onward touring.

A driver arranged in advance meets you at arrivals or at the port, handles the luggage, and gets the trip moving without a taxi queue or the hassle of finding a rental counter first. For a cruise arrival specifically, timing matters a bit more than it does for a flight — ships dock and disembark on their own schedule, and a chauffeur familiar with the port can adjust around that rather than treating it like a fixed flight time. Our [airport transfers](/airport-transfers) page has more general background on how arrival transfers are typically arranged if you're weighing options.

Some visitors fly into Bari and never leave the region by air again, looping back to the same airport at the end of the trip. Others use Bari for one leg of the journey and a different airport, such as Brindisi further south, for the other — Brindisi also serves parts of Puglia, particularly the southern end of the region, though we don't have a dedicated page for transfers through it on this site. Whichever shape your arrival takes, it's worth sharing it clearly when booking rather than assuming it will sort itself out.

## Touring the Trulli Towns, Coast and Countryside

Puglia's appeal is genuinely spread out, and that's both the charm and the challenge of visiting. Alberobello, a UNESCO World Heritage site known for its clusters of trulli — the distinctive conical-roofed stone houses unique to this part of Italy — sits inland from Bari, as does much of the surrounding trulli countryside. Ostuni, often called "the White City" for its whitewashed buildings climbing a hillside, sits closer to the coast. Lecce, known for its ornate baroque architecture, is further south still. Add in a coastline that runs from rocky inlets to long sandy stretches, and a single day rarely covers more than a slice of what the region offers.

A private chauffeur suits this kind of touring well, mainly because it turns a list of separate destinations into one continuous day (or several) rather than a series of disconnected transfers. The same driver and vehicle can take you from a morning in Alberobello's trulli district to an afternoon in Ostuni's old town and on to a coastal stop for the evening, without the group needing to manage parking, navigation, or the logistics of switching between taxis and trains along the way.

We don't have verified drive times or distances for specific legs between Puglia's towns on this site, so we won't attach a number to any particular route here — treat anything you see elsewhere as a rough estimate rather than a promise, since actual travel time depends heavily on the road, the season, and how much the smaller towns' narrow streets slow things down.

## Building a Multi-Day Itinerary

Most visitors touring Puglia by chauffeur are stringing together several days rather than booking a single transfer. A typical shape might include:

- An arrival through Bari, with a night or two based in the city itself
- A day trip inland to Alberobello and the surrounding trulli countryside
- A stop in Ostuni or along the coast
- A day further south toward Lecce and the baroque towns nearby
- A departure from the same gateway, or a transfer toward Brindisi if the trip ends in the south

Because Puglia mixes coastal roads, inland countryside lanes, and historic town centers with narrow, sometimes pedestrianized streets, it helps to build some slack into the plan rather than scheduling back-to-back stops too tightly. A chauffeur who regularly drives the region can suggest a sensible order for the day once your priorities are clear, even without a guaranteed timing for any specific leg.

## Weddings, Events and Group Travel

Puglia has become a genuinely popular setting for destination weddings, partly thanks to the trulli countryside and the masseria-style estates scattered through the region, and partly because of the coastline and historic towns nearby. Guest transportation for a Puglia wedding often means coordinating arrivals through two different airports — Bari in the north, Brindisi further south — depending on where the venue sits, and making sure everyone converges smoothly rather than arriving in a scattered, ad hoc way. ## Choosing the Right Vehicle

Vehicle choice in Puglia depends mostly on group size and how much luggage a multi-day tour involves. A couple or solo traveler moving between the airport, hotels and town centers is usually well matched to an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan), both suited to up to three passengers and two suitcases. A group of four or five, particularly on a touring itinerary with more bags than a single transfer would involve, tends to do better in a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), built for up to five passengers and four suitcases. Larger families or small groups generally need an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), both built for up to seven passengers with room for six suitcases — a common choice for multi-day Puglia itineraries where everyone travels together.

## What to Expect When Booking

Booking a Puglia chauffeur usually starts with the basics: which airport or port you're arriving through, group size, luggage, and a general sense of the itinerary — a single transfer, a multi-day tour, or a wedding weekend with several guest arrivals. More specific details, including which driver is assigned, typically firm up closer to the travel date. If there's something unusual to flag — a child seat, an odd pickup location, or a flight or ship schedule worth tracking — it's worth mentioning at booking rather than assuming it will be handled by default.
