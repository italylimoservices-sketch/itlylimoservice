Anyone who has wheeled a suitcase through Positano's center already knows the problem. The town is built in layers up a cliffside, connected by staircases rather than streets in many places, and a hard-shell case with bad wheels turns a short walk into a workout. **Traveling the Amalfi Coast with luggage** takes a bit more planning than most Italian destinations, not because the coast is unusually difficult, but because its geography genuinely limits where a vehicle can take you and your bags.

This isn't meant to discourage anyone from bringing what they need for a proper trip. It's a practical look at where luggage becomes a real logistical question on this coast, and how a private transfer changes the parts of that equation it can actually change.

## Why Positano Is the Town to Plan Around

Positano's layout is the main reason luggage comes up as its own topic on this coast. The town descends from the coastal road down to the beach in a series of terraces, linked by stepped lanes that are too narrow and too steep for vehicle access along most of that route. Many hotels sit partway down or at the very bottom, which means the last stretch of the journey to your room may be on foot, by stairs, regardless of how you arrive at the town's edge.

Some hotels have porter service to help with this last stretch; others don't, or charge for it separately. This is genuinely worth confirming directly with your hotel before you travel, since it affects both what you should pack and how you should pack it. A single large case per person is far more manageable on stairs than a mountain of smaller bags, and a soft-sided duffel is often easier to carry up steps than a hard case with wheels that are only useful on flat ground.

Amalfi and Ravello are comparatively easier. Amalfi's center is flatter near the main piazza, with vehicle access closer to many hotels than Positano typically offers, though it still narrows into pedestrian lanes further in. Ravello sits at the top of its own climb no matter how you approach it, so a short uphill stretch with luggage is close to unavoidable there.

## What a Private Transfer Actually Solves

A private chauffeur gets you and your luggage to the point closest a vehicle can reasonably reach — often the edge of a pedestrian zone, a hotel's nearest drivable access point, or a designated drop-off area in a town center. It doesn't carry your bags up a flight of stairs to a hotel that sits above where cars can go, but it removes several harder problems that come before that final stretch.

The biggest one is simply not having to manage bags through the process of finding and negotiating a taxi, or standing at a bus stop with a full set of luggage while a crowded SITA bus decides whether there's room for it. The coastal buses connecting the towns can get genuinely full in peak season, and a stroller or several large cases in that kind of crowd is its own daily stress. A private vehicle means your luggage travels with you directly, loaded once at pickup and unloaded once at your destination's nearest access point, without a public transit connection in between.

The other advantage is that the vehicle can wait, or return, on your schedule rather than a fixed timetable. If you're doing a same-day excursion and don't want to drag full luggage along for a day trip, a private transfer built around your actual plans — pack light for the day, keep the rest at the hotel — is simpler to arrange than trying to fit that flexibility around public transport departures.

## Choosing a Vehicle With Luggage in Mind

Vehicle capacity is worth thinking about honestly, since the passenger count alone doesn't tell the whole story once luggage is factored in. An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits a solo traveler or couple with a reasonable amount of luggage, but fills up quickly if you're checking in with more than the trip's headcount would suggest. A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) comfortably handles up to five passengers and four suitcases, which is usually the right call for a small family or a couple who packed for more than a long weekend. For a larger group or a family bringing car seats, strollers, and extra bags on top of standard luggage, an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), each seating up to seven passengers with room for six suitcases, avoids the situation where someone's holding a bag on their lap for a winding coastal drive.

The habit that actually matters here is giving an honest luggage count when you request a quote — not just how many people are traveling, but roughly how many bags and what size. That's what determines whether the vehicle suggested to you genuinely fits, rather than being a tight squeeze once everything is loaded.

## Packing Choices That Make the Coast Easier

A few practical adjustments make a real difference on this coast specifically:

- **Favor one larger bag over several smaller ones per person.** Fewer individual pieces are easier to manage on stairs and easier to load into a vehicle without a puzzle.
- **Choose soft-sided luggage or a duffel where possible**, especially if your hotel sits down one of Positano's stepped lanes. Hard-shell wheeled cases are excellent on flat ground and considerably less so on stairs.
- **Pack a smaller day bag separately** for excursions, so you're not carrying (or storing) your full luggage for a single day trip to another town.
- **Confirm porter or luggage assistance with your hotel in advance**, particularly in Positano, rather than assuming it's included or available on arrival.

## Arrival Day Specifically

Landing at Naples International Airport after a long flight with a full set of luggage is exactly the kind of moment a pre-arranged private transfer is built for. A driver waiting with your name means you're not managing bags through a taxi line or trying to fit an oversized case into a shared shuttle with other passengers' luggage already loaded. The drive out to the coast itself follows the coastal road's many curves — there's no fixed, verified duration to quote here, since it depends on your destination town, traffic, and season, but it's realistically a half-day commitment worth planning your arrival schedule around rather than a short hop.

## When You're Traveling Onward

If your trip continues from the coast to Naples, Rome, or another region, the same logic applies in reverse on departure day. A private transfer timed to your flight or train, rather than a fixed shuttle schedule, gives you control over when you leave relative to how much luggage-wrangling the morning requires — useful if your hotel is somewhere that needs an early start just to move bags down to a drivable pickup point.

## The Bottom Line

Luggage on the Amalfi Coast is less about how much you bring and more about matching your packing and your transportation to the coast's actual geography — steep, stepped towns that a vehicle can only reach so far into. A private chauffeur handles the parts of that equation a car can solve: the airport run, the connections between towns, and getting your bags as close to your hotel's door as the terrain allows.
