Sardinia doesn't have a single gateway the way some Italian regions do. Depending on where you're headed, you'll land at one of three separate airports — Cagliari, Olbia, or Alghero — each serving a different part of an island that's considerably larger and more spread out than first-time visitors tend to expect. Picking the right one, and knowing what arrival day actually involves, makes a real difference to how smoothly your trip starts.

## Three Airports, Three Different Islands (Almost)

It helps to think of Sardinia less as one destination and more as three loosely connected regions, each best reached through its own airport.

**Cagliari Airport** sits in the south and serves the island's capital city along with the surrounding south coast. If your trip centers on Cagliari itself, or you're using the south as a base for exploring nearby coastline and countryside, this is almost certainly your arrival point.

**Olbia Airport** — officially Olbia Costa Smeralda Airport — is the main gateway to the northeast. This is the airport most travelers heading to the Costa Smeralda, Porto Cervo, and the glamorous stretch of coast in that corner of the island will use. Given how much of Sardinia's higher-end tourism concentrates in this region, Olbia sees a steady flow of visitors heading straight for the coast rather than lingering near the airport itself.

**Alghero Airport** covers the northwest, putting travelers within reach of Alghero's old town and the coastline running along that side of the island. It's a smaller, quieter entry point than the other two, but it's the sensible choice if northwestern Sardinia is your focus.

The important thing to understand is that these three airports are not close together and not realistic alternates for each other. Choosing the wrong one because a flight happened to be cheaper can add a long, unplanned drive across the island to your itinerary — so it's worth confirming which airport actually matches your destination before you book flights, not after.

## Matching the Airport to Your Trip

A simple way to think about it:

- Staying in or near Cagliari, or exploring the south coast → **Cagliari Airport**
- Heading to the Costa Smeralda, Porto Cervo, or northeastern Sardinia → **Olbia Airport**
- Visiting Alghero or the northwest → **Alghero Airport**

If your trip spans more than one region — say, a few days near Cagliari followed by time on the Costa Smeralda — it's worth deciding upfront whether you'll fly into one airport and out of another, or build in a longer private transfer between regions partway through your stay. Either approach can work, but it's a decision best made while planning rather than discovered mid-trip.

## What Arrival Day Looks Like, Wherever You Land

Regardless of which of the three airports you use, the sequence of events after your plane touches down follows a familiar pattern: deplaning, clearing passport control if you've arrived from outside the Schengen area, collecting any checked luggage, and then making your way out to arrivals. How long that takes varies a lot — sometimes it's quick, sometimes a terminal is handling several arrivals close together and the whole process stretches out. It's sensible to build a reasonable buffer into any plans for the rest of arrival day, particularly if you have a hotel check-in time, a dinner reservation, or anything else time-sensitive lined up.

If you're unsure how much buffer to allow for your specific route, the most reliable answer will come directly from whoever is arranging your transfer, since they'll have current, route-specific knowledge rather than a generic estimate.

It's also worth thinking about the order of events on travel days with a connecting flight. A delay on an earlier leg can shift your real arrival time in Sardinia even if your ticket shows something fixed, so flagging a connection at booking means your transfer can adjust rather than leaving you stranded or, just as inconveniently, having a driver wait around for hours longer than necessary.

## Same-Day Onward Travel

Some visitors use one Sardinian airport as an entry point and then need to reach a destination served by a different region entirely — for example, flying into Cagliari but continuing toward the northeast later in the trip, or vice versa. Because the island is larger than its compact outline on a map suggests, this kind of cross-island travel is a genuine undertaking, not a short hop. If your itinerary includes a move like this, it's best treated as its own leg of the trip, planned and booked separately from your airport arrival, rather than squeezed into the same day as a long-haul flight.

## Flight Tracking Matters More in Summer

Sardinia's airports — especially Olbia — see a significant jump in traffic during the summer months, when flights arrive from across Europe in clusters. A private transfer arranged with your actual flight number allows the pickup to track your real landing time rather than a fixed clock time, which matters more during busy periods when delays are more common and terminals are more crowded. It's worth sharing your flight number, approximate passenger count, and luggage volume at booking regardless of which airport you're using, so your driver can plan accordingly.

## Luggage and Vehicle Choice

Arrival day is typically when you're carrying the most luggage of the whole trip — suitcases for the full stay, plus anything bulky like beach gear or, depending on the season, golf equipment. A private driver meets you in the arrivals area and handles loading everything into the car, which takes a genuinely tiring part of travel off your plate.

Matching the vehicle to your group and luggage matters, particularly in summer when bags tend to multiply. An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits couples or solo travelers, comfortably handling 3 passengers and 2 suitcases. Families or anyone with more luggage should look at a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), which fits up to 5 passengers and 4 suitcases. Larger groups can book an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), each seating up to 7 passengers with 6 suitcases. If you're unsure which size you need, mentioning your group size and luggage at booking lets the right vehicle be arranged in advance rather than sorted out awkwardly at the curb.
