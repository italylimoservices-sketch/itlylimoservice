Most Italian towns let you drive right up to where you're staying, hand your bags to someone, and forget about them until checkout. Portofino doesn't quite work that way, and if you're arriving with luggage — whether for a few hours or a few nights — it's worth understanding why before you pack.

## Why Luggage Is a Different Question in Portofino

The village's harbor and the lanes immediately around it are largely pedestrian, with narrow, often stepped streets that weren't built with vehicles in mind. Cars reach the edge of the village, not the harbor itself, which means the last stretch into the center — and into most hotels there — happens on foot. For a day visitor carrying a light bag, that's barely worth noticing. For anyone arriving with a suitcase, or several, it changes the calculation.

## What This Means in Practice

A few practical consequences follow directly from Portofino's restricted vehicle access:

- Wheeled hard-shell cases can be awkward over cobblestones and steps — they roll fine on flat pavement but less gracefully on an uneven lane with a slope
- There's no guarantee a vehicle can stop directly outside your hotel's entrance; the walk from the vehicle access point varies by where you're staying
- Porters and hotel staff in the village are generally used to this and can often help, but it's sensible to plan as though you may be carrying your own bags for part of the way
- A large group with a lot of luggage will take noticeably longer to move from a vehicle to a hotel than the same group with day bags only

None of this is unique to Portofino — plenty of historic Italian centers have similar restrictions — but the scale of Portofino's pedestrian zone and the length of the walk-in make it worth specific attention rather than an afterthought.

## Packing Choices That Help

If you know in advance that you'll be walking the last stretch with your bags, a few adjustments make a real difference:

- A soft-sided duffel or a backpack-style bag is generally easier to manage over uneven ground than a rigid case
- Packing a smaller separate bag for just an overnight or a day trip, rather than carrying everything from a larger suitcase, reduces what needs to be hauled on foot
- If you're checking into a hotel for several nights, your main luggage matters less in the moment — the walk happens once — but it's still worth packing with the walk in mind rather than assuming a vehicle will get you door to door

## If You're Changing Hotels Mid-Trip

Travelers moving between accommodations on the Italian Riviera — leaving a hotel in Santa Margherita Ligure or Rapallo for a stay in Portofino itself, for instance — face a slightly different version of the same issue. The vehicle can handle the transfer between towns without any trouble; it's only the final stretch into Portofino's pedestrian core that requires walking bags in. If you know which hotel you're headed to, it's worth asking them directly, before you travel, roughly how far their entrance is from the nearest point a vehicle can reach. Hotels inside the village are used to this question and can usually give a straightforward answer.

## Weather and Luggage

Rain changes the luggage equation more than most travelers expect. Wet cobblestones are harder on wheeled cases than dry ones, and carrying bags any distance in the rain is simply less pleasant for everyone involved. If the forecast looks unsettled, it's worth packing with slightly more tolerance for carrying bags by hand rather than rolling them, and keeping anything that can't get wet — documents, electronics — in a separate bag you're carrying yourself rather than buried in a suitcase.

## Day Trip vs. Overnight Stay

The luggage question looks different depending on why you're in Portofino.

**Day visitors** usually just need a bag with essentials — water, a layer for the evening, maybe a camera — and the restricted access barely registers. The practical issue is smaller and easy to plan around.

**Overnight guests** have more at stake. If you're staying at a hotel in the village itself, confirm with your driver and, ideally, the hotel ahead of time roughly how far the walk-in is from the vehicle access point, so you're not caught off guard with a full suitcase and no plan for the last stretch.

## Coordinating the Drop-off With Your Driver

This is where a private chauffeur earns their place in the logistics, even though they can't drive into the pedestrian zone any more than anyone else can. A driver familiar with Portofino can set a sensible drop-off point, help with bags right up to where the vehicle can go, and be clear with you in advance about where that handoff point is — rather than you discovering it on arrival.

When you submit a booking request, mention your luggage specifically: how many bags, roughly what size, and whether you're arriving for a day or staying overnight. ## Choosing the Right Vehicle for Your Luggage

Vehicle choice matters more than it might seem once luggage is factored in properly. A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) comfortably handles up to five passengers and four suitcases, which covers most couples and small families traveling with a normal amount of luggage. For larger groups, or anyone with more bags than passengers would suggest — extra bags for a longer trip, golf or sports equipment, gifts picked up along the way — an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) offers room for up to seven passengers and six suitcases, so nobody's bag ends up wedged against their knees for the drive.

## A Quick Pre-Trip Checklist

Before heading to Portofino with any real amount of luggage, it's worth running through a short list:

- Confirm with your hotel, if you're staying overnight, roughly how far the walk-in is from the nearest vehicle access point
- Favor soft-sided bags over rigid cases where you have a choice
- Pack a separate smaller bag for the day itself if your main luggage is substantial
- Mention your bag count and trip type when requesting a quote, so the vehicle and the plan both match reality
- Build a little extra time into the schedule for the walk, particularly with a larger group or heavier bags

## The Short Version

Portofino's charm is tied directly to the fact that it hasn't been paved over for cars, and that's exactly why luggage deserves a little more thought here than in most Italian towns. Pack with the walk-in in mind, be upfront about your bag count when booking, and let your driver handle the part of the logistics that's actually within their control — getting you, and your bags, to the right spot at the right time.
