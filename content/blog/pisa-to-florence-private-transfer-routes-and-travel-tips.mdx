After a few days on the Tuscan coast, Florence is the natural next stop for a lot of itineraries — and the trip there from Pisa is short enough that it barely interrupts the day. This is the reverse direction of the more commonly searched Florence-to-Pisa route: practical notes for travelers based in or around Pisa who are continuing on to Florence next, covering the route itself and a few things worth knowing before you go.

## The Route Itself

The drive from Pisa to Florence covers approximately 85 km and takes around an hour under normal conditions. It runs east across Tuscany, away from the coast and into the Arno valley toward Florence, and it's a consistently predictable route compared to some of the region's longer transfers — there's simply less distance for traffic or road conditions to meaningfully affect your arrival time. If you're coming from Pisa Airport specifically rather than the city center, note that the airport-to-Florence figure runs closer to 80 minutes, slightly different from the city-to-city distance.

## Starting Points Around Pisa

Where exactly you're starting from in the Pisa area shapes the pickup more than the drive itself does:

- **Central Pisa hotels**, particularly near Piazza dei Miracoli or the train station, are straightforward pickups with good vehicle access.
- **Pisa Airport**, if you're arriving and continuing straight on to Florence without stopping in the city, is covered in more detail in the [Pisa Airport to Florence guide](/blog/pisa-airport-to-florence-complete-private-transfer-guide) — the logistics differ slightly from a city-center pickup.
- **The wider coastal area**, if you've been staying somewhere near Pisa as a base for exploring the Tuscan coast, may add a little time to the pickup depending on exactly where you are, which is worth mentioning when you book.

## A Stop at Piazza dei Miracoli Before You Go

If you're leaving from somewhere in or near Pisa, it's worth considering whether you've actually seen the Leaning Tower and Piazza dei Miracoli yet. Plenty of travelers pass through Pisa en route to somewhere else without stopping, and if that describes your trip, this transfer is a reasonable opportunity to fit it in before continuing on, rather than leaving Tuscany without seeing one of its most recognizable sights.

This needs to be planned into the booking rather than decided at pickup — allow at least an hour at the square if you want to actually go inside the cathedral or baptistery rather than just see the tower from outside. If your plan for the day in Florence is tightly scheduled, it's fine to skip this and treat the drive as a direct hour-long transfer instead.

## What to Expect on Arrival in Florence

Florence's historic center operates a restricted traffic zone (ZTL), which affects where a vehicle can actually stop near hotels inside the old city. This isn't something to worry about — a driver familiar with Florence will know the practical drop-off points for most central addresses — but it's worth sharing your exact accommodation address when booking so the approach is planned rather than figured out on the day.

If your hotel is outside the historic core, in areas like Campo di Marte or along the river further from the center, access is generally simpler and more direct.

## Luggage and Group Considerations

Travelers making this trip after several days on the coast often have more luggage than they arrived with — a common pattern for anyone who's done some shopping or is carrying beach gear along with everything else. It's worth mentioning your actual luggage count when booking rather than assuming a standard allowance, so the right vehicle is arranged from the start.

- **Solo travelers or couples** with standard luggage are well served by a sedan.
- **Families or travelers with extra bags** usually do better in something with more cargo space.
- **Groups traveling together** benefit from staying in one vehicle rather than splitting across cars for what is, after all, a fairly short drive.

## Timing Considerations Beyond the Hour on the Road

Because the drive itself is short and predictable, the more useful planning question is usually what's happening on either end of it:

- **If you're catching a later arrival in Florence** with no fixed plans, timing is flexible and there's little to worry about beyond general traffic patterns.
- **If you have a specific Florence activity booked** — a museum entry, a scheduled tour, a dinner reservation — build in a reasonable buffer beyond the hour drive, particularly if you're also planning a stop at Piazza dei Miracoli first.
- **If you're arriving from the airport rather than central Pisa**, allow extra time for the arrival process itself — passport control and baggage claim — before the drive even starts.

## Why a Private Transfer Suits This Direction

The train between Pisa and Florence is a solid, well-established option for this route, and plenty of travelers use it without issue. A private transfer makes more sense on this leg specifically when you're carrying more luggage than you arrived with, traveling as a group or family, want the flexibility to add a stop at Piazza dei Miracoli without worrying about a train schedule, or simply prefer being picked up directly from your accommodation rather than making your way to a station with bags in tow. For a broader comparison of the two options on this corridor, see [train or private chauffeur](/blog/florence-to-pisa-private-transfer-train-vs-chauffeur).

## Choosing the Right Vehicle

An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) comfortably covers up to 3 passengers with 2 suitcases, suiting most solo travelers and couples. A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) seats up to 5 passengers with 4 suitcases, a better fit for families or anyone who's accumulated extra luggage along the way. Larger groups can travel together in a [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) rather than splitting into separate cars.

## Leaving the Coast Behind

For travelers who've spent several days exploring the Tuscan coast — whether based in Pisa itself or using it as a jumping-off point for the surrounding area — this drive marks a clear shift in the trip. The road east toward Florence moves away from the coastal flatlands and into the greener Arno valley, a reasonably scenic hour that works well as a transition between the two very different characters of coastal Tuscany and inland Florence.

## If You're Combining This With Other Stops

Some travelers use the Pisa-to-Florence leg as an opportunity to add a brief stop beyond Piazza dei Miracoli, depending on the exact route and timing. This isn't something to overcomplicate — the appeal of this transfer is its simplicity — but if there's a specific small town or viewpoint along the way that interests you, it's worth raising when you book rather than assuming it can be added spontaneously. A private transfer offers that flexibility; a fixed train schedule does not.

## Returning the Favor: Florence to Pisa Later in Your Trip

If your itinerary brings you back through Pisa later — say, for a flight home from PSA — it's worth knowing that the return version of this trip is covered from the opposite direction in the [Florence to Pisa transfer guide](/blog/florence-to-pisa-private-transfer-guide), which focuses on timing a transfer around a departing flight. Keeping both directions in mind when planning a longer Tuscany itinerary can simplify booking, since the same driver or company can often be arranged for both legs.

## A Note on Seasonal Timing

Tuscany's roads and both cities themselves see noticeably more visitors in the peak summer months, which can add modest time to any drive, including this one. It rarely changes the fundamental hour-long nature of the trip, but booking a little further ahead during peak season — and allowing a slightly more generous buffer if you have a fixed commitment in Florence — is a reasonable precaution rather than a necessity.

## Final Thoughts

The trip from Pisa to Florence is short, direct, and rarely the hard part of this leg of a Tuscany itinerary — the real planning points are where exactly you're starting from, whether a Piazza dei Miracoli stop makes sense before you go, and what's waiting for you on arrival in Florence. Sort out your pickup location, luggage count, and any stop you'd like along the way when you book, and the hour itself takes care of itself.
