Booking a private chauffeur in Genoa isn't complicated, but knowing what information actually speeds up the process helps — both for getting an accurate quote and for making sure the right vehicle and driver show up at the right place. This guide walks through what a booking request typically needs, how the quote-to-confirmation process generally works, and what's worth clarifying before the day of travel.

## Starting the Request

Most chauffeur bookings, in Genoa or elsewhere, start with a quote request rather than an instant purchase, since pricing depends on the specifics of the trip. A typical request form asks for:

- **Pickup location** — a specific address, the cruise terminal, or the airport, rather than just "Genoa."
- **Destination** — same level of detail, since "somewhere in the old town" is harder to plan around than a named address.
- **Date and time** — the actual pickup time, not a rough window.
- **Number of passengers** — exact count, since it determines which vehicle fits.
- **Vehicle type** — if you already know which category you need, or you can leave this open for a recommendation.
- **Trip type** — one-way or round trip, since a round trip needs a return date and time as well.
- **Special requirements** — anything that doesn't fit the standard fields: a child seat, extra luggage, a wheelchair-accessible vehicle, or a note about a flight or cruise ship you're connecting from.

Filling these in as specifically as possible — a real address instead of a neighborhood, an exact passenger count instead of "a few of us" — tends to produce a faster and more accurate quote than a vague request does.

## Genoa-Specific Details Worth Including

A few pieces of information matter more for Genoa bookings than they might for a simpler transfer elsewhere:

- **If you're arriving by cruise ship**, note the ship name and expected disembarkation time if you have it, since port pickups are coordinated around the terminal's logistics rather than a fixed clock time. Our [cruise port transfers](/cruise-port-transfers) page has more on how this works.
- **If you're arriving by air**, share your flight number. Genoa Airport (Cristoforo Colombo, IATA code GOA) is a smaller airport without a dedicated transfer page on this site, but a pickup there is handled through our general [airport transfers](/airport-transfers) booking, and the flight number lets the service track your actual arrival time.
- **If your destination is deep in the historic center**, mention it — Genoa's old town has narrow lanes that aren't all vehicle-accessible, and knowing this in advance lets the chauffeur plan the most practical drop-off point rather than one that turns out to be unreachable by car.
- **If the trip includes a day out to the coast**, say so at booking. A trip to [Portofino](/destinations/portofino) or the [Cinque Terre](/destinations/cinque-terre) is a different kind of booking from a city transfer, usually involving a longer block of the driver's time.

## One-Way vs. Round Trip

The form distinguishes between a one-way transfer and a round trip, and it's worth picking deliberately rather than defaulting to whichever seems simpler. A one-way booking covers a single leg — airport to hotel, for instance — with its own date and time. A round trip adds a return date and time, which matters if you know both ends of the trip already (say, a cruise port pickup followed by a drop-off back at the terminal before the ship departs). If you're not sure yet whether you'll need a return leg, it's generally fine to book the first leg as one-way and arrange the return separately once your plans firm up.

## What Happens After You Submit a Request

After a request comes in, the usual next step is a quote based on the specifics provided — vehicle type, distance, and any special requirements. This is where providing accurate details upfront pays off: a request with a vague pickup location or an uncertain passenger count often needs a follow-up question before a firm quote can be given, which adds a delay that's easy to avoid.

Once a quote is accepted, the booking moves toward confirmation, which typically includes the finalized pickup details and, closer to the travel date, the assigned driver and vehicle information. Exact timing for when driver details are shared varies, so if you need that confirmation by a specific point before travel, it's reasonable to ask directly rather than assume a default timeline applies to your situation.

## Providing Contact Information

Alongside the trip details, a booking request asks for your name and a way to reach you — a phone number or email, typically. This matters more than it might seem: a chauffeur service needs a reliable way to reach you if a flight is delayed, a ship arrives early, or a pickup location needs to be confirmed on the day. Double-checking that the contact information you provide is one you'll actually be reachable at during the trip — not a number that only works at home — avoids a problem that's hard to fix once you're already traveling.

## Special Requirements Worth Flagging Early

The special requirements field is easy to skip if nothing obviously applies, but it's worth using deliberately for anything outside a standard transfer:

- A child or infant car seat.
- Extra luggage beyond what the vehicle category typically allows — relevant for cruise passengers with a full set of bags.
- A multi-stop day rather than a single transfer, if you know the full schedule already.
- Any accessibility need that affects vehicle choice.

Flagging these at booking, rather than mentioning them to the driver on arrival, gives the chauffeur service time to actually arrange for them rather than improvise on the day.

## Choosing the Right Vehicle at Booking

If you're unsure which vehicle category fits your trip, it generally helps to describe the group and luggage rather than guess at a category name. As a general guide, an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits up to three passengers and two suitcases, a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) suits up to five passengers and four suitcases, and an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) suits up to seven passengers with six suitcases.
