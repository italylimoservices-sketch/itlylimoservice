Leaving Portofino for Milan is, in most ways, the Milan-to-Portofino trip run backward — the same long cross-region drive, the same coastal roads near the village giving way to faster inland routes. But departure day has its own set of practical concerns that arrival day doesn't, particularly if you're heading to catch a flight rather than simply checking into a Milan hotel.

## The Same Drive, Different Pressures

There's no verified, published drive time for the Portofino-to-Milan route on this site, and that doesn't change depending on which direction you're traveling. It's a genuinely long transfer, crossing from the Ligurian coast back into Lombardy, and actual time on the road depends on traffic, time of day, and conditions on the narrower coastal roads near Portofino before you reach faster roads further inland. What does change on the return leg is the kind of planning it calls for: on arrival day, running a bit later than expected is an inconvenience; on departure day, especially with a flight to catch, it can matter a lot more.

## Getting Out of Portofino Itself

Before the long drive even begins, there's the matter of getting your car and bags out of Portofino in the first place. The village's harbor area has restricted vehicle access, so if you're staying in the old center near the waterfront, your departure will likely start with a short walk from your hotel to where your driver can actually pick you up. This is the same restriction that applies to arrivals, just in reverse, and it's worth allowing a few extra minutes for it rather than assuming the car will be waiting right outside your door. A driver familiar with the village will know the most practical pickup point for your specific hotel.

## Planning Around an Onward Flight

If your trip continues from Milan, the most important thing to get right is the buffer between your estimated arrival in Milan and your flight's departure time. Because this is a long drive with real variability — coastal roads near Portofino, then a longer inland stretch, then potentially city traffic approaching the airport — it's not a route to plan tightly around a specific flight time. A reasonable approach is to treat the drive itself as the baseline and then add a generous margin on top for the airport's own check-in and security process, rather than calculating a single tight window and hoping the day goes exactly to plan.

Whether you're flying out of [Milan Malpensa](/airport-transfers/milan-malpensa) or [Milan Linate](/airport-transfers/milan-linate) also matters here, since the two airports sit in different parts of the Milan area and the final stretch of your drive will differ depending on which one you're headed to. Sharing your flight details at booking means your driver can plan the departure time from Portofino with your actual flight in mind, working backward from when you need to be at the airport rather than a vague sense of "morning" or "afternoon."

## If You're Heading Into Milan Itself

Not every return trip ends at an airport. If you're continuing your Italy trip with time in Milan itself — business meetings, a hotel stay, or simply the next leg of a longer itinerary — the pressure is naturally lower, since you're not working against a flight departure. Even so, it's worth sharing your hotel address and any fixed commitments (a dinner reservation, a meeting time) when you book, so the drive is planned with enough buffer to arrive relaxed rather than rushed.

## Luggage on the Way Out

By departure day, you may be carrying more than you arrived with — anything picked up during your stay on the Riviera, alongside your original luggage. It's worth mentioning any change in luggage volume when you book your return transfer, particularly if you've added bags since arrival, so the vehicle arranged still fits comfortably.

## Choosing a Vehicle for the Return Drive

As with the outbound trip, vehicle comfort matters more on a long drive like this than it would on a short city hop. An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits couples or solo travelers well, seating 3 passengers with 2 suitcases for an extended ride. Families or anyone with more luggage should consider a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), fitting up to 5 passengers and 4 suitcases with noticeably more space. Larger groups can book an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), each seating up to 7 passengers with 6 suitcases — useful if your group is traveling together back to Milan for a shared onward flight or train.

## Returning During Busier Periods

If your stay in Portofino falls during the peak summer season, it's worth factoring that into your departure planning specifically. The Riviera's busiest months tend to bring heavier traffic on the coastal roads leading out of the village, on top of the normal variability of a long drive. Travelers departing on a Saturday or Sunday during peak season, in particular, may find the roads busier than a weekday departure in the shoulder months of spring or autumn. None of this means the trip is unpredictable — it simply means the margin you build in around a flight or evening commitment is worth treating as more important during high season than it might be at quieter times of year.

## What the Drive Looks Like

Leaving Portofino, the route initially follows the same narrow, winding coastal roads that characterize arrival into the village, before gradually opening onto faster roads as you move inland toward Milan. It's worth treating that first stretch as slower-paced almost by nature of the roads themselves, rather than a sign that something's wrong with the timing. Once you're further from the coast, the drive typically settles into a more predictable pace, though as with any road trip of this length, traffic around Milan itself — especially during weekday rush periods — can add time as you approach the city.

## Timing Your Departure From Portofino

If you're working backward from a flight or an evening commitment in Milan, it helps to build your departure time around the least predictable parts of the journey rather than the most predictable ones. The coastal stretch near Portofino and the approach into Milan are the two segments most likely to vary with traffic and time of day; the middle, inland portion of the drive tends to be more consistent. Discussing your schedule with your driver in advance means the departure time from your hotel is set with that variability already factored in, rather than discovered on the day.
