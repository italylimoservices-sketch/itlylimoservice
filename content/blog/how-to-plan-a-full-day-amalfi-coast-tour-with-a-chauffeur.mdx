A full day gives you enough time to see the Amalfi Coast properly, but it still needs real structure to work. The road between Positano, Amalfi and Ravello is narrow and its travel times genuinely unpredictable, which means a full day here benefits from being planned in phases — a morning stop, a deliberate midday pause, and an afternoon stop — rather than a loose plan to "see the coast" and hope it all fits. This guide walks through how to structure that kind of day from start to finish.

## Before the Day Starts: Setting Expectations

The single most important planning decision for a full Amalfi Coast day is accepting that you're seeing two, maybe three towns, not conquering the entire coast at leisure. The road's narrow curves and variable traffic mean that time spent driving is real time, not a gap to be filled loosely — treating the day as a framework with built-in flexibility, rather than a fixed timetable, is what keeps it from falling apart if one stretch of road runs long. It's also worth discussing your rough priorities with your chauffeur before setting off, so the day can be adjusted sensibly if something runs ahead of or behind schedule.

## Morning Phase: An Early Start at the First Town

Starting early matters more on the Amalfi Coast than in most Italian destinations, partly because towns and roads both get busier as the day goes on, and partly because a full day genuinely needs the extra hours a morning start provides. A common structure begins with a drop-off at Positano, letting you take in the town's staircased lanes and beach view while the light and crowds are both more manageable. Because Positano's vertical layout takes real time to move through, give this stop a generous window rather than a quick hour — budget for the walk down toward the water and the climb back up, or arrange pickup from a different point to avoid retracing your steps.

## Midday Phase: The Drive to Amalfi and a Proper Break

Late morning into early afternoon is a sensible time to move on toward Amalfi town, both because it puts you there around a natural lunch window and because Amalfi's more level, walkable center is a comfortable place to slow down after a morning of Positano's staircases. This is the point in the day worth protecting rather than rushing — a real lunch break, not a rushed sandwich between stops, tends to make the rest of the afternoon noticeably more enjoyable. Amalfi's cathedral and main piazza are worth a proper visit here, along with a slower walk through the surrounding lanes and harbor area once you've eaten.

## Early Afternoon: Deciding on Ravello

By early-to-mid afternoon, you'll have a reasonable sense of how the day is running relative to your original plan, which makes this the natural decision point for whether to continue on to Ravello. Ravello sits up and away from the coastal road, and reaching it involves a genuine climb rather than a continuation of the same route — worth factoring in if the morning and midday phases ran later than expected. If the day is on schedule, Ravello's clifftop gardens and quieter atmosphere make a rewarding final stop. If the morning ran long, it's worth being honest about whether pushing on to Ravello will make for a rushed, unsatisfying visit rather than a relaxed one.

## Late Afternoon: Ravello's Gardens at a Slower Pace

Assuming the day allows for it, Ravello works best as an unhurried close rather than another stop to rush through. Its gardens and villas reward simply sitting for a while and taking in the view rather than moving briskly from point to point, and the quieter atmosphere here — a real contrast to Positano's crowds and Amalfi's midday bustle — makes it a natural place to slow the pace down as the day winds toward evening. Give yourself enough time here to actually enjoy it rather than treating it as a box to check before heading back.

## Alternative Afternoon: A Second Look at Amalfi or Positano

Not every full day needs to include Ravello's climb. Visitors whose morning ran long, or who'd simply rather spend more time at sea level, can instead use the afternoon for a slower, more thorough look at whichever of Positano or Amalfi they found more compelling in the morning — revisiting a part of town rushed the first time, or simply finding a spot to sit and watch the coast for a while. This version of the afternoon suits travelers who prefer less driving overall, and it's just as valid a way to close out a full day as the push up to Ravello.

## Evening: Winding Down

However the afternoon plays out, it's worth building in a reasonable buffer before any evening plans — dinner reservations, an onward transfer, or simply the return to your accommodation. Because travel times on this coast are never fully predictable, treating the end of the day with the same flexibility as the rest of it, rather than cutting things close, avoids turning a relaxed day into a stressful finish.

## Where a Chauffeur Fits Into a Full Day

A full day with this many phases and this much uncertainty in travel time is much better suited to a flexible, [hourly chauffeur arrangement](/chauffeur-service) than a series of fixed point-to-point bookings. A driver who can adjust to whichever direction the day actually takes — whether that means cutting the Ravello leg, extending lunch in Amalfi, or starting the drive back earlier than planned — removes a significant amount of the planning pressure from a day with this many moving parts. It also means someone experienced with the coast's narrow curves is handling the driving throughout, rather than you managing both the road and the schedule yourself.

## Comparing to a Focused Single-Town Visit

If, reading through this, a full day covering two or three towns sounds like more than you want to take on, it's worth considering a shorter alternative. Our guide to [planning a half-day Amalfi Coast tour](/blog/how-to-plan-a-half-day-amalfi-coast-tour-with-a-private-driver) makes the case for focusing on a single town instead, which suits travelers who'd rather go deeper in one place than cover more ground across a full day.

## Choosing a Vehicle for a Full Day

A full day on the coast means extended time in the car between stops, so comfort matters over the course of the day in a way it might not for a shorter trip. A [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits a couple well, while families or small groups benefit from the extra room of a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), particularly if the day includes luggage from an onward journey. Whichever vehicle you choose, this is a route where the driver's experience with the road matters just as much as the vehicle itself.

## Final Thoughts

A full-day Amalfi Coast tour works best as a structured sequence of phases rather than a single continuous plan: an early morning start in Positano, a proper midday break in Amalfi, and an afternoon that either climbs to Ravello or settles into a slower second look at one of the earlier towns. Building real flexibility into each phase, rather than a tight hour-by-hour schedule, is what keeps a full day on this coast feeling like a genuinely good day out rather than a race against unpredictable traffic on a narrow road.
