A surprising number of travelers book a flight listed as "Milan Bergamo," see the word Milan, and assume they'll be landing somewhere near the city. Then they get to arrivals, open a map, and realize Bergamo is actually its own city, sitting a genuine distance away. Planning a **Bergamo Airport to Milan** transfer starts with resetting that expectation, because this is the longest of Milan's three airport journeys, not the shortest.

This guide covers what makes Bergamo different from Milan's other airports, why so many travelers end up there, and how to handle the transfer into the city without being caught off guard.

## Bergamo Isn't Actually in Milan

Bergamo Airport, also known as Orio al Serio, sits roughly 45 km from central Milan. The drive between the two typically takes 50 to 65 minutes, depending on traffic — making Bergamo the farthest of Milan's three airports from the city center, even slightly longer in typical travel time than Malpensa.

The confusion is understandable. Airlines and booking sites often list Bergamo flights under "Milan" as the destination city, since Bergamo functions as one of the metropolitan area's airports in a broader sense. But Bergamo is a separate city with its own historic center, roughly an hour's drive from Milan itself, and travelers who don't check this in advance sometimes discover the distance only once they're standing at arrivals trying to arrange transportation.

## Why So Many Travelers Land at Bergamo

Bergamo has become closely associated with low-cost and charter airlines, which use it as a base for a large volume of European routes. For budget-conscious travelers, a flight into Bergamo can often be noticeably cheaper than an equivalent route into Malpensa or Linate, which is exactly why so many people end up booking it without necessarily planning around the distance.

That trade-off — a lower fare against a longer, less central airport — is worth factoring in before you book, not after. If your total trip budget includes an hour-long transfer each way, it's worth weighing that against the savings on the flight itself.

## Getting From Bergamo Into Milan

Once you've landed, a few transport options are available for the journey into the city.

**Bus services** run between Bergamo Airport and Milan, typically arriving at one of the city's main stations. This tends to be the most budget-friendly option, though it means managing your own luggage through a station and potentially transferring to another form of transport to reach your final destination.

**Taxis** are available at the airport, offering a direct route without needing to plan a bus schedule, though fares for a journey this length can add up, and availability can vary depending on flight arrival patterns.

**Rideshare** apps operate in the area as well, with similar caveats around vehicle availability and comfort for a longer drive.

**Private chauffeur transfer** is generally the most comfortable option for a journey of this distance, particularly if you're traveling with family, significant luggage, or simply don't want to manage a station transfer after a long or delayed flight. A driver arranged in advance meets you at the terminal, tracks your flight for delays, and takes you directly to your destination in Milan without stops. Given that the Bergamo-to-Milan drive is close to an hour under normal conditions, having that hour be predictable and comfortable matters more than it might for a shorter airport transfer.

## Planning Around the Distance

A few things are worth checking before you travel through Bergamo:

- Confirm your onward destination in Milan and how it factors into the roughly 50 to 65 minute drive, since traffic can extend this further at busy times.
- If you're catching an onward connection — a train, another flight, or a scheduled tour — build in a generous buffer given the length of the transfer.
- If your hotel is in a specific district, such as near [Porta Nuova](/destinations/milan) or Milano Centrale, mention this when arranging transport so your driver can plan the most direct route for that time of day.

## Bergamo the City Versus Bergamo the Airport

It's worth remembering that Bergamo is a real, historic city in its own right, not simply a footnote attached to Milan's airport network. Some travelers, especially those with a flexible itinerary, choose to spend a day exploring Bergamo's old town before continuing on to Milan, rather than treating the airport purely as a transit point.

That's a reasonable option if your schedule allows for it, but it's a separate decision from the airport transfer itself. If your plan is simply to reach Milan as efficiently as possible after landing, the distinction matters mainly so you don't confuse "I'm near Bergamo" with "I'm near Milan" when telling a taxi driver or rideshare app where you're headed.

## Arriving Late at Night or Early in the Morning

Bergamo Airport sees a meaningful share of its traffic at less conventional hours, partly a byproduct of how low-cost carriers schedule their routes. If your flight lands late at night, it's worth checking in advance whether your preferred transport option is actually running at that hour. Bus schedules in particular can thin out significantly outside daytime hours, and taxi availability may be less predictable too.

This is one of the more practical reasons travelers choose to arrange a private transfer for a Bergamo arrival specifically: a pre-booked driver isn't affected by reduced overnight schedules the way public transport can be, and doesn't depend on how many other taxis happen to be waiting at that hour.

## Who Should Plan Extra Carefully

Bergamo's distance matters more for some travelers than others. Families with young children, travelers arriving very late at night, and anyone with a tight same-day connection should pay particular attention to how they'll cover the nearly 45 km into Milan, since the options at 11 p.m. look different from the options at 11 a.m.

If your flight was booked primarily for its low fare, it's worth treating the ground transfer as a separate line item in your planning rather than an afterthought. An hour in a comfortable, pre-arranged vehicle is a very different experience from an hour spent working out unfamiliar bus routes with luggage in tow.

## Comparing Bergamo to Milan's Other Airports, Briefly

Bergamo isn't the only airport where the "Milan" label can be misleading about actual distance — Malpensa also sits well outside the city center, though it generally handles a different mix of long-haul and international traffic rather than low-cost routes. Linate, by contrast, is genuinely close to downtown Milan.
