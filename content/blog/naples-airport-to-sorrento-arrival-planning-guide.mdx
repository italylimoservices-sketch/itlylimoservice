Landing at Naples Airport is usually just the start of a longer day, not the end of one. If Sorrento is where you're actually headed, the drive itself is well understood — a verified 60 to 75 minutes under normal conditions. What's less talked about is everything that happens between touching down and actually being in that car: clearing the airport, finding your driver, and settling in for the ride south. That's the part worth planning for.

## Why Arrival Day Deserves Its Own Plan

It's easy to treat the airport transfer as a footnote to the rest of a trip — something to sort out after the more exciting parts like hotel bookings and itineraries are settled. In practice, arrival day is often the most logistically demanding day of the whole trip. You're managing jet lag, an unfamiliar airport, a language you may not speak, and a full set of luggage, often with a plane's worth of other travelers doing the exact same thing at the exact same time. Treating this day as its own small project, rather than an afterthought, tends to make the rest of the trip start off on a better footing.

## Naples Airport Is a Single Terminal, Which Simplifies Things

One piece of good news for anyone arriving stressed after a long flight: Naples Airport operates as a single terminal. There's no need to figure out which building you've landed in or whether your driver is waiting on the wrong side of the airport. Once you're through arrivals, everyone — regardless of airline or flight origin — funnels out through the same general area, which makes meeting a pre-arranged driver considerably simpler than at airports with multiple, spread-out terminals.

That said, "single terminal" doesn't mean there's only one door. Arrivals halls typically have more than one exit, and after a long flight it's easy to walk out through the first set of doors you see. It's worth knowing that a private driver will typically wait in the main arrivals area with a sign showing your name, so keep an eye out as you clear the doors rather than assuming you need to search for a specific pickup point.

## Building in a Realistic Buffer After Landing

The 60 to 75 minute drive to Sorrento is a reliable figure, but it only accounts for time on the road — not everything that happens before you're actually in the car. After an international or connecting flight, you'll typically need to get through passport control (if arriving from outside the Schengen area), collect checked luggage, and walk from the gate to arrivals. Depending on how busy the airport is and how quickly bags come out, that process can take anywhere from 20 minutes on a quiet day to well over an hour during peak summer arrivals.

If you're booking a hotel check-in time, a dinner reservation, or a same-day activity in Sorrento, it's worth adding a real buffer on top of the drive time rather than counting the 60 to 75 minutes as your total travel window from the moment your flight lands. A rough rule of thumb: assume closer to two to two and a half hours total from touchdown to arriving at your Sorrento hotel, especially on a first flight of the day when the airport tends to be busiest.

## What to Share Before You Land

A private transfer works best when the driver has your actual flight details rather than just a pickup time. Sharing your flight number lets a driver track real-time arrival status, which matters more than people expect — flights land early or get delayed often enough that a fixed pickup time without flight tracking can mean either an unnecessarily long wait for you or, worse, no one there when you actually walk out.

It's also worth mentioning if you're arriving with connecting flights, since a delayed first leg can shift your actual Naples arrival time even if your ticket shows a fixed schedule. The more specific the information given at booking — flight number, terminal (even though there's only one), and an estimate of how many bags you're checking — the smoother the handoff at arrivals tends to go.

## Managing Luggage From the Jet Bridge to the Car

One advantage of a private pickup that's easy to underestimate is how much friction it removes from a fairly mundane but tiring part of travel: getting bags from baggage claim into a vehicle. After a long flight, navigating trolleys, uneven pavement outside the terminal, and a walk to a pickup zone can be more tiring than the flight itself, particularly with kids, elderly travelers, or more suitcases than hands.

With a private transfer, your driver meets you inside or just outside arrivals and handles loading the car directly, so there's no need to wheel bags across a parking structure or figure out where a shuttle bus actually stops. If you're traveling with an unusually large amount of luggage — sports equipment, multiple oversized suitcases, or gear for an extended stay — it's worth mentioning that at booking so the right vehicle size is arranged in advance rather than discovered as a problem at the curb.

## Once You're on the Road

After leaving the airport area, the route to Sorrento runs along the base of the Sorrentine Peninsula, and the character of the drive changes noticeably in the final stretch. The road narrows and curves as it nears the coast, passing through smaller towns before reaching Sorrento itself. This isn't a fast motorway run the whole way, which is part of why the 60 to 75 minute figure is given as a range rather than a fixed number — traffic, time of day, and the time of year all play a role.

For a jet-lagged traveler, this last section of the drive tends to be a welcome change of pace rather than a frustration — a chance to actually see the coastline for the first time, rather than sitting through more motorway miles after an already long flight.

## Choosing the Right Vehicle for Your Arrival

Matching the vehicle to your group and luggage matters more on an arrival transfer than almost any other leg of a trip, simply because you're dealing with maximum luggage volume right at the start. An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) comfortably handles 3 passengers and 2 suitcases, which suits most couples or solo travelers. For families or anyone with extra bags, a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) seats up to 5 passengers with 4 suitcases. Larger groups can use an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), each fitting up to 7 passengers and 6 suitcases.

## Arriving in Sorrento Itself

Sorrento's town center includes areas with restricted vehicle access, particularly near the main piazza and the narrower streets closer to the clifftop. A driver who regularly works this route will generally know where they can actually stop near your hotel versus where a short walk with bags is unavoidable. Sharing your hotel name or address in advance helps your driver plan the most practical drop-off point rather than working it out on arrival.

## If Your Flight Is Delayed or Arrives Early

International arrivals rarely land exactly on schedule, and it's worth thinking through both directions of that uncertainty. A delay is the more obvious concern — a driver tracking your flight will simply adjust the pickup time, so there's no need to notify anyone yourself if your arrival slips by an hour or two. Less obviously, flights sometimes arrive early, particularly on long-haul routes with favorable tailwinds, and it's worth knowing that a driver working from your flight number rather than a fixed clock time will generally be there before you clear customs, not after.

What you shouldn't plan around is a same-day connection that leaves no margin at all. Even with flight tracking, the process of clearing an unfamiliar airport takes as long as it takes, and no amount of planning on the transfer side can speed up a slow passport control queue or a delayed baggage carousel. Build the buffer into your onward plans, not into the assumption that the transfer itself will somehow compress the time you need at the airport.

## Seasonal Traffic

The 60 to 75 minute drive time holds up reasonably well year-round, but summer months bring more traffic to the roads toward Sorrento, from both other travelers and local seasonal traffic on the peninsula. It's part of why the estimate is given as a range, and why a little extra buffer tends to pay off if you have plans immediately after arriving.

## Planning Ahead Makes the Difference

None of this requires much effort on your part beyond booking in advance and sharing a few details — flight number, passenger count, luggage, and destination. But doing that ahead of time is what turns an otherwise tiring arrival day into a fairly smooth one: someone is tracking your flight, waiting with your name at arrivals, and ready to load your bags the moment you walk out, rather than you having to sort out a plan after already being on your feet for the better part of a day.
