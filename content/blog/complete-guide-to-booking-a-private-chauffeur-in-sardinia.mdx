Booking a private chauffeur in Sardinia is less complicated than it sounds, but it helps to know what information actually gets asked for and why. A vague request — "a car for a week in Sardinia" — takes longer to turn into a usable quote than one that answers the handful of questions that matter most: where, when, how many people, and what kind of trip it is.

## What a Booking Request Actually Needs

A standard quote request is built around a short list of practical details, and having them ready before you start makes the whole process faster.

**Pickup location and destination.** This can be an airport — Cagliari, Olbia, or Alghero, Sardinia's three main gateways — a hotel, a specific address, or a town name if the exact pickup point isn't settled yet. For a tour rather than a point-to-point transfer, naming the town or towns you want to visit works in place of a fixed destination.

**Date and time.** Sardinia's roads and airports get busier at certain times of year, so giving an accurate date — and a realistic time, factoring in flight schedules if the trip starts or ends at an airport — helps avoid timing problems later.

**Number of passengers.** This determines which vehicle actually fits the group, so it's worth counting everyone traveling, not just the adults.

**Vehicle type.** If you already know you want a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van), a [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan), or something else, you can specify it directly. If you're not sure, it's fine to leave this open and let the passenger and luggage count guide the suggestion.

**Trip type.** Whether the booking is one-way or round-trip changes the logistics meaningfully, particularly for a multi-day visit, so this needs to be clear from the start. A round-trip request also asks for a return date and time, which is worth deciding even loosely so the schedule can be planned around it.

**Special requirements.** This is the field that tends to get skipped and shouldn't be. Car seats for children, extra luggage or beach gear, mobility needs, a preference for a specific stop along a route, or anything else particular to your trip belongs here — not mentioned to the driver on the day, when there's far less room to actually plan for it.

**Contact details.** A name and a way to reach you — phone or email — so the quote and any follow-up questions can actually get to you.

## From Quote to Confirmation

Once a request is submitted, the process generally follows a simple sequence:

1. **The request is reviewed** against the details provided — pickup, destination, timing, passenger count, and any special requirements.
2. **A vehicle and quote are proposed** based on the group size, luggage, and the nature of the trip — a straightforward airport transfer is a simpler proposal than a multi-day tour spanning several parts of the island.
3. **Any adjustments get worked out.** If the suggested vehicle doesn't quite fit, or the itinerary needs refining — adding a stop, adjusting timing, splitting a large group across two vehicles — this is the stage where that gets sorted before anything is locked in.
4. **The booking is confirmed**, at which point the specifics — driver, vehicle, pickup time and location — are set for the trip.

The more detail included in the original request, the fewer rounds of back-and-forth this usually takes. A request that already specifies passenger count, luggage, and any special needs tends to get a usable quote back on the first pass, rather than needing a follow-up to fill in gaps.

## Booking for a Tour Versus a Transfer

The process looks slightly different depending on whether you're booking a simple transfer — airport to hotel, or one town to another — or a tour that involves stops, sightseeing time, and a flexible schedule. For a tour, it helps to describe roughly what you want out of the day: a specific town, a stretch of coastline, a pace that leaves room for lingering rather than a tight schedule. ## Timing Advice for Peak Summer Season

Sardinia's busiest travel months are generally the summer, when both demand for vehicles and the island's road traffic tend to increase, particularly around the Costa Smeralda and other coastal resort areas. A few habits make a real difference during this period:

- **Book earlier rather than later.** Peak-season availability tightens as the date approaches, particularly for specific vehicle types or multi-day itineraries.
- **Build extra time into airport transfers.** Summer traffic around Cagliari, Olbia, and Alghero can be heavier than in other seasons, so a tighter connection window carries more risk.
- **Confirm timing again closer to the date.** Flight schedules and plans can shift, and a quick confirmation closer to travel avoids any last-minute surprises.
- **Expect warmer conditions to affect pacing.** Midday heat in summer is a real factor in how comfortable a day of touring feels, which is worth factoring into when you schedule stops during a longer itinerary.

Outside of peak summer, booking tends to be more flexible, with more room to adjust dates or vehicle choice closer to the trip.

## Common Questions That Come Up Mid-Process

A few practical points tend to come up after the initial quote is sent, and knowing them ahead of time can save a round of back-and-forth:

- **Changing the itinerary after booking.** Plans shift, especially on longer trips — a town gets added, a timing changes. Most adjustments are straightforward to make as long as they're raised with reasonable notice rather than the morning of.
- **Multiple pickup points in one day.** If a day involves picking up different people at different times, or stopping at more than one hotel, it's worth describing that sequence clearly rather than listing a single pickup and destination and explaining the rest later.
- **Uncertain final addresses.** If a hotel or villa isn't booked yet at the time of the request, naming the town or area is enough to get a workable quote started — the exact address can follow once it's confirmed.
- **Combining a tour day with a transfer.** A request that's really "pick us up at the airport, then spend the afternoon touring before heading to the hotel" is a normal kind of day to plan for, but it helps to describe it as one continuous day rather than two separate, disconnected legs.

## Why Lead Time Matters Beyond Just Availability

Booking further ahead isn't only about securing a vehicle before it's taken. It also gives more room to get a multi-day or multi-stop itinerary right. A request submitted with a week or two of lead time allows for a genuine back-and-forth about pacing, vehicle choice, and any special requirements, whereas a same-day or next-day request is necessarily handled more reactively — there's simply less time to refine anything before the trip starts. This matters most for the kind of itineraries that come up often in Sardinia: a multi-day plan connecting the coast and the interior, a multi-vehicle group trip, or a tour built around one of the island's three main airport gateways. None of these are complicated to arrange, but they benefit from not being rushed.

## The Short Version

Booking a private chauffeur in Sardinia comes down to providing a handful of specific details upfront — pickup and destination, timing, passenger count, vehicle preference, trip type, and any special requirements — and giving the process a bit more lead time during the busy summer season. The more precisely a request answers those questions the first time, the faster it turns into a confirmed, well-matched booking.
