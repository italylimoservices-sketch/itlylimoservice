Positano is the closest of the well-known Amalfi Coast towns to Sorrento, and for many travelers based in Sorrento it's the first coastal day trip they plan. The two towns sit at opposite ends of the same stretch of coastline — Sorrento on the peninsula, Positano just around the bend where the Amalfi Coast road properly begins — which makes this one of the shorter hops you can plan from a Sorrento base. There's no fixed published travel time for the route, since it depends heavily on traffic, season, and how many buses and cars are sharing the same narrow road that day, but it's generally treated as one of the more manageable coastal transfers precisely because the distance is short even when the driving is slow.

That said, "short distance" and "quick trip" aren't quite the same thing on this coast, and planning the journey properly makes a real difference to how the day feels.

## Why This Route Still Needs Planning

The road connecting Sorrento to Positano is part of the same coastal route that continues on to Amalfi and beyond — narrow, cut into the cliffside, and full of tight curves with sea views on one side and rock face on the other. It's a beautiful drive, but it isn't a fast one. Two coaches meeting at a bend, a delivery truck making a stop in a village center, or simply the volume of cars in high season can add real time to what looks like a modest distance on a map.

This is worth knowing before you plan a day around a fixed arrival time — a lunch reservation, a boat booking, or a return trip that has to line up with a flight. Building in a buffer, rather than assuming the shortest possible driving time, tends to make for a much less stressful day.

## When to Go

Positano gets busy, and the roads leading into it get busy along with it. Late morning through mid-afternoon is typically the most congested window, especially between spring and early autumn, as day-trippers arrive from Sorrento, Naples, and the cruise ports. An early departure — leaving Sorrento not long after breakfast — tends to mean a calmer drive in and a chance to see the town's beach and main street before it fills up.

If your plan is more about the drive itself than an early start in Positano, a later morning departure still works, but it's worth mentioning your preferred timing when you book so the day can be shaped around it rather than fixed to a single default slot.

## What to Pack for the Day

A few practical items make a noticeable difference on this kind of trip:

- **Comfortable walking shoes.** Positano is built on a steep hillside, and getting from the main road down to the beach, and back up again, involves a lot of steps. Flat, sturdy shoes matter more here than they might in a flatter town.
- **A light layer.** The coast can be breezier near the water than it is inland in Sorrento, even on a warm day.
- **Sun protection.** There's not much shade on the walk down to the beach or along the seafront.
- **Cash and cards.** Smaller shops and cafés in Positano don't all handle payment the same way, so it's worth having both on hand.
- **A reasonably sized bag.** If you're only spending the day, you don't need to bring much, and less luggage makes the walking sections of the visit noticeably easier.

## Booking Considerations

A private transfer between Sorrento and Positano is usually booked either as a one-way trip, a round trip with a set return time, or a half-day/full-day arrangement that includes waiting time while you explore. Which one makes sense depends on how firm your plans are. If you know roughly when you want to head back, a round trip with a scheduled pickup is simple and predictable. If your day is more open-ended — you might stay for lunch, might stay longer, might want to walk along the coast a little — a arrangement with flexible timing, confirmed the day before or the morning of, tends to suit better than locking in a fixed return hour.

It's also worth thinking about group size and luggage before booking, even for a day trip. An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) comfortably covers a couple or small group with day bags, while a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) gives more room if you're traveling with more people or just prefer extra space for a day that involves a fair bit of walking gear — hats, cameras, a change of shoes. ## Parking Is Part of the Problem

Positano wasn't built with cars in mind, and it shows. Parking near the town center is limited, often expensive, and during busy periods can mean a long walk from wherever a space is actually available — which somewhat defeats the purpose of a short trip. This is one of the more practical reasons travelers choose a private transfer for this route rather than renting a car for the day: a driver can drop you close to the center and pick you up again at an agreed time and place, without the detour of finding and paying for parking on both ends of the visit.

## Making the Most of a Short Trip

Because the distance from Sorrento is relatively short, Positano works well both as a standalone half-day outing and as one stop on a longer day that continues further down the coast. Some travelers pair it with [Amalfi town](/blog/sorrento-to-amalfi-private-transfer-complete-guide) or extend the day toward [Ravello](/blog/sorrento-to-ravello-private-transfer-what-travelers-should-know) for a broader look at the coast rather than a single-town visit. If you'd rather see several towns without committing to a fixed itinerary in advance, a chartered day with a private driver allows that kind of adjustment as the day unfolds — starting in Positano and deciding from there rather than locking in every stop beforehand.

Whichever way you plan it, the basic shape of the day stays the same: an early-ish start to beat the worst of the traffic, comfortable shoes for the hillside streets, and a realistic sense that the drive itself, however short on the map, is part of the experience rather than something to rush through. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Positano the closest Amalfi Coast town to Sorrento?**
It's generally considered one of the closest and most commonly visited from a Sorrento base, sitting at the western end of the coast road nearest the Sorrentine Peninsula. There's no single official figure for the exact distance or drive time, since it varies with traffic and route conditions, so it's best to treat any estimate as approximate.

**How long should I plan to spend in Positano?**
That depends on your interests, but a half-day is enough to walk the main street, see the beach, and have a coffee or lunch. A full day allows for a more relaxed pace, more time on the beach, or a short boat trip if one is part of your plans.

**Is it better to drive myself or book a private transfer?**
Self-driving is possible, but the combination of narrow roads, limited parking, and unfamiliar hairpin turns leads many visitors to prefer being driven. A private transfer also means you're not the one navigating the road while trying to enjoy the views.

**Can I combine Positano with other Amalfi Coast towns in one day?**
Yes, and many travelers do. Positano works well as either a standalone trip or the first stop on a longer day that continues to Amalfi or further along the coast, especially with a private driver who can adjust the route as the day goes.
