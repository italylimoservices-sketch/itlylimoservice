A group of ten traveling together on the Amalfi Coast faces a decision most solo travelers and couples never have to think about: how do you actually move as one group, on roads that are narrow, winding, and often down to a single lane in places where two buses meet coming from opposite directions? Splitting into multiple taxis sounds like the obvious answer, and it usually creates more problems than it solves.

**Private transportation for families and groups on the Amalfi Coast** is as much about staying together as it is about getting anywhere. This coast's roads reward a coordinated plan over an improvised one, and vehicle sizing is where that plan usually starts.

## Why Splitting Into Taxis Rarely Works Well

The instinct with a larger group is often to just grab two or three taxis and meet at the destination. On the Amalfi Coast specifically, this tends to go worse than it would elsewhere. The coastal road has limited passing room in places, traffic can back up behind a delivery truck or a tour bus for a stretch, and multiple independent taxis moving at their own pace will not reliably arrive together — or sometimes even within a reasonable window of each other. A family or group that planned to have lunch together at a set time can end up with half the party waiting twenty minutes for the other half, on a trip that didn't need to include that kind of uncertainty.

There's also the simpler issue of coordination itself: relaying a pickup time, address, and plan to multiple separate drivers, each potentially with a different understanding of the day's itinerary, adds a layer of managing logistics that a group trip doesn't need. Keeping a group together in fewer vehicles, or in vehicles coordinated as a single booking, removes most of this.

## Sizing the Right Vehicle for Your Group

Getting vehicle capacity right is the first real decision for a family or group trip, and it's worth thinking about in terms of both passengers and luggage, not passengers alone.

- An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) suits a couple or two close travelers with reasonable luggage.
- A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) comfortably carries up to five passengers and four suitcases — a natural fit for a small family or a group of friends traveling light.
- An [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) seats up to seven passengers with room for six suitcases, which tends to be the right call for a multi-generational family, a larger group of friends, or any party where the passenger count alone would otherwise mean splitting up.

For groups larger than a single van comfortably fits, the practical answer is usually two coordinated vehicles booked and briefed together, rather than an odd assortment of independent taxis. The difference is that coordinated vehicles are working from the same itinerary and timing, even if they're not physically driving in convoy the entire way.

## Multi-Generational Groups Have Their Own Needs

A family trip that spans grandparents, parents, and young children carries a different set of practical requirements than a same-age group of friends. Older travelers may need a slower pace on the Amalfi Coast's stepped towns and more frequent stops. Young children may need a car seat, which should always be requested and confirmed directly at booking rather than assumed to be available — specifics vary, and flagging the need early gives an operator an actual chance to plan for it rather than improvising on the day. And a group spanning generations often has a wider range of luggage needs than a same-age group would, from a grandparent's larger suitcase to a parent traveling with a stroller and diaper bag.

None of this is unusual to ask for. Raising it directly when you request a quote — an honest passenger count, an honest luggage count, and any specific requirements like a car seat or a slower pace — is what actually gets a group the vehicle and plan that fits, rather than a generic booking that turns out to be a tight fit once everyone and everything is loaded in.

## Coordinating a Group Itinerary, Not Just a Ride

Private transportation for a group works best when it's planned as a coordinated itinerary rather than a series of separate point-to-point rides booked as they come up. A family spending a week based in Sorrento or on the coast itself, moving between Positano, Amalfi, and Ravello over several days, benefits from having that full plan in mind from the start — even if each leg is still its own booking — since it means vehicle sizing, timing, and any special requirements can be worked out once rather than re-negotiated for every single transfer.

This matters particularly for day trips that involve the whole group moving together, like an excursion to Pompeii or a day exploring Naples. Keeping the group in a single vehicle or a small set of coordinated ones for the whole day, rather than reassembling transportation at each stop, is what actually keeps a group trip feeling like one trip instead of a series of logistics exercises.

## What This Looks Like for Arrivals and Departures

Group arrivals at Naples International Airport carry their own version of this problem. A large family or group landing together, with a full set of luggage between them, benefits significantly from arranging transportation as a group in advance rather than each smaller unit finding its own way to the coast separately. One coordinated pickup, sized correctly for the whole party, means everyone travels the same route together rather than arriving at the hotel at staggered times throughout the afternoon. The drive itself follows the coastal road's curves — there's no fixed, verified duration worth quoting here since it depends on your destination town, traffic, and season, but it's realistically a half-day commitment worth building into arrival-day plans for the whole group.

The same logic holds in reverse on departure day, particularly if flights or trains are staggered across the group. Coordinating pickup times around each traveler's actual departure, rather than assuming everyone leaves together, avoids the scramble of arranging transportation at the last minute for whoever's flight happens to leave earliest.

## A Few Practical Notes for Group Bookings

- Give an honest total passenger and luggage count upfront, rather than rounding down, so the vehicle or vehicles suggested actually fit
- Flag car seats, mobility needs, or a slower pace directly when requesting a quote
- Plan multi-day group itineraries as a whole where possible, even if each leg is booked separately
- For groups too large for one van, ask about coordinating multiple vehicles on the same itinerary rather than booking them independently
- Book earlier rather than later for peak summer months, when demand for larger vehicles on this coast rises
