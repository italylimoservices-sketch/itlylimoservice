First-time visitors to Sardinia tend to underestimate one thing: how big the island actually is. It's Italy's second-largest island, and that size shapes almost every transportation decision you'll make, from which airport to fly into to whether a rental car or a private driver makes more sense for the trip you're planning. This isn't a place where you pick a single base and see everything on day trips — getting around Sardinia well starts with understanding the distances involved and planning around them rather than against them.

## Start With Orientation, Not an Itinerary

Before mapping out which towns to visit, it helps to understand Sardinia's rough shape. The island has three main airport gateways — Cagliari in the south, Olbia in the northeast near the Costa Smeralda, and Alghero in the northwest — and each one realistically serves a different part of the island well. Flying into Cagliari and basing yourself near the Costa Smeralda, for instance, means a long drive before your trip even properly starts. Choosing the airport that matches where you're actually staying is one of the simplest ways to avoid wasted travel time.

Beyond the airports, Sardinia's interior is its own world — mountainous in parts, with a different pace and character than the coastal resort towns. A trip that only sees the coast misses a real side of the island, but it also means more driving if the interior is on your list alongside the beaches.

## When Self-Driving Makes Sense

Renting a car is a reasonable choice for some Sardinia trips, particularly if:

- You're staying in one general area and want the freedom to explore nearby beaches and villages at your own pace without planning each outing in advance
- Your schedule is loose enough that getting slightly lost or taking a scenic detour isn't a problem
- You're comfortable navigating unfamiliar roads, including the narrower mountain routes common in the interior
- You don't need to arrive anywhere — a dinner reservation, a flight, an event — at a precise time

Self-driving gives you control over the small, spontaneous decisions — stopping at a beach that looks good from the road, lingering somewhere longer than planned. For a flexible, exploratory trip without firm timing commitments, it can work well.

## When a Private Chauffeur Is Worth It

A private driver tends to make more sense once any of the following apply:

- **You're covering real distances.** Multi-day touring between Sardinia's regions, or combining the coast with the interior, involves the kind of long drives that are more comfortable handled by someone who already knows the roads.
- **You have a flight or ferry to catch.** Airport and port transfers carry real time pressure, and self-driving adds the risk of parking, unfamiliar signage, or traffic eating into a schedule that doesn't have room for it.
- **You're traveling with children or a larger group.** Long drives are harder on young kids than on adults, and a larger group often needs more than one vehicle's worth of luggage space, which a private driver can help coordinate. Our guides to [family travel in Sardinia](/blog/family-travel-in-sardinia-why-a-private-chauffeur-can-help) and [private transportation for groups](/blog/sardinia-private-transportation-for-families-and-groups) go into this in more depth.
- **You'd rather look at the scenery than the road.** Sardinia's coastal roads are part of the experience, and that's easier to actually enjoy as a passenger than as the person driving a route you've never seen before.
- **You're touring rather than just transferring.** A chauffeur who can wait at a stop, adjust the day's pacing, or take a route suggestion is a different experience than a fixed-schedule shuttle or a rental car you have to park and manage at every stop.

## A Middle Ground: Mixing Both

Some visitors do both over the course of a trip — a private transfer for the airport arrival and any long cross-island legs, with a rental car for a few days spent based in one area and exploring locally. This isn't the right fit for every itinerary, but it's worth considering if your trip genuinely has both a "cover distance" phase and a "stay put and wander" phase.

## First-Time Visitor Mistakes Worth Avoiding

A few patterns tend to trip up visitors planning a Sardinia trip for the first time:

- **Treating the island like a single compact area.** Sardinia is Italy's second-largest island, and a plan that assumes everything is a short drive from everything else tends to run into trouble by day two.
- **Choosing a base before checking the drive times involved.** A hotel that looks central on a map isn't necessarily close to the places on your list — Sardinia's geography doesn't always match intuition.
- **Underestimating how different the coast and the interior feel to drive.** Coastal roads and interior mountain roads are different kinds of driving, and a rental car plan that works for one doesn't automatically work for the other.
- **Not deciding on a transportation approach until after arrival.** Working this out at the airport, under time pressure, tends to produce a worse decision than thinking it through beforehand.

## Getting Oriented on Arrival

Whichever airport you land at, the first hour in Sardinia sets the tone for the rest of the trip. A few things make that first stretch easier:

- Know roughly how far your accommodation is from your arrival airport before you land, even without an exact time estimate — Sardinia's distances mean this matters more than it might in a smaller region
- Decide in advance whether you're renting a car, arranging a private transfer, or using both, rather than figuring it out at the arrivals hall
- If arranging a pickup, confirm the details — flight number, arrival time, pickup point — ahead of time so there's no ambiguity on either end

## Planning Around Sardinia's Size, Not Despite It

The instinct for a first-time visitor is often to try to see too much in too short a trip, treating Sardinia like a compact region where everything is a short hop apart. It isn't, and the trip tends to go better once that's accepted upfront. A realistic itinerary builds in enough time for the drives themselves, treats travel days as part of the experience rather than dead time to minimize, and picks a transportation approach — self-driven, chauffeured, or a mix — that matches the actual shape of the trip rather than a guess made before arriving.

## The Short Version

Getting around Sardinia well starts with respecting its size rather than underestimating it. Match your airport to where you're actually staying, be honest about whether your trip calls for the flexibility of self-driving or the comfort of a private chauffeur over the longer legs, and build your itinerary around the real distances involved rather than a compressed version of them. The island rewards a trip that's planned around how it actually works.
