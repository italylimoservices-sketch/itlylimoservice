## Arriving at Florence Airport

Florence Airport, officially Amerigo Vespucci Airport and often called Peretola after the neighborhood it sits in, is one of Italy's smaller international airports. That's mostly good news for arriving passengers. The terminal is compact, signage toward arrivals and ground transportation is straightforward, and you won't find yourself walking for fifteen minutes just to reach the exit the way you might at a larger European hub.

Baggage claim is close to the gates, and once you're through with your bags, ground transportation — taxi stands, drop-off and pickup areas, and shuttle connections — is within easy reach, generally just outside the terminal doors. It's a manageable airport to navigate even if you've never been to Florence before, which is one advantage of it being smaller than, say, Rome Fiumicino.

If you're arriving on an international flight, expect to pass through passport control before reaching baggage claim, as is standard at any international airport. Lines can move at different speeds depending on how many flights have landed around the same time, so build a little cushion into your plans if you have a tight connection afterward, such as a train departure from the city center.

## How Far Is Florence Airport From the City Center?

This is usually the first practical question travelers have after landing, and the answer is reassuring: Florence Airport sits approximately 5 km from central Florence, which typically works out to around 15 to 20 minutes of driving, depending on traffic. Traffic conditions, time of day, and events in the city can all affect that window, so treat it as a general guide rather than a guarantee, but by the standards of major-city airport transfers, this is a short hop.

That short distance shapes a lot of the decision-making around how to get into town. Unlike airports that sit an hour or more from the city they serve, Florence Airport doesn't force you into a long, high-stakes transfer decision. Still, "short" doesn't mean "effortless," particularly if you're tired, traveling with family, or carrying more luggage than you'd like to manage through a taxi line.

## Getting From Florence Airport Into the City

Once you're out of the terminal, there are a few realistic ways to cover the distance into Florence.

### Taxi

Florence Airport has a taxi rank just outside arrivals, and it's a common choice for travelers who want to walk out, get in a car, and go. Wait times are usually manageable given the airport's size, though they can stretch during busy arrival windows or peak travel season. Taxis are metered, and it helps to have your hotel address ready to show the driver.

### Shuttle Bus

A shuttle bus service connects the airport with central Florence, typically running to or near the main train station area. It's a budget-friendly option and works well for travelers without much luggage who don't mind a shared vehicle and a walk from the drop-off point to their actual hotel. Schedules and stops are worth checking ahead of time, since shuttle service isn't quite as flexible as a direct transfer.

### Private Transfer

Arranging a private transfer in advance means a driver is waiting for you when you land, luggage assistance is handled, and the car takes you directly to your hotel door rather than to a general drop-off point. For travelers arriving after a long flight, with children, or with a group that would otherwise need multiple taxis, this removes a layer of decision-making right when you're least equipped to deal with it. ## Matching the Vehicle to Your Group

If you do go the private transfer route, it's worth thinking about vehicle size along with the timing. A couple traveling with two suitcases fits comfortably in something like an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan), while a family of five with a corresponding amount of luggage will want more room, such as a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) or an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van). Larger groups traveling together — say, an extended family or a small business delegation landing on the same flight — can avoid splitting into multiple cars altogether by booking a van-class vehicle from the start. It's a small detail, but arriving to find a vehicle that doesn't comfortably fit your group and bags is an avoidable frustration after a long flight.

## What to Consider When Choosing

None of these options is universally "correct." A solo traveler with a carry-on and a flexible schedule might be perfectly happy joining the taxi queue or catching the shuttle. A family with four suitcases and a toddler who's had enough of transit will likely value the predictability of a pre-arranged car far more than the marginal cost difference.

Worth thinking through before you land:

- How much luggage are you carrying, and how far is it from a shuttle stop to your actual hotel entrance
- Are you arriving late at night, when taxi availability and shuttle frequency may be reduced
- Is your group large enough that you'd need more than one taxi anyway
- Do you have a tight connection afterward, such as a train out of Florence Santa Maria Novella, where a predictable arrival time matters

## Florence's Popular Arrival and Pickup Points

Beyond the airport itself, Florence has a handful of spots where most visitors end up first: the airport terminal, the area around [Florence Santa Maria Novella](/destinations/florence) train station for those arriving by rail, hotels scattered through the historic center, and Piazzale Michelangelo for those heading straight up to the viewpoint. If your itinerary includes more than one of these — say, landing at the airport and later catching a train onward, or combining a hotel drop-off with a stop at a viewpoint — it's worth thinking about the whole day's movement rather than just the airport leg in isolation.

## A Note on Florence's Historic Center

Florence's historic center, like many Italian city centers, has restricted traffic zones that limit which vehicles can enter certain streets at certain times. This mostly matters if you're renting a car and planning to drive yourself in; it's less of a concern for taxis, shuttles, and private transfers, since local drivers are familiar with the restrictions and routes around them. Still, it's worth knowing this exists if your hotel is deep in the historic core, since it can affect exactly where a vehicle is able to drop you.

## Planning Ahead vs. Deciding on Arrival

You don't have to lock in a transportation decision weeks before your trip. But if you know you're arriving during a busy period, traveling with a group, or landing at an inconvenient hour, deciding in advance saves you from making that call while standing in a crowded arrivals hall with a phone at 3% battery.
