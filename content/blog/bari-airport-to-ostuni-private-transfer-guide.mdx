Ostuni's hilltop old town, with its tightly packed whitewashed houses visible from miles across the surrounding olive groves, is one of the most recognizable sights in Puglia. For travelers arriving at Bari Karol Wojtyła Airport, getting there is less a matter of distance and more a matter of planning arrival day sensibly — figuring out luggage, timing, and how to actually reach a hill town once you're inside it.

## Landing at Bari Airport

Arrival at Bari Airport follows the usual pattern of any mid-sized regional airport: deplaning, passport control if you're coming from outside the Schengen area, and baggage claim. How smoothly that goes depends partly on how many other flights are clearing arrivals at the same time, so it's sensible to leave some flexibility in your plans for this stretch rather than assuming a fixed number of minutes.

If you've arranged a private transfer, your driver will be tracking your flight number, which means the pickup time adjusts to when you actually land rather than the time printed on your itinerary. Your driver will typically be waiting in the arrivals area holding a sign with your name, ready to help with bags and get you on the road.

## How Far Is Ostuni, Really?

We'd rather not give you a confident-sounding number that isn't backed by verified data, because that's exactly the kind of overly specific claim that tends to circulate online without much basis. What we can say honestly: Ostuni sits inland and uphill from the Adriatic coast, south of Bari, and the drive involves a mix of highway and secondary roads. Actual travel time on any given day depends on traffic conditions around Bari, the time of day you're traveling, and the specific route taken.

If timing matters for your plans — a hotel check-in window, a restaurant booking, or simply wanting a rough sense of your day — ask your driver or the booking team for a realistic estimate closer to your travel date, and treat it as a planning estimate rather than a guarantee. Building in a comfortable buffer rather than cutting things close tends to make arrival day far less stressful.

## Why Ostuni Takes a Bit More Planning Than It Looks

Ostuni is often described as "the White City," and the historic center that gives it that name sits at the top of a hill, with narrow, stepped streets that were never designed for vehicle traffic. That means a private transfer typically brings you to the edge of the old town rather than directly to your hotel door if you're staying inside the historic core.

A driver who knows Ostuni will generally be familiar with where vehicles can reasonably stop close to the historic center and where the remaining walk with luggage begins. Sharing your accommodation's exact location in advance — including whether it's inside the walled old town or in the newer part of Ostuni below — helps your driver choose the most sensible drop-off point rather than working it out on arrival.

## Luggage Considerations

Because the final stretch into Ostuni's old town often involves some walking over cobbled or stepped streets, it's worth thinking realistically about how much luggage you're bringing and how it will handle that last bit of distance. Larger hard-shell suitcases can be more cumbersome on uneven stone streets than soft bags, something worth keeping in mind if you're packing for Ostuni specifically versus staying somewhere with direct vehicle access.

Mentioning your luggage situation when you book means your driver can plan accordingly and, where needed, help you understand what the drop-off and short walk will actually look like.

## Choosing a Vehicle

As with most airport transfers in Puglia, the arrival leg is usually when you're carrying the most luggage of your whole trip, so it's worth matching the vehicle properly:

- An [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan) works well for 3 passengers and 2 suitcases, suited to couples or solo travelers.
- A [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv) comfortably fits up to 5 passengers and 4 suitcases, a practical option for families.
- An [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van) seats up to 7 passengers with 6 suitcases, useful for larger groups traveling together.

## Pairing Ostuni With the Rest of Your Trip

Ostuni is often visited alongside other well-known Puglia stops. Some travelers combine it with [Alberobello](/blog/bari-airport-to-alberobello-complete-transfer-guide) and other Valle d'Itria towns as part of a loop through inland Puglia, while others treat Ostuni as a base for exploring the nearby Adriatic coastline. If your itinerary includes multiple stops near Ostuni, it's worth deciding in advance whether you want everything routed through a single transfer from the airport or split across separate, less rushed trips on different days.

## Getting Around Once You're in Ostuni

Ostuni's old town is compact enough to explore entirely on foot once you've arrived, and most visitors find they don't need a car again until they're ready to head somewhere else. That said, if your accommodation is outside the historic center — in the surrounding countryside among the area's olive groves and masserie, as a fair number of stays in this region are — you'll want transportation sorted for getting into town for dinner or sightseeing and back again. It's worth discussing this with your driver or accommodation when you book, since rural stays around Ostuni can be a short but impractical walk without a car after dark.

Ostuni is also within reach of the Adriatic coast, and some travelers split their time between the hilltop old town and the beaches below, which is another reason to think through your overall transportation plan for the stay rather than just the initial airport transfer.

## Why Ostuni Earns the Extra Planning

Unlike a transfer that ends at a hotel with a driveway and clear vehicle access, Ostuni's charm is tied directly to the fact that its streets weren't built for cars. That's part of what makes the town worth visiting, but it does mean the transfer itself benefits from a slightly different mindset than a standard point-to-point drop-off. Travelers who go in expecting a short final walk, rather than door-to-door service all the way to a hotel room, tend to find the arrival experience matches their expectations far better than those who haven't thought about it in advance.

## When Flights Don't Run on Schedule

A transfer arranged around your actual flight number rather than a fixed clock time naturally absorbs early or delayed landings — your driver adjusts to when you actually arrive rather than when the schedule originally said you would. What it can't absorb is an unrealistic same-day plan stacked on top of a long drive, so if your arrival day already includes other commitments, it's worth keeping Ostuni's hilltop location and the walking portion of the arrival in mind when deciding how much else to schedule.

## Settling Into Ostuni

Reaching Ostuni from Bari Airport isn't complicated, but it benefits from a bit of forethought: knowing roughly what the drive involves, understanding that the final approach to the historic center means some walking, and having the right vehicle booked for your group and luggage. Handle those basics in advance, and the trip from the airport becomes the easy part of a day that ends with a walk through one of Puglia's most striking old towns.
