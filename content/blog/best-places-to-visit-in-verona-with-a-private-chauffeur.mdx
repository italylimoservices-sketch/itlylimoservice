Verona's historic center is smaller and more tightly packed than most first-time visitors expect, and that changes the planning question considerably. The best places to visit in Verona with a private chauffeur aren't scattered across a city that demands constant driving between stops — they sit close together along a bend in the Adige river, within an old town that rewards walking far more than it rewards being shuttled from landmark to landmark by car. Knowing how these sights actually sit on the map, and being honest about where a chauffeur genuinely adds value versus where it's better to simply walk, makes for a far more efficient day than treating every stop as a separate drive.

## The Best Places to Visit in Verona Sit Close Enough to Walk

Verona's centro storico occupies a compact loop formed by a bend in the Adige, and most of the landmarks visitors come to see sit inside or right at the edge of that loop. Unlike cities where major sights are separated by long boulevards or entire neighborhoods, Verona packs the Arena, its two main piazzas, and Juliet's House into an area you can cross on foot in well under half an hour. The honest starting point for any Verona itinerary is that a private chauffeur's real value here isn't ferrying you between the Arena and Piazza delle Erbe — it's handling the arrival, the departure, and whatever comes before or after the walking part of the day.

## Piazza Bra and the Arena di Verona

Piazza Bra sits at the southern edge of the historic center and is where most visitors get their first proper look at the Arena di Verona, the well-preserved Roman amphitheater that now doubles as one of the city's best-known opera and concert venues. The piazza itself is wide and open, lined with cafes and gardens, and it functions as a natural gateway into the rest of the old town from this side. Because Piazza Bra sits closer to the edge of the pedestrian zone than the piazzas further in, it's often the most practical spot for a drop-off, giving you a straightforward walk into the center from here rather than trying to push a vehicle deeper into narrower streets.

## Piazza delle Erbe and Piazza dei Signori

A short walk north from the Arena brings you to Piazza delle Erbe, the old market square built over what was once Verona's Roman forum, still lined with market stalls, cafes, and buildings spanning several centuries. From there, a brief walk through a connecting archway leads into Piazza dei Signori, a quieter and more formal square framed by government buildings and statuary from Verona's medieval and Renaissance past. The two piazzas are close enough that treating them as a single stop rather than two separate destinations makes the most sense — there's no reason to summon a car for a walk that takes a few minutes on foot.

## Casa di Giulietta

Just off Piazza delle Erbe, the Casa di Giulietta draws a steady stream of visitors thanks to its association with the Romeo and Juliet story, and it's worth building into the same walking loop rather than visiting on a separate trip. It's a popular stop, which means the courtyard and surrounding lane can get crowded at busier times of day, something worth factoring into how you sequence the rest of the walk. Because it sits so close to Piazza delle Erbe, there's little logistical reason to involve a vehicle at all for this particular leg of the day.

## Castelvecchio and the Ponte Scaligero

Castelvecchio sits further west along the river, close enough to the rest of the old town to remain part of a single day's plan but far enough that it reads as a distinct stop rather than an extension of the piazzas. The medieval castle and its adjoining bridge, the Ponte Scaligero, offer a different character from the market squares — more fortified and riverside than civic and commercial — and the walk along the Adige to reach it is part of the appeal rather than a detour to rush through. This is often the point in a Verona day where a chauffeur becomes useful again, either to close the loop back toward a hotel or the station, or to continue on to whatever comes next.

## Accounting for the Season

Verona's outdoor squares behave differently depending on the time of year, and it's worth factoring that into how you sequence a day rather than assuming the landmarks feel the same in every season. Piazza Bra and the open stretch around the Arena can be considerably hotter in midsummer than the narrower, shaded streets around Piazza delle Erbe, while a colder or wetter month makes the covered stretches of the old town more appealing than lingering in an open square. None of this changes which landmarks are worth seeing, but it's a reasonable factor in deciding how long to spend at each stop and how much of the day to leave unstructured for a coffee break or a change of pace.

What a chauffeur genuinely adds isn't a series of short hops between these stops but a dependable start and finish to the day, and a smooth transition into whatever comes next on the itinerary.
