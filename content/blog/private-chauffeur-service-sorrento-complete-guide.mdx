Sorrento sits on a clifftop above the Bay of Naples, close enough to the Amalfi Coast to use as a base for it and close enough to Naples Airport to make arrival straightforward, without actually being on the fast train network that connects most of Italy's larger cities. That last point surprises a lot of first-time visitors, and it shapes almost everything about how private transportation works here. This guide covers the practical pieces — getting in, using Sorrento as a base, and the kinds of trips a private chauffeur typically handles — so you know what to expect before you arrive.

## Getting to Sorrento

Most visitors reach Sorrento from [Naples Airport](/airport-transfers/naples), which is the nearest major airport and the most common arrival point for anyone flying into the region. From there, a private transfer takes you directly to your hotel without the transfers and waiting that come with public options. The exact drive time depends on traffic and the time of day, so rather than quote a fixed figure, it's worth simply booking with some flexibility built in, particularly during peak summer months when coastal roads and the approach into Sorrento can slow down.

Some travelers also arrive via [Rome](/destinations/rome), either flying into Rome first or combining a Rome stay with a trip south. A private transfer between the two cities avoids the multiple connections that a train-and-taxi combination would otherwise require, especially useful if you're carrying more luggage than fits easily on a train.

## Why Sorrento Doesn't Work Like Other Italian Cities

Cities like Florence, Bologna or Verona sit on Italy's high-speed rail network, so a traveler can reasonably plan a trip around trains and fill in the gaps with local transport. Sorrento is different — it isn't served by the fast intercity lines that connect most of Italy's larger destinations, which means getting there, and moving on from there, usually means a car, a local train, or a ferry rather than a quick high-speed rail hop. This is one of the more practical reasons travelers arrange a private chauffeur for the Sorrento leg of a trip rather than assuming they'll figure out train connections the way they might elsewhere in Italy.

## Sorrento as a Base for the Amalfi Coast

Sorrento's real strength is its position. It sits right at the edge of the [Amalfi Coast](/destinations/amalfi-coast), close enough that Positano, Amalfi town and Ravello are all realistic day trips without needing to change hotels. A private driver who knows the coast road handles the narrow curves and the stop-and-go traffic that builds up in peak season, and can build in stops at viewpoints or smaller towns along the way rather than sticking to a fixed coach itinerary.

The alternative — a public bus or the seasonal ferry service — works fine for travelers on a loose schedule, but both run on fixed timetables that don't always match how a day actually unfolds. A private chauffeur removes that constraint, which matters most on a coast where lingering an extra half hour in Positano is often the whole point of the trip.

## Capri and the Sorrentine Peninsula

Sorrento is also one of the more convenient jumping-off points for Capri, with hydrofoils and ferries running from the town's port. A private driver can get you to the port on your own schedule, which matters when ferry departures are tied to a specific time and you don't want to be rushing through a hotel checkout to make it. Beyond Capri, the wider Sorrentine Peninsula has its own smaller towns and coastline worth a slower half-day trip for anyone staying more than a couple of nights.

## Business and Leisure Use Cases

Most visitors to Sorrento are on vacation, but the town also hosts business travelers attending meetings or small conferences at coastal hotels, some of which have event and meeting facilities. For that kind of trip, a [corporate chauffeur](/corporate-chauffeur) arrangement built around a fixed schedule tends to matter more than flexibility — a driver who treats a meeting time as non-negotiable and plans the route with a buffer for coastal traffic.

For leisure travelers, the [chauffeur service](/chauffeur-service) side covers everything from a single airport transfer to a multi-day arrangement that combines Sorrento, the Amalfi Coast and Capri connections into one continuous trip without you having to re-book transportation at each stage.

## Choosing a Vehicle

Vehicle choice comes down to group size and how much luggage you're bringing. A couple or solo traveler moving between the airport, a hotel and day trips is usually well suited to an [executive sedan](/fleet/executive-sedan) or [luxury sedan](/fleet/luxury-sedan), both built for up to three passengers and two suitcases. Groups of four or five, or anyone packing extra bags for a longer stay, tend to do better in a [luxury SUV](/fleet/luxury-suv), which handles up to five passengers and four suitcases. Larger families or small groups traveling together usually need an [executive van](/fleet/executive-van) or [luxury van](/fleet/luxury-van), both suited to up to seven passengers with room for six suitcases — a practical option if you're coordinating an Amalfi Coast day trip for a bigger party.

## Planning Ahead

Because Sorrento sits outside the fast rail network and because coastal roads can get busy in peak season, it's worth booking transportation with a little more lead time than you might in a bigger city. Confirm your arrival details, group size and luggage when you book, and raise anything specific — a child seat, an early morning Capri ferry connection — so it can be arranged in advance rather than sorted out on the day.

## Seasonal Considerations

Sorrento's character shifts noticeably between seasons, and that affects how transportation planning should work. In peak summer months, the coast road toward Positano and Amalfi can slow considerably, particularly around midday when day-trippers, tour coaches and local traffic overlap on roads that weren't built for today's volume. A driver who knows the coast can often suggest an earlier or later departure to avoid the worst of it, something a fixed bus timetable can't offer.

Outside peak season, Sorrento is quieter and easier to move around, but it's also worth checking which attractions and ferry routes run reduced schedules — Capri connections in particular can thin out considerably once the summer crowds leave. Either way, a private chauffeur adapts the day to the season rather than assuming a single approach works year-round.

## Multi-Day Itineraries

Many visitors don't treat Sorrento as a single stop but as the anchor for a longer regional stay — a few nights in town combined with day trips to the Amalfi Coast, a Capri crossing, and perhaps a visit to Pompeii or Herculaneum on the way in or out from Naples. Structuring this as one connected itinerary, discussed with the transportation provider upfront, tends to work better than booking each leg separately as the trip unfolds. It gives the driver or company a clearer sense of the whole stay and makes it easier to line up vehicle availability across several days rather than scrambling to arrange each piece a day or two ahead.

A private chauffeur fills that gap, turning Sorrento into a comfortable starting point for the wider region rather than just a town to pass through.
